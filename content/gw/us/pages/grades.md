---
title: "How we rate evidence and safety"
---

Every recipe carries an **evidence rating (★)** and a **safety color (🟢🟡🔴)**. We pass on grandma's wisdom as it was handed down, and we also tell you how much of it has been checked.

## Evidence (★)

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ★★★ | Confirmed in studies in people |
| ★★ | Some studies in people, with limited or mixed results |
| ★ | Traditional wisdom (no studies in people yet, or only lab and animal studies) |

One star does not mean a remedy doesn't work. It means we haven't found studies that tested it in people yet.

## Safety (🟢🟡🔴)

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 | Safe in normal food amounts |
| 🟡 | Take care with large amounts, or if you take certain medicines |
| 🔴 | Shared for interest only. Please don't try it |

## How we check

- **Old books:** we look at what classic texts such as the Donguibogam (Korea), the Bencao Gangmu (China), and old herbals and household books from Japan and the West say about the ingredient.
- **Research today:** we search PubMed and other databases for studies in people, and note the year, country, number of participants and limits.
- Every article lists its sources at the end.

These articles introduce traditional food customs. They are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
