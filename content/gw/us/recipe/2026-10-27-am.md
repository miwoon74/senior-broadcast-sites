Ever feel a little chilly after a meal? In Thai homes, the answer was a pot of lemongrass and ginger simmering on the stove. Four ingredients, ten minutes, and a bright, fresh smell that fills the kitchen. Here's what science can and can't tell us about it.

## Where It Comes From

In Thailand, families have long brewed this herbal tea (ชาตะไคร้ขิง, *cha takrai khing*) after meals or whenever they felt cold.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lemongrass | 40 g (2 stalks, or 4 g dried) |
| Fresh ginger | 10 g (about ⅓ oz) |
| Water | 500 ml (about 2 cups) |
| Honey (optional) | 7 g (1 tsp) |

## How to Make It

1. Bruise the lemongrass with the back of a knife and cut it into 3 cm (about 1-inch) pieces.
2. Add the ginger and water and simmer for 10 minutes.
3. Strain and drink. Stir in honey if you like.

## What's Nice About It

- There are human studies suggesting ginger may ease nausea.
- Studies (in children) suggest honey may help calm a cough.
- Sipping it warm is an easy way to get fluids.

## What Tradition Says
- **Thai herbal tradition**: ginger is used to ease a bloated belly (แก้ท้องอืด), relieve gas (ขับลม) and settle nausea.

## What Research Says Today
There are few studies in people on lemongrass tea itself. **Ginger**, though, has studies in people suggesting it may ease nausea, especially pregnancy nausea (summarized by the NIH's NCCIH).

## Today's Rating

| Item | Rating |
|---|---|
| Evidence | ★★ Some studies, limited results |
| Safety | 🟢 Safe in normal food amounts |

## How to Enjoy It

**One or two cups a day, after meals.**

## Just One Caution

- **If you get heartburn or an upset stomach, use less ginger.**
  For some people, ginger can cause mild side effects such as heartburn, stomach discomfort, or diarrhea.

## General Note

- **Never give honey to babies under 1 year old.**
  Honey can contain spores of the botulism bacteria, and babies under 1 year old can't yet fight them off well.
