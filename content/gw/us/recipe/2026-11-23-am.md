Remember the warm ginger milk Grandma made on icy winter nights? In Guangdong, China, that same ginger and milk turns into a silky pudding you eat with a spoon. Just three ingredients and a few minutes at the stove.

## Where It Comes From

Jiang zhuang nai (姜撞奶) is a dessert loved in Guangdong, in southern China. It has long been eaten on cold days to warm up. The fun part: pour hot milk over ginger juice and it sets, with no gelatin and no eggs.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fresh ginger | 30 g (about 1 oz) |
| Whole milk (regular, not low-fat) | 250 ml (about 1 cup) |
| Sugar | 15 g (about 1.5 tbsp) |

## How to Make It

1. Grate the ginger, squeeze out the juice, and divide it between 2 bowls.
2. Heat the milk and sugar in a saucepan to about 70–80°C (158–176°F), until tiny bubbles form around the edge of the pan.
3. Pour the milk over the ginger juice from up high, all at once.
4. Leave it untouched for 5–10 minutes, and it sets.

## The Good Stuff

- Studies in people suggest ginger may ease nausea.
- With milk in the bowl, you get some protein too.

## What Science Says

An enzyme in ginger that breaks down protein is thought to be what sets the milk proteins. That's why regular (not low-fat) milk and the right temperature matter. The idea that it warms the body hasn't been tested yet — that's not the same as wrong. It's a warm bowl of milk with a little protein, and grandmothers have made it for generations.

## Today's Rating

- Evidence: ★ (A remedy passed down through generations)
- Safety: 🟢 (Safe in normal food amounts)

## How to Enjoy It

On a chilly day, enjoy about 1 bowl.

## Just One Caution

- **If you get heartburn or an upset stomach, use less ginger** — in some people, ginger can cause mild side effects like heartburn, stomach discomfort, or diarrhea.

## General Caution

- **If you have diabetes, go easy on the sugar** — sugary foods and drinks can raise blood sugar quickly. Feel free to use less sugar.
