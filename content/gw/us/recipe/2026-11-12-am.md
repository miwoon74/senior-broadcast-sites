When things felt stuck for days, Grandma served a little bowl of prunes soaked in tea. Pour hot tea over them, wait overnight — that's the whole recipe.

## Where it comes from
In Britain and France, people have long said, "prunes for constipation." The British way was to soak dried prunes in hot black tea and eat them, juice and all, whenever things felt a little backed up.

## Ingredients (serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Prunes (dried plums) | 50 g (about 6, 1¾ oz) |
| Hot black tea or water | 100 ml (scant ½ cup) |

## How to make it
1. Put 50 g of prunes in a bowl.
2. Pour 100 ml of hot black tea over them.
3. Once cool, chill in the fridge overnight. Eat them with the soaking liquid.

## The good parts
- A randomized trial found that eating 6 prunes twice a day increased bowel movements
- The soaking liquid adds fluids
- Prunes contain dietary fiber

## What science says
Grandma was onto something. In a small randomized trial of 40 people with chronic constipation, eating 6 prunes twice a day increased bowel movements, and did better than psyllium. The study had limits, though: it was small, not blinded, and supported by industry.

## Today's rating
| Evidence | Safety |
|---|---|
| ★★ (studies point the same way) | 🟢 (safe in normal food amounts) |

## How to enjoy it
When you feel a bit backed up, start with about 6 prunes a day.

## Just be careful about this
- **If you limit potassium because of kidney disease, eat fewer prunes or skip them**
  Prunes are high in potassium. When the kidneys don't work well, potassium can build up in the blood and affect your heart rhythm.
- **Start with about 6 a day**
  Twelve prunes have about 260 calories, which adds up quickly.
