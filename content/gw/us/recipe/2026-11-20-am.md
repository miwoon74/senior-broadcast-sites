On cold autumn evenings, when a chill crept in, Grandma made a pot of miso soup packed with mushrooms. Torn by hand and simmered in stock, the mushrooms fill the kitchen with an earthy, savory aroma. Four ingredients, one pot.

## Where It Comes From
Kinoko-jiru (きのこ汁, "mushroom soup") is a warm mushroom miso soup from Japan's mountain regions, made during the autumn mushroom season. When the weather turned cool and the mushrooms came up in the hills, families simmered a big pot of it.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mixed mushrooms (shimeji, maitake, shiitake, etc.) | 150 g (about 5 oz) |
| Dashi (Japanese soup stock) | 400 ml (about 1⅔ cups) |
| Miso (fermented soybean paste) | 24 g (about 1½ tablespoons) |
| Green onion | 5 g |

## How to Make It
1. Tear 150 g (about 5 oz) of mushrooms into bite-size pieces by hand.
2. Add them to 400 ml (about 1⅔ cups) of dashi and simmer for at least 5 minutes. (Make sure shiitake are fully cooked.)
3. Stir in 24 g (about 1½ tablespoons) of miso and add 5 g of green onion.

## Why It's Nice
- A warm broth helps you get fluids in
- It contains dietary fiber

## What Science Says
We haven't found research showing that mushroom soup warms the body. Science hasn't tested it yet — that's not the same as wrong. And Grandma had good reasons: on a chilly day, a warm broth gets fluids into you, along with a little fiber from the mushrooms.

## How to Enjoy It
One warm bowl on a chilly autumn day.

## Just Be Careful About This
- **Cook shiitake for at least 5 minutes — skip them if you've ever had shiitake dermatitis.**
  When shiitake are undercooked, a compound called lentinan can cause an itchy rash that looks like whip marks (shiitake dermatitis) in some people. Lentinan breaks down with heat, so thorough cooking reduces it.

## General Note
- If you have high blood pressure, keep it lightly seasoned and go easy on the broth. Eating a lot of salt can raise blood pressure.
