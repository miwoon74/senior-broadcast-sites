After a long day of reading or sewing, when her eyes felt tired, Grandma floated dried chrysanthemum flowers and red goji berries in a cup and poured in hot water. The flowers slowly opened, and a soft floral scent filled the air. Three ingredients, one cup, five minutes.

## Where It Comes From
Goji chrysanthemum tea (枸杞菊花茶, gǒuqǐ júhuā chá) is a tea from China, where goji berries and chrysanthemum flowers have long been steeped together and sipped "to brighten the eyes." It has been a go-to cup on days when the eyes feel tired.

## Ingredients (Serves 1)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dried chrysanthemum flowers | 2 g (5–6 flowers) |
| Dried goji berries | 5 g (about ½ tablespoon) |
| Hot water | 250 ml (about 1 cup) |

## How to Make It
1. Put 2 g of dried chrysanthemum and 5 g of dried goji berries in a strainer and give them a quick rinse.
2. Place them in a cup and pour in 250 ml (about 1 cup) of hot water.
3. Steep for 5 minutes, until the flowers open and the scent rises.

## Why It's Nice
- Sipping it warm helps you get fluids in
- It's caffeine-free, so it's easy to enjoy in the evening

## What Science Says
There are only small studies on goji berries and the eyes, and research on chrysanthemum tea is still limited. Science hasn't tested it much yet — that's not the same as wrong. And Grandma had good reasons: a warm cup gets fluids into you, and with no caffeine, it's a gentle evening drink.

| Evidence | Safety |

## How to Enjoy It
On days when your eyes feel tired, enjoy 1 cup a day, warm and slowly.

## Please Note
- **If you take warfarin or another blood thinner, avoid it or talk to your doctor**
  Several cases have been reported in which people taking warfarin had a rise in their blood-clotting number (INR) after drinking goji tea or goji wine. A higher INR can mean bleeding is harder to stop.
- **Skip it if you're allergic to chrysanthemum, ragweed, chamomile or daisies**
  They belong to the same plant family, so an allergic reaction can carry over.
