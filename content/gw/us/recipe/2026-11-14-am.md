When cold weather sets in and you feel chilly down to your bones, grandmas around the world reach for a steaming mug. From American spiced cider to Chinese ginger tea and Korean jujube tea, here is how three traditional cold-weather drinks compare.

## Where they come from
- **United States (Hot Mulled Cider)**: In American orchards during the autumn harvest, freshly pressed apple juice has long been warmed with aromatic spices like cinnamon and cloves to take the chill off brisk evenings.
- **China (Brown Sugar Ginger Tea / 红糖姜茶)**: In Chinese households, brewing sliced ginger with dark brown sugar is a time-tested habit whenever the weather turns cold or someone feels under the weather.
- **Korea (Jujube Tea / 대추차)**: In Korean tradition, fragrant red dates (jujubes) are simmered into a rich, amber tea before bed to warm the body and encourage a restful night.

## Ingredients

### 1. American Hot Mulled Cider (Serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pasteurized 100% apple juice | 400 ml (about 1⅔ cups) |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Whole cloves | 3 |
| Orange slices | 2 |

### 2. Chinese Brown Sugar Ginger Tea (Serves 1)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fresh ginger (thinly sliced) | 10 g (about ⅓ oz) |
| Brown sugar | 15 g (about 1½ Tbsp) |
| Water | 300 ml (about 1¼ cups) |
| Dried jujubes (optional) | 2 |

### 3. Korean Jujube Tea (Serves 4)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dried jujubes | 40 g (about 10 dates) |
| Water | 1000 ml (about 4¼ cups) |
| Honey (optional) | 7 g (1 tsp) |

## How to make them

### American Hot Mulled Cider
1. If using raw, unpasteurized orchard cider, bring it to a rolling boil first.
2. Add the pasteurized cider, cinnamon stick, cloves, and orange slices to a pot.
3. Warm on low heat for 10 minutes.
4. Strain out the whole spices before serving.

### Chinese Brown Sugar Ginger Tea
1. Add the sliced ginger (and jujubes, if using) to the water in a small saucepan.
2. Simmer for 10 minutes.
3. Stir in the brown sugar until fully dissolved, then pour into a mug.

### Korean Jujube Tea
1. Wash the dried jujubes thoroughly and make small cuts in the skin (removing the seeds yields a deeper flavor).
2. Simmer gently in the water over low heat for 30 minutes.
3. Mash the softened dates through a sieve to catch the skins, stir in honey if desired, and serve warm.

## Safe adaptations: Hot cider
- **Traditional way**: Pouring freshly pressed, raw apple cider straight from the farm into the pot and warming it gently.
- **Modern safe change**: Use commercial juice labeled "pasteurized," or bring raw orchard cider to a full boil first.
- **Why make the change**: Raw apple cider can harbor bacteria like E. coli O157 or Salmonella from bruised ground fruit or unwashed pressing equipment. Gentle warming does not kill these germs. Older adults can become very ill from foodborne infections, and refrigeration does not kill the bacteria. Boiling or using pasteurized cider protects your health without losing that comforting spiced flavor.

## The good parts
- **Hydration**: All three offer a warm, comforting way to stay hydrated during dry, cold months.
- **Naturally caffeine-free**: Ginger and jujube teas contain zero caffeine, making them wonderful evening drinks.
- **Nutrient-rich ingredients**: Cider incorporates orange slices and juice containing vitamin C.

## What science says
- **Hot Mulled Cider**: There are no clinical studies showing that hot cider changes body temperature or stops winter ailments. Its primary benefit is warm, comforting hydration.
- **Brown Sugar Ginger Tea**: Clinical trials in humans have confirmed that ginger helps relieve nausea. However, studies have not confirmed that ginger raises or sustains core body temperature. Brown sugar is nutritionally similar to ordinary table sugar.
- **Jujube Tea**: Tales of jujube helping sleep stem mostly from laboratory research on concentrated jujube seed extracts, not from tea brewed with whole fruit. Meanwhile, studies in children suggest a small spoonful of honey may soothe nighttime coughs.

All three score one star for scientific evidence. That does not mean grandmas were wrong—holding a steaming mug provides real comfort and hydration. They simply work as warm foods rather than medical remedies.

## How to enjoy them
- **Hot Mulled Cider**: Sip 1 mug on crisp autumn or winter evenings.
- **Brown Sugar Ginger Tea**: Drink 1 cup when feeling chilled during the day or evening.
- **Jujube Tea**: Enjoy 1 cup as a relaxing bedtime ritual.

## Just be careful about this
- **Always choose pasteurized cider**: Unpasteurized fruit juice can carry dangerous bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli O157. Older adults and those with sensitive digestive systems should only drink juice marked "pasteurized" or boil fresh cider thoroughly.
- **Dial back the ginger if stomach upset occurs**: Ginger can occasionally cause mild heartburn, gas, or stomach discomfort in sensitive individuals.
- **Watch the sweetness if managing blood sugar**: Dried jujubes, brown sugar, and sweet cider all contain natural sugars. If you have diabetes, omit added honey, limit brown sugar, and keep portions modest.
- **Never give honey to infants under 1 year old**: Honey can contain spores of Clostridium botulinum, which can cause infant botulism. Always omit honey for babies.
