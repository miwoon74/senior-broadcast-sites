All week long, grandmothers around the world brewed something warm. Korean taro soup and five-grain tea, Thai lemongrass ginger tea, a pear ginger tea inspired by Korean steamed pear, and classic American hot mulled cider — here are all five recipes, with the stories passed down behind them. Pick one to make tonight.

## 1. Taro Soup (Toran-guk) — Korea

### Where it comes from
In Korea, families simmer a clear beef and taro soup with freshly dug taro around Chuseok, Korea's Thanksgiving. It's a holiday when rich food piles up, and people have long made taro soup at that time of year. Korea's most famous medical classic, the Dongui Bogam — written by a royal doctor about 400 years ago — says taro is mild by nature, keeps the stomach and bowels running smoothly, is harmful raw, but nourishes the body when cooked.

### Ingredients (serves 4)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Taro | 300 g (about 10½ oz) |
| Beef (brisket or thinly sliced) | 150 g (about 5 oz) |
| Water | 1200 ml (about 5 cups) |
| Minced garlic | 10 g (2 tsp) |
| Korean soup soy sauce | 15 ml (1 Tbsp) |
| Salt | 2 g (to taste) |
| Green onion | 10 g |

### How to make it
1. Wearing gloves, peel the taro.
2. Blanch the taro for 5 minutes, then pour off the water.
3. Put the beef in the 1200 ml (about 5 cups) of water and simmer for 15 minutes.
4. Add the taro and garlic, simmer 15 minutes more, season with soy sauce and salt, and add the green onion.

### What's good about it
- A warm broth helps you get fluids in
- You get protein along with it

### What science says
We haven't found research testing whether taro soup helps digestion. We share it as wisdom passed down through generations.

**Today's rating: Evidence ★ · Safety 🟢** · Best enjoyed: in the harvest season, 1 bowl

### Just be careful about
- Always cook taro thoroughly — raw taro contains needle-shaped crystals called calcium oxalate that make the mouth and throat sting and itch.
- Wear gloves when peeling — the same substance can make bare hands itch.

## 2. Lemongrass Ginger Tea (ชาตะไคร้ขิง) — Thailand

### Where it comes from
Thai families have long brewed ginger and lemongrass tea after meals or when they feel chilled. In traditional Thai herbal knowledge, ginger was said to ease a bloated belly.

### Ingredients (serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lemongrass | 40 g (2 stalks, or 4 g dried) |
| Ginger | 10 g |
| Water | 500 ml (about 2 cups) |
| Honey | 7 g (1 tsp, optional) |

### How to make it
1. Bruise the lemongrass with the back of a knife and cut into 3 cm (about 1 inch) pieces.
2. Add the ginger and water and simmer for 10 minutes.
3. Strain and enjoy. Add honey if you like.

### What's good about it
- Human studies suggest ginger may ease nausea
- Sipping it warm helps you stay hydrated

### What science says
There's little human research on lemongrass tea itself. For ginger, studies in people suggest it may ease nausea, especially during pregnancy (summary by the U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, NCCIH).

**Today's rating: Evidence ★★ · Safety 🟢** · Best enjoyed: after meals, 1–2 cups a day

### Just be careful about
- If you get heartburn or an upset stomach, use less ginger — in some people ginger can cause mild side effects like heartburn, belly discomfort, or diarrhea.

## 3. Pear Ginger Tea — A World Twist

### Where it comes from
This is an easy tea version of Korean baesuk (steamed or simmered pear) and Chinese pear soup. In Korea, grandmothers made baesuk for colds, sore throats, and coughs. Ginger was believed to warm the body, honey to soothe the throat, and pear to help digestion. China's 1596 herbal classic, the Bencao Gangmu, describes pear as moistening the lungs and easing phlegm.

### Ingredients (serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pear | 200 g (about 7 oz) |
| Ginger | 10 g (thinly sliced) |
| Water | 500 ml (about 2 cups) |
| Honey | 14 g (2 tsp) |

### How to make it
1. Slice the pear thinly.
2. Simmer with the ginger and water for 15 minutes.
3. Strain, then stir in the honey.

### What's good about it
- Human studies suggest ginger may ease nausea
- Studies (in children) suggest honey may help calm a cough
- Sipping it warm helps you stay hydrated

### What science says
There's no research on pear ginger tea itself. For honey, a 2021 analysis of 14 studies (BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine) found it reduced how often and how hard people coughed with a cold, compared with usual care (evidence versus placebo is limited). Ginger has studies on nausea.

**Today's rating: Evidence ★★ · Safety 🟢** · Best enjoyed: when your throat feels scratchy, 1–2 cups a day

## 4. Hot Mulled Cider (No Alcohol) — USA

### Where it comes from
In America, during the fall harvest, people have long warmed apple juice with spices on chilly evenings.

### Ingredients (serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pasteurized 100% apple juice | 400 ml (about 1⅔ cups) |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Whole cloves | 3 |
| Orange slices | 2 |

### How to make it
1. If you're using raw (unpasteurized) cider, bring it to a boil first.
2. Put all the ingredients in a pot.
3. Warm on low for 10 minutes.
4. Lift out the spices.

### The old way and the safer way
- The old way: freshly pressed, unpasteurized cider went straight into the pot with the spices and was simply warmed.
- What we changed: use juice labeled "pasteurized," or bring raw cider to a boil first.
- Why: raw cider can carry food-poisoning germs like E. coli O157 or Salmonella, and just warming it may not kill them all. Older adults can get very sick if infected.
- Any trade-off? Boiling or pasteurizing can change that fresh-pressed aroma a little, but no health benefit unique to raw cider has been shown. A small difference in flavor isn't worth the risk of food poisoning.

### What's good about it
- Sipping it warm helps you stay hydrated
- It uses ingredients that contain vitamin C

### What science says
We haven't found research on the health effects of mulled cider itself. We share it as a traditional American fall drink.

**Today's rating: Evidence ★ · Safety 🟢** · Best enjoyed: on a chilly fall evening, 1 cup

### Just be careful about
- Always use apple juice labeled "pasteurized." If you use raw cider from a farm or market, boil it first — when apples are pressed, germs like E. coli O157 or Salmonella can get in from fallen or unwashed apples or unsanitized equipment. Pasteurization heats the juice to a high temperature to kill these germs. Older adults, children, and people with weak immune systems can get very sick if infected, and refrigerating or freezing does not kill the germs.

## 5. Five-Grain Tea (Ogok-cha) — Korea

### Where it comes from
A nutty Korean home tea made by simmering roasted grains and drinking it like water. Korean families have long sipped roasted grain tea instead of water at meals or when their stomach felt off. A Korean book of medicine from about 400 years ago, the Donguibogam, describes barley as a grain that warms the body and settles the stomach.

### Ingredients (serves 5)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Roasted five grains (barley, brown rice, black soybeans, corn, job's tears, etc.) | 20 g (about 2 Tbsp) |
| Water | 1000 ml (about 4 cups) |

### How to make it
0. (If roasting your own) Rinse and dry the grains, then toast each grain separately — they brown at different speeds — in a dry pan over low heat until golden and nutty-smelling. Pre-roasted grain-tea mixes are also sold in stores.
1. When the water boils, add the grains.
2. Simmer on low for 10–15 minutes.
3. Strain and drink warm or chilled.

### What's good about it
- Sipping it warm helps you stay hydrated
- No caffeine, so it's easy to enjoy in the evening

### What science says
No one has tested five-grain tea itself. For its main grain, barley, a small 2019 study in Japan (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, 12 people analyzed) found that people who drank roasted barley tea kept warmer feet in a 25.5°C (78°F) air-conditioned room. It's one small study, so we simply say "there's a report."

**Today's rating: Evidence ★★ · Safety 🟢** · Best enjoyed: with meals, instead of water

### Just be careful about
- With store-bought grain mixes, check the allergy label — they may contain allergens like soy or wheat.

Mulled cider on a chilly evening, pear ginger tea when your throat feels scratchy, five-grain tea at the table instead of water — and on the weekend, a pot of taro soup that serves 4, to share with family.

## Common cautions
- Never give honey to babies under 1 year old — honey can contain botulism spores, and babies under 1 can't yet fight off these germs. (Lemongrass ginger tea, pear ginger tea)
- If you have diabetes, go easy on the sweet ingredients — sugary foods and drinks can raise blood sugar quickly. (Honey in pear ginger tea, apple juice)
