Grandmothers around the globe have long relied on warm, nourishing foods to care for their families through chilly weather and slow digestion. This week, we explored five comforting recipes from Korea, Japan, India, and Europe to see what tradition taught and what modern science says.

## 1. Korean Pumpkin Porridge (Hobak-juk)
- **Origin & Tradition**: In Korea, yellow squash and kabocha pumpkin porridge (*hobak-juk*) has traditionally been prepared during postpartum recovery care (*sanhujori*) and throughout winter to help ease puffiness and warm the body.
- **Rating**: Evidence ★ | Safety 🟢
- **Ingredients (Serves 4)**:
  - Pumpkin or kabocha (peeled and seeded): 500 g (about 1.1 lbs)
  - Water: 600 ml (about 2½ cups)
  - Glutinous rice flour: 40 g (about 4 Tbsp)
  - Sugar: 15 g (about 1.5 Tbsp)
  - Salt: 2 g (about ⅓ tsp)
  - Boiled red beans (optional): 50 g (about ⅓ cup)
- **How to make**: Simmer diced pumpkin in water for 15 minutes, blend smooth, stir in rice flour dissolved in a little water, simmer for 5 minutes, and season with salt, sugar, and beans.
- **What science says**: No clinical studies confirm that pumpkin porridge reduces puffiness. However, pumpkin is naturally rich in beta-carotene (which converts to vitamin A in the body) and dietary fiber, and the smooth warm broth is wonderfully gentle on low-appetite days.

## 2. Japanese Roasted Sweet Potato (Yaki-imo)
- **Origin & Tradition**: A beloved winter street food in Japan, roasted sweet potato (*yaki-imo*) has long been recommended by grandmothers for sluggish digestion.
- **Rating**: Evidence ★ | Safety 🟢
- **Ingredients (Serves 2)**: 2 medium sweet potatoes (400 g / about 14 oz).
- **How to make**: Wrap washed sweet potatoes in damp paper towels, then foil. Bake at 160°C (320°F) for 60 to 90 minutes until tender.
- **What science says**: Studies examining sweet potato alone for regularity are limited, but sweet potatoes are a natural source of dietary fiber that supports digestive wellness.

## 3. Indian Coriander Seed Water (Dhaniya Pani)
- **Origin & Tradition**: In Indian households, warm coriander seed infusion (*dhaniya pani*) is traditionally sipped in the morning to encourage smooth digestion and refresh the body.
- **Rating**: Evidence ★ | Safety 🟢
- **Ingredients (Serves 1)**: 5 g coriander seeds (about 2 tsp), 300 ml water (about 1¼ cups).
- **How to make**: Lightly crush the seeds, boil in water for 5 minutes (or soak overnight), and strain.
- **What science says**: There are no human clinical trials confirming digestion or puffiness claims for coriander water, but it serves as an aromatic, caffeine-free way to hydrate early in the day.

## 4. European Stewed Prunes
- **Origin & Tradition**: Across Britain and France, dried plums steeped in hot black tea or warm water have been the go-to home remedy for occasional constipation for generations.
- **Rating**: Evidence ★★ | Safety 🟢
- **Ingredients (Serves 2)**: 50 g dried prunes (about 6 prunes), 100 ml hot black tea or warm water (about 7 Tbsp).
- **How to make**: Place prunes in a heatproof dish, pour hot tea over them, let cool, and refrigerate overnight to plump up.
- **What science says**: A randomized controlled trial in 40 adults with chronic constipation found that consuming six prunes twice daily significantly increased bowel movement frequency, outperforming psyllium fiber.

## 5. Japanese Job's Tears Tea (Hatomugi-cha)
- **Origin & Tradition**: Roasted job's tears grain (*hatomugi*) has been brewed as daily tea in Japan to ease feelings of heavy puffiness. While its seed extract (*yokuinin*) is recognized as an OTC pharmacy medicine ingredient in Japan, the brewed beverage is simply a nourishing food.
- **Rating**: Evidence ★ | Safety 🟢
- **Ingredients (Serves 5)**: 15 g roasted job's tears (about 1½ Tbsp or 1 tea bag), 1000 ml water (about 4¼ cups).
- **How to make**: Bring water to a boil, add grains, simmer on low heat for 10 minutes, and strain.
- **What science says**: Clinical trials have not evaluated whether job's tears tea reduces puffiness. Nevertheless, it is naturally caffeine-free and provides toasty, soothing evening hydration.

## This Week's Standout & Gentle Cautions
- **Strongest Evidence**: European stewed prunes earned a ★★ rating thanks to randomized clinical research supporting their role in regular bowel movements.
- **Kidney Diet Caution**: Both sweet potatoes and prunes are naturally rich in potassium. If you are managing chronic kidney disease or have been advised to monitor dietary potassium, keep portions modest or speak with your doctor.
- **Allergy Check**: Coriander belongs to the celery and carrot plant family; avoid coriander seed water if you have known sensitivities to plants in this botanical group.
- **Pregnancy Caution**: While job's tears tea is a common household grain drink, safety data during pregnancy are limited; check with your healthcare provider before use.
