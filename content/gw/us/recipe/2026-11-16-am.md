When cold winds blew and chills set in, Grandma brewed a big, fragrant pot of ginger jujube tea. With dried jujubes, sliced fresh ginger, water, and a touch of honey, this warming traditional Korean drink is simple to prepare on cold evenings.

## Where it comes from
In Korean households, ginger jujube tea (saenggang-daechu-cha) is a winter staple. Families often keep a large pot gently simmering on the stove throughout the colder months. Traditionally, grandmothers poured a steaming mug whenever family members came in shivering from the cold or complained of a scratchy, dry throat.

## Ingredients (serves 5)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dried jujubes (Korean dates) | 30 g (about 8 jujubes) |
| Fresh ginger | 20 g (thinly sliced) |
| Water | 1000 ml (about 4¼ cups) |
| Honey | 21 g (about 1 Tbsp, to taste) |

## How to make it
1. Rinse the dried jujubes clean and make small slits in each fruit so their flavor infuses into the water.
2. Add the jujubes, thinly sliced ginger, and 1000 ml of water to a pot. Simmer on low heat for 30 minutes (active prep time is only about 5 minutes).
3. Strain the liquid into mugs and stir in honey to taste.

## The good parts
- Human studies suggest ginger may help ease nausea.
- Honey has been reported in clinical studies (in children) to help soothe coughs.
- A warm, comforting drink that provides easy hydration on dry winter days.

## What science says
Folk tradition often says jujubes help with restful sleep, but those findings come primarily from laboratory tests on jujube seed extracts rather than human studies on the jujube fruit itself. Science hasn't tested the whole fruit drink yet—that's not the same as wrong. And Grandma had good reasons: hot spiced tea provides gentle warmth, steady hydration, and comforting ginger when the weather turns frosty.

## How to enjoy it
Enjoy 1 to 2 warm cups a day whenever you feel chilled.

## Just be careful about this
- **If you are managing diabetes, omit the honey and reduce the jujubes**
  Dried jujubes are naturally rich in concentrated fruit sugars and can raise blood sugar levels.
- **Never give honey to babies under 1 year old**
  Honey can contain spores of Clostridium botulinum, which can lead to infant botulism because an infant's digestive system is not yet fully developed.
