On dry autumn days when energy ran low, grandmas in China filled a big pot with lotus root and pork ribs and let it simmer. Five ingredients, about 15 minutes of hands-on work, and the low flame does the rest.

## Where It Comes From
Lotus root and pork rib soup (莲藕排骨汤, *lián'ǒu páigǔ tāng*) is the classic autumn nourishing soup of Hubei province in China. In Hubei, families have long made it in the dry autumn months, when people feel worn out.
Lotus root is the crunchy, lacy-looking root of the lotus plant. You'll find it fresh or vacuum-packed at most Asian grocery stores.

## Ingredients (Serves 4)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pork spare ribs | 400 g (about 14 oz) |
| Lotus root | 300 g (about 10½ oz) |
| Fresh ginger | 10 g (about ⅓ oz) |
| Water | 1500 ml / 1.5 L (about 6⅓ cups) |
| Salt | 4 g (⅔ tsp) |

## How to Make It
1. Blanch the ribs in boiling water for 3 minutes, then rinse.
2. Cut the lotus root into big chunks.
3. Put the ribs, lotus root, ginger and water in a pot and simmer on low for 60 minutes (about 15 minutes of hands-on time).
4. Season with salt at the end.

## Why People Love It
- You get protein along with a warm, comforting broth
- Studies in people suggest ginger may ease nausea
- One pot and five ingredients, simple enough for a weeknight

## How to Enjoy It
One bowl per person on a dry autumn day.

## Just Be Careful About This
- **Skim off the fat that firms up on top once it cools.**
  Rib fat is high in calories and saturated fat.

## General Note
- **If you have high blood pressure, go light on salt and have less of the broth.**
  Too much salt can raise blood pressure.
