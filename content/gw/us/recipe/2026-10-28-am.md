When a scratchy throat set in, Korean grandmothers simmered a pear. We've turned that old idea into a simple tea with just pear, ginger, and honey. So is "pears for a cough" really true? Here's what science can tell us.

## Where It Comes From

In Korea, people have long eaten *baesuk* — pear that's steamed or simmered — for colds, sore throats, and coughs. Ginger was believed to warm the body, honey to soothe the throat, and pear to help digestion. China has its own pear soups, too. This tea is a simple, modern take on those Korean and Chinese pear dishes.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pear | 200 g (about 7 oz) |
| Fresh ginger, thinly sliced | 10 g (about ⅓ oz) |
| Water | 500 ml (about 2 cups) |
| Honey | 14 g (2 tsp) |

## How to Make It

1. Slice the pear thin.
2. Simmer it with the ginger and water for 15 minutes.
3. Strain and stir in the honey.

## What's Nice About It

- There are human studies suggesting ginger may ease nausea.
- Studies (in children) suggest honey may help calm a cough.
- Sipping it warm is an easy way to get fluids.

## What the Old Books Say
- **Bencao Gangmu (China, 1596)**: pear "moistens the lungs, cools the heart, clears phlegm and calms heat."
- In Korea, steamed or simmered pear, **baesuk**, was a go-to for colds, sore throats and coughs.

## What Research Says Today
No one has studied this tea itself. But **honey** was found to reduce how often and how badly people coughed, compared with usual care, in a 2021 review of 14 studies (BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine). Evidence against placebo is still limited. **Ginger** has studies on easing nausea.

## How to Enjoy It

**One or two cups a day, when your throat feels scratchy.**

## Just Two Cautions

- **Never give honey to babies under 1 year old.**
  Honey can contain spores of the botulism bacteria, and babies under 1 year old can't yet fight them off well.
- **If you have diabetes, go easy on sweet ingredients.**
  Foods and drinks high in sugar can raise blood sugar quickly.
