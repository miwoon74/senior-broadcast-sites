On days when the body felt puffy and tired, grandmas in Korea cooked a big pot of golden pumpkin porridge. Boil, blend, thicken with sweet rice flour, and in about 30 minutes you have 4 bowls.

## Where It Comes From
Hobak-juk (호박죽) is a Korean porridge made from mature pumpkin. In Korea it was said to ease puffiness, and it was eaten in winter and during sanhujori, postpartum recovery care for new mothers. Grandmas made it whenever the body felt puffy or low on energy.
It's a smooth, lightly sweet porridge, a bit like a thick pumpkin soup, not a pumpkin pie filling.

## Ingredients (Serves 4)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mature pumpkin or kabocha squash (peeled, seeds removed) | 500 g (about 1.1 lb) |
| Water | 600 ml (about 2½ cups) |
| Sweet (glutinous) rice flour | 40 g (about 4 Tbsp) |
| Sugar | 15 g (about 1½ Tbsp) |
| Salt | 2 g (⅓ tsp) |
| Cooked red beans (optional) | 50 g (about 1¾ oz) |

## How to Make It
1. Cut the pumpkin and boil it with the 600 ml of water for 15 minutes.
2. Blend until smooth.
3. Mix the 40 g of sweet rice flour with a little water, stir it in, and cook for 5 minutes, stirring so it doesn't stick.
4. Season with the 15 g of sugar and 2 g of salt, and add the 50 g of red beans.

## Why People Love It
- A warm bowl that also gives you fluids
- Soft and easy to eat when you have no appetite or feel unwell
- It contains fiber

## What Science Says
No study has confirmed that pumpkin porridge eases swelling. Science hasn't tested it yet — that's not the same as wrong. Pumpkin is rich in beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A. And Grandma had good reasons: it's soft and warm, so even with no appetite you can finish a bowl and get fluids, too.

## How to Enjoy It
One warm bowl on a puffy day.

## Just One Thing to Watch
- **If swelling keeps coming back, don't rely on food — see a doctor.**
  Swelling can be a sign of heart or kidney disease.

## General Note
- **If you have diabetes, go easy on the sweet ingredients.**
  Foods and drinks high in sugar can raise blood sugar quickly. Even with less sugar, the pumpkin stays naturally sweet.
