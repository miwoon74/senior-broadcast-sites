After a long day on your feet, when your ankles feel puffy, what did grandmothers around the world make? Today we compare three traditions from Japan, India and Korea.

## Japan: Job's Tears Tea (Hatomugi-cha)
Job's tears is a grain that people in Japan have roasted and brewed as tea for a long time. It was sipped instead of water when the body felt puffy.
Ground job's tears seed, called yokuinin, is also a medicine ingredient sold in Japanese pharmacies for warts and rough skin. But **the tea is a food, and it is not the medicine.**

**Ingredients (Serves 5)**
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Roasted job's tears (hatomugi) | 15 g (about 1½ Tbsp), or 1 tea bag |
| Water | 1000 ml (about 4¼ cups) |

**How to make it**
1. When the water boils, add the job's tears.
2. Simmer on low for 10 minutes.
3. Strain and drink warm, or let it cool.

- Why people love it: a warm way to get fluids / no caffeine, so it's nice in the evening
- Rating: Evidence ★ / Safety 🟢 — sip it instead of water
- Caution: **If you're pregnant, talk to your doctor or pharmacist first.** There isn't enough information on whether job's tears is safe during pregnancy.
- Caution: **Avoid it if you're allergic to grasses or grains in the grass family.** Job's tears is in the grass family too, so allergic reactions can happen.

## India: Coriander Seed Water (Dhaniya Pani)
Indian families have long drunk coriander seed water in the morning. It was believed to help digestion and ease puffiness.

**Ingredients (Serves 1)**
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Coriander seeds | 5 g (about 2 tsp) |
| Water | 300 ml (about 1¼ cups) |

**How to make it**
1. Lightly crush the coriander seeds.
2. Simmer with the water for 5 minutes (or soak overnight).
3. Strain and drink.

- Why people love it: a warm way to get fluids
- Rating: Evidence ★ / Safety 🟢 — one cup in the morning
- Caution: **Avoid it if you're allergic to cilantro, celery or carrots.** They're in the same plant family, so reactions can show up together.

## Korea: Pumpkin Porridge (Hobak-juk)
In Korea, porridge made from mature pumpkin was said to ease swelling. It was eaten in winter and during sanhujori, postpartum recovery care for new mothers, and whenever the body felt puffy or low on energy.

**Ingredients (Serves 4)**
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mature pumpkin or kabocha squash (peeled, seeds removed) | 500 g |
| Water | 600 ml (about 2½ cups) |
| Sweet (glutinous) rice flour | 40 g (about 4 Tbsp) |
| Sugar | 15 g (about 1½ Tbsp) |
| Salt | 2 g (⅓ tsp) |
| Cooked red beans (optional) | 50 g (about 1¾ oz) |

**How to make it**
1. Cut the pumpkin and boil it with the water for 15 minutes.
2. Blend until smooth.
3. Mix the rice flour with a little water, stir it in, and cook for 5 minutes, stirring.
4. Season with sugar and salt, and add the red beans.

- Why people love it: a warm bowl that also gives you fluids / soft and easy to eat when you have no appetite or feel unwell / contains fiber
- Rating: Evidence ★ / Safety 🟢 — one bowl
- Caution: **If swelling keeps coming back, don't rely on food — see a doctor.** Swelling can be a sign of heart or kidney disease.

## What Science Says
There are some human studies on job's tears extract (mostly about skin), but none has confirmed that the tea eases swelling. We found no human studies on coriander seed water or pumpkin porridge for puffiness either. Pumpkin is rich in beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A. Science hasn't tested these yet — that's not the same as wrong. All three are warm ways to get fluids, and that's a good reason grandmas reached for them.

## General Note
- **If you have diabetes, go easy on the sweet ingredients** (pumpkin porridge). Foods and drinks high in sugar can raise blood sugar quickly.
