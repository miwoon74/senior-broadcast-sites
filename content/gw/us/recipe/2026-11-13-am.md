On puffy, heavy days, Grandma brewed a big kettle of toasty job's tears tea to drink instead of water. 15 g of roasted grains, 1 liter of water, and 10 minutes on low make 5 servings.

## Where it comes from
Job's tears (hatomugi in Japanese) is a grain. In Japan, people have long roasted it and brewed it as tea, drinking it instead of water when the body felt puffy. The seed is also used in traditional herbal medicine: in Japan, a seed preparation called yokuinin is an ingredient in some over-the-counter pharmacy medicines. But **the tea you drink is food — it is not the medicine.**

## Ingredients (serves 5)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Roasted job's tears (hatomugi) | 15 g (about 1½ Tbsp, or 1 tea bag) |
| Water | 1000 ml (about 4¼ cups) |

## How to make it
1. Bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, then add 15 g of roasted job's tears.
2. Simmer on low heat for 10 minutes.
3. Strain. Drink warm, or chill it.

## The good parts
- A warm drink that helps you get fluids in
- No caffeine, so it's easy to enjoy in the evening

## What science says
There are a few studies in people on job's tears extract (for skin and other things), but none that look at whether job's tears tea eases puffiness. Science hasn't tested it yet — that's not the same as wrong. And Grandma had good reasons: it's caffeine-free and toasty, so it's easy to sip all day instead of water. Also keep in mind that the seed used in herbal medicine is different from the food you brew as tea.

## Today's rating
| Evidence | Safety |
|---|---|
| ★ (a remedy passed down through generations) | 🟢 (safe in normal food amounts) |

## How to enjoy it
On puffy days, drink it in place of water.

## Just be careful about this
- **If you're pregnant, talk to your doctor or pharmacist before drinking it**
  There isn't enough information on whether job's tears is safe during pregnancy.
- **Skip it if you're allergic to grass-family grains**
  Job's tears is also in the grass family, so allergic reactions can happen.
