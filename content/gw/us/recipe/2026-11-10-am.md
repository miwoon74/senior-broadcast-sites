On days when things felt a little stuck, Grandma roasted sweet potatoes until they were soft and golden. One ingredient, five minutes of work — the oven does the rest.

## Where it comes from
In Japan, yaki-imo (slow-roasted sweet potato) is a beloved winter street snack. In autumn and winter, when someone felt a bit backed up, people have long said, "sweet potato for constipation."

## Ingredients (serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sweet potatoes | 400 g (2 medium, about 14 oz) |

## How to make it
1. Wash the sweet potatoes. Wrap each in a wet paper towel, then in aluminum foil.
2. Roast at 160°C (320°F) for 60–90 minutes (about 5 minutes of hands-on time).
3. It's done when a chopstick slides in easily.

Low and slow roasting makes the inside moist and extra sweet.

## The good parts
- Sweet potatoes contain dietary fiber
- One ingredient and five minutes of work

## What science says
Sweet potatoes contain fiber, but there isn't much research on sweet potatoes themselves and constipation. Science hasn't tested it well yet — that's not the same as wrong. And Grandma had good reasons: a warm, sweet snack with fiber that's easy to enjoy in the cold months.

## Today's rating
| Evidence | Safety |
|---|---|
| ★ (a remedy passed down through generations) | 🟢 (safe in normal food amounts) |

## How to enjoy it
As an autumn or winter snack, half to one medium sweet potato at a time.

## Just be careful about this
- **If you limit potassium because of kidney disease, eat less or skip it**
  Sweet potatoes are high in potassium. When the kidneys don't work well, potassium can build up in the blood and affect your heart rhythm.
- **If you have diabetes, stick to about half a potato**
  Roasted sweet potato is very sweet and can raise blood sugar.
