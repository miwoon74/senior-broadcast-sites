When the wind turned cold and you felt a shiver, something was always simmering in Grandma's kitchen. Here are the five warm teas, sides, and soups from Korea, Japan, and China that we shared this week, all in one place.

## This Week's Five Recipes

### 1. Korean Ginger Jujube Honey Tea (Saenggang Daechu-cha, serves 5)
- **When it was made:** When you feel chilled, or your throat feels scratchy
- **The story:** Korean families kept a big pot of ginger and jujube tea (jujubes are small, sweet dried red dates) on the stove all winter long.
- **Ingredients:** Dried jujubes 30 g (about 1 oz, about 8), fresh ginger 20 g (about 0.7 oz, thinly sliced), water 1000 ml (about 4¼ cups), honey 21 g (1 tablespoon, to taste when serving)
- **How to make it:** Rinse the jujubes and cut a slit in each. Simmer jujubes, ginger, and water on low heat for 30 minutes (about 5 minutes of hands-on time). Strain and stir in honey.
- **Good points:** There are human studies on ginger easing nausea. A warm cup also helps you get fluids.
- **How much:** 1–2 cups a day

### 2. Japanese Persimmon Namasu (Kaki Namasu, serves 4)
- **When it was made:** In autumn, and the day after drinking too much
- **The story:** Japan has a saying, "When persimmons turn red, doctors turn pale," showing how persimmons became a symbol of autumn health. This side dish tosses persimmon with daikon radish.
- **Ingredients:** Persimmon 200 g (about 7 oz, 1 fruit), daikon radish 200 g (about 7 oz), salt 2 g (⅓ teaspoon), vinegar 30 ml (2 tablespoons), sugar 9 g (1 tablespoon), yuzu peel 1 g (optional)
- **How to make it:** Cut the daikon and persimmon into thin matchsticks. Sprinkle the daikon with salt, let it sit 10 minutes, and squeeze out the water. Mix vinegar and sugar, then toss in the daikon and persimmon.
- **Good points:** It contains dietary fiber and uses ingredients with vitamin C.
- **How much:** A small side dish (don't eat lots of persimmon at once)

### 3. Apple Ginger Lemon Tea (a modern twist, serves 2)
- **When it was made:** On chilly days, or when your throat feels scratchy
- **The story:** An autumn twist that simmers apple, ginger, and lemon together.
- **Ingredients:** Apple 100 g (about 3½ oz, thinly sliced), fresh ginger 10 g (about ⅓ oz, thinly sliced), lemon slices 2, water 500 ml (about 2 cups), honey 14 g (2 teaspoons, optional)
- **How to make it:** Simmer apple and ginger in the water for 10 minutes. Add the lemon and turn off the heat. Strain and stir in honey.
- **Good points:** There are human studies on ginger easing nausea. A warm cup also helps you get fluids.
- **How much:** 1–2 cups a day

### 4. Chinese Goji & Chrysanthemum Tea (Gouqi Juhua Cha, serves 1)
- **When it was made:** When your eyes feel tired
- **The story:** In China, goji berries and chrysanthemum flowers were steeped together, said to "brighten the eyes."
- **Ingredients:** Dried chrysanthemum 2 g (5–6 flowers), dried goji berries 5 g (about ½ tablespoon), hot water 250 ml (about 1 cup)
- **How to make it:** Rinse lightly, place in a cup, pour in hot water, and steep for 5 minutes.
- **Good points:** A warm cup helps you get fluids, and it's caffeine-free, so it's nice in the evening.
- **How much:** 1 cup a day

### 5. Japanese Mushroom Miso Soup (Kinoko-jiru, serves 2)
- **When it was made:** On chilly autumn days
- **The story:** A warm mushroom miso soup eaten in Japan's mountain regions during autumn mushroom season.
- **Ingredients:** Mixed mushrooms (shimeji, maitake, shiitake, etc.) 150 g (about 5 oz), dashi broth 400 ml (about 1⅔ cups), miso 24 g (1½ tablespoons), green onion 5 g
- **How to make it:** Tear the mushrooms into bite-size pieces and simmer in the dashi for at least 5 minutes. Stir in the miso and add the green onion.
- **Good points:** The warm broth helps you get fluids, and it contains dietary fiber.
- **How much:** 1 bowl

## What Science Says

All five recipes this week are rated ★ — traditional wisdom passed down through generations. Among the ingredients, ginger has human studies on easing nausea, and honey has been reported in studies of children as possibly easing coughs. The idea that jujubes help with sleep mostly comes from lab studies of jujube seed extract, and the goji-and-eyes story has only small studies so far. Science simply hasn't checked everything yet — and a grandmother's habit of caring for the family with a warm cup or bowl is good everyday wisdom on its own.

## Please Be Careful With

- **Persimmon Namasu — don't eat lots of persimmon at once (especially after stomach surgery, in older adults, or with diabetes):** Persimmon tannin (shibuol) can harden when it meets stomach acid and form a stone-like lump called a bezoar. Older adults have less stomach acid and slower stomach movement, so the risk is higher, and some bezoars are hard enough to need surgery. Poorly chewed persimmon is a common cause, so chew well.
- **Goji & Chrysanthemum Tea — if you take warfarin or another blood thinner, avoid it or ask your doctor:** Several cases have been reported where people on warfarin drank goji tea or goji wine and their blood-clotting number (INR) went up. A high INR can make bleeding harder to stop.
- **Goji & Chrysanthemum Tea — avoid if you're allergic to chrysanthemum, ragweed, chamomile, or daisies:** They're in the same plant family, so allergic reactions can cross over.
- **Ginger Jujube Honey Tea — with diabetes, skip the honey and use fewer jujubes:** Dried jujubes are also high in sugar and can raise blood sugar.
- **Apple Ginger Lemon Tea — cut back on ginger if you get heartburn or an upset stomach:** Ginger can cause mild side effects like heartburn or diarrhea in some people.
- **Mushroom Miso Soup — cook shiitake for at least 5 minutes, and avoid it if you've had shiitake dermatitis:** Lentinan in undercooked shiitake can cause an itchy, whip-mark-like rash in some people. Lentinan breaks down with heat, so cooking well reduces it.

## General Cautions

- **Never give honey to babies under 1 year old:** Honey can contain botulism spores, and babies under 1 can't yet fight them off. (Ginger Jujube Honey Tea, Apple Ginger Lemon Tea)
- **If you have high blood pressure, keep it mild and have less broth:** Eating a lot of salt can raise blood pressure. (Mushroom Miso Soup)
