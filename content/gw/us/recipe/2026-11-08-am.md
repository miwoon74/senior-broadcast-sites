## Lotus Root & Pork Rib Soup — China
A clear soup simmered on dry fall days, or when energy ran low.
- Serves 4: pork spare ribs 400 g (about 14 oz), lotus root 300 g (about 10½ oz), ginger 10 g (about ⅓ oz), water 1500 ml (about 6⅓ cups), salt 4 g (⅔ tsp)
- Blanch the ribs for 3 minutes and rinse. Cut the lotus root into big chunks. Simmer everything on low for 60 minutes (about 15 minutes hands-on). Season with salt.
- Why people love it: human studies suggest ginger can ease nausea / you get protein, too
- Caution: once it cools, skim off the fat that hardens on top — rib fat is high in calories and saturated fat.

## Ginkgo Nuts (Caution Episode) — Japan
In Japan, ginkgo nuts are a fall food, and there was an old folk belief that they were good for coughs. This episode was about what to watch out for.
- **Don't follow the folk habit of eating lots of them "for a cough." Keep it to a few a day, and keep them away from children.** Ginkgo nuts contain a toxin (MPN) that can cause seizures, and eating about 10 cooked nuts at once can cause acute poisoning. Children are especially vulnerable.
- **Cooking doesn't make them safe, and never eat them raw.** Heat doesn't remove the toxin, and raw nuts are even more toxic.
- **If you vomit, feel dizzy or have seizures within 1 to 12 hours of eating them, get medical help right away.** There's no antidote, so quick care for the symptoms matters.

## Sweet Potato Ginger Soup — Southern China
A sweet dessert soup from Guangdong, eaten on chilly days.
- Serves 2: sweet potato 200 g (about 7 oz), ginger 10 g (about ⅓ oz, thinly sliced), brown sugar 20 g (about 2 Tbsp), water 600 ml (about 2½ cups)
- Cut the sweet potato into bite-size pieces. Simmer with the water and ginger for 20 minutes, add the brown sugar, and simmer 5 minutes more.
- Why people love it: human studies suggest ginger can ease nausea / a warm way to get fluids / contains fiber
- Caution: if you limit potassium because of kidney disease, eat less sweet potato or skip it — sweet potato is high in potassium. When the kidneys don't work well, potassium can build up in the blood and affect heart rhythm.

## Apple Tea — Japan
A simple drink from Japan's apple country, sipped after a heavy meal.
- Serves 1: apple 100 g (about 3½ oz, thinly sliced, skin on), water 300 ml (about 1¼ cups), honey 7 g (1 tsp, optional)
- Slice the apple thin with the skin on, simmer with the water for 10 minutes, strain, and stir in the honey. (You can eat the soft apple, too.)
- Why people love it: a warm way to get fluids / studies in children suggest honey may help ease coughs
- Caution: **never give honey to babies under 1 year old**

## Apple-Pear Soup — China
A fruit soup made in dry fall weather. It was said to keep the throat moist.
- Serves 2: apple 200 g (1, about 7 oz), pear 300 g (1, about 10½ oz), rock sugar or sugar 15 g (about 1 Tbsp), water 800 ml (about 3⅓ cups)
- Cut the apple and pear into big chunks, simmer with the water for 20 minutes, and stir in the rock sugar until it melts.
- Why people love it: a warm way to get fluids / contains fiber

## What Science Says
All five recipes this week rated Evidence ★. We couldn't find studies that looked directly at these dishes themselves. Science hasn't tested them yet — that's not the same as wrong. What is clearly known is that ginkgo nuts contain a toxin, so they need care.

## This Week's Cautions
- **Don't overdo ginkgo nuts, keep them away from children, and get medical help right away for vomiting, dizziness or seizures after eating them.**
- **Never give honey to babies under 1 year old.** Honey can carry botulism spores, and babies under 1 can't fight them off well yet.
- **If you have diabetes, go easy on the sweet ingredients** (sweet potato soup, apple-pear soup). Foods and drinks high in sugar can raise blood sugar quickly.
- **If you have high blood pressure, keep it lightly salted and have less broth** (lotus root rib soup). Too much salt can raise blood pressure.
