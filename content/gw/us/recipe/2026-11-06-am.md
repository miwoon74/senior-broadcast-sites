When the fall air turned dry and throats felt parched, grandmas in China simmered apples and pears in a pot. Four ingredients, one pot, and about 20 minutes.

## Where It Comes From
Apple-pear soup (苹果雪梨汤, píngguǒ xuělí tāng) is a fruit soup that families in China simmer in the fall. It was said to keep a dry throat moist, and it has long been made when the autumn air turns dry and the throat feels parched.
It's a light, sweet, sippable soup: just fruit simmered in water, not a creamy or savory soup.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Apple | 200 g (about 7 oz), 1 apple |
| Pear | 300 g (about 10½ oz), 1 pear |
| Rock sugar (or regular sugar) | 15 g (about 1 Tbsp) |
| Water | 800 ml (about 3⅓ cups) |

## How to Make It
1. Cut the apple and pear into big chunks.
2. Put them in a pot with the 800 ml of water and simmer for 20 minutes.
3. Stir in the 15 g of rock sugar until it melts.

## Why People Love It
- A warm bowl that helps you get fluids
- It contains fiber

## General Note
- **If you have diabetes, go easy on the sweet ingredients.**
  Foods and drinks high in sugar can raise blood sugar quickly. You can cut back on the rock sugar or skip it; the fruit is still sweet.
