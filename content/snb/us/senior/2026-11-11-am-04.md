How much does it actually cost families to help an aging relative remain safely in their own residence? For millions of households navigating elder care, the answer is an overwhelming financial shock: informal caregivers spend an average of **$15,900 out of pocket each year** to cover basic assistance, accessibility retrofits, and health needs.

As older adults express an overwhelming desire to age in place, unpaid family members step into the gap to provide daily hands-on care. Yet without adequate structural support or insurance coverage for standard long-term assistance, this commitment exacts a severe toll on personal financial security. Understanding the breakdown of these rising expenses is the first step toward finding relief and safeguarding retirement savings.

## By the Numbers: The Escalating Cost of Home Care

Data published by AARP New York highlights the immense scale of informal elder assistance and its corresponding financial strain. In New York alone, an estimated 2.2 million family caregivers provide 2.1 billion hours of unpaid support each year. 

To sustain this vital safety net, caregivers spend personal savings directly on needs that traditional health insurance plans do not reimburse. The average annual out-of-pocket expenditure stands at $15,900, forcing households to reallocate funds away from their own emergency cushions and retirement plans.

Across the caregiving population, these expenditures deplete an average of 26 percent of the caregiver's personal income. The burden is even more pronounced for lower-income households. For caregivers with an annual income below $50,000, out-of-pocket caregiving expenditures absorb an astounding 44 percent of total household earnings. Spending nearly half of one's earnings on elder care leaves virtually no buffer for rising living expenses, personal medical care, or routine household bills.

## Where the Money Goes: Retrofits, Supplies, and Daily Aid

The expenses shouldered by family caregivers extend far beyond medical doctor visits. Most spending goes toward physical alterations and specialized equipment required to keep an aging relative secure and functional within a private home.

- **Housing and Home Safety Modifications (50 percent):** Half of all out-of-pocket caregiver spending goes directly to physical property modifications. Aging in place often requires installing exterior wheelchair access ramps, bathroom grab-bar retrofits to prevent slips, and mechanized stairlifts to maintain safe movement between floors.
- **Medical Co-pays and Supplies (30 percent):** Nearly a third of expenditures cover items that insurance policies rarely provide in full. These include prescription copayments, personal hygiene supplies, wound-care items, and disposable protective products.
- **Transportation and Assistive Technology (20 percent):** The remaining share covers accessible transport services, vehicle retrofits, travel to specialized appointments, and assistive technology devices designed to support daily monitoring and independence.

Because these modifications and recurring supplies are largely paid up front by family members, caregivers frequently drain their personal bank balances, borrow from credit lines, or defer routine maintenance on their own residences.

## Long-Term Repercussions on Retirement Security

The financial consequences of prolonged caregiving extend far into the future. Family members who assist aging relatives often find themselves cutting back on paid work hours, turning down promotions, taking unpaid leave, or retiring ahead of schedule to fulfill hands-on care duties. 

Combined with an annual drain of $15,900 in direct cash expenses, the loss of wages and diminished retirement contributions place the caregiver's own future retirement in jeopardy. Over time, depleting personal savings accounts to support an aging parent or spouse creates a recurring cycle of financial vulnerability, leaving the caregiver with insufficient assets when they eventually require care themselves.

Advocacy groups are actively lobbying state legislatures to expand targeted financial relief, including refundable caregiver tax credits, to help families offset these mounting renovation and medical supply costs.

## International Models: State-Subsidized Home Assistance

Other developed nations address home-based care through formalized government subsidies designed to protect family savings from severe depletion:

- **Australia:** Under the My Aged Care system administered by the Australian Department of Health, the government assigns direct subsidy packages directly to eligible older adults. These packages provide up to 59,000 Australian dollars per year to fund professional in-home assistance, personal care aides, and certified home modifications, reducing out-of-pocket expenses for family relatives.
- **France:** The Personalized Autonomy Allowance (Allocation Personnalisée d'Autonomie, or APA), overseen by the French Ministry of Health and Prevention, delivers public funds directly to older citizens living at home. Grant amounts are determined by the individual's specific level of functional dependency, allowing families to hire qualified aides and complete necessary technical retrofits without exhausting household income.

These models demonstrate that structural public support can significantly ease the private financial strain borne by family members.

## Financial Relief Steps for Family Caregivers

While broader legislative reforms remain under debate, family caregivers can take immediate practical steps to uncover local resources, subsidies, and tax reductions:

- ☐ Apply for the federal Credit for Other Dependents on your annual federal income tax return if you contribute significantly to a parent or relative's financial support.
- ☐ Check with state tax departments to determine if your state offers an active Family Caregiver Tax Credit to offset accessibility modifications such as ramps and grab bars.
- ☐ Contact your local Area Agency on Aging by calling the Administration for Community Living Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116.
- ☐ Search the Eldercare Locator online directory at eldercare.acl.gov to identify regional respite care vouchers that fund temporary relief for family caregivers.
- ☐ Request an in-home assessment through municipal aging programs to explore subsidized assistive equipment, grab-bar installation, or home-delivered supplies.
- ☐ Retain detailed receipts and invoices for all wheelchair ramps, stairlifts, personal hygiene supplies, and transportation expenditures to support potential tax deductions.

Connecting with community programs and tax credits as early as possible can help family caregivers mitigate thousands of dollars in annual out-of-pocket expenses and safeguard their own financial future.
