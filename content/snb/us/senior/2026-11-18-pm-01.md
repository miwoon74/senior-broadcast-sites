Will your next routine medical checkup soon require two separate trips to the clinic instead of one? Under finalized federal regulations from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), **roughly 60 percent of dermatologists and orthopedic specialists** may soon require patients to schedule two distinct visits rather than providing exams and minor procedures during the same appointment. 

For decades, Medicare beneficiaries have appreciated the convenience of addressing multiple concerns in a single trip. A senior attending an annual evaluation could have a suspicious skin patch biopsied or receive a cortisone injection for an aching knee on the very same afternoon. However, recent regulatory changes set for implementation in 2027 reduce how much physicians are paid when an office evaluation and a minor in-office surgical procedure are billed together. This impending payment reduction is forcing healthcare practices across the country to rethink their scheduling practices, leaving older adults facing potential treatment delays, increased travel challenges, and doubled out-of-pocket costs.

## Understanding the 2027 Medicare Payment Rule Changes

The core of the issue lies in the annual update to the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. Under the revised framework finalized for 2027, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services will apply reimbursement cuts to claims where physicians use modifier codes to bill for both an evaluation and management service and a minor procedure during the same patient visit.

When doctors provide combined same-day care, clinics face estimated reimbursement reductions of up to 20 percent on these combined billing modifier codes. Federal regulators established these adjustments to reduce what they characterize as overlapping overhead expenses. Medical groups and specialty organizations, however, warn that cutting payments by up to 20 percent makes same-day care financially unsustainable for many outpatient practices. 

Because clinics must cover staff time, facility maintenance, and specialized clinical supplies, physician organizations estimate that roughly 60 percent of specialists in high-volume procedural fields—most notably dermatology and orthopedics—will adapt by separating evaluations from hands-on treatments. Rather than conducting an exam and performing a minor procedure within the same block of time, practices are planning to require patients to return on a separate date to receive their medical interventions.

## How Split Appointments Impact Out-of-Pocket Costs and Daily Schedules

For older adults living on fixed incomes, splitting one comprehensive doctor visit into two separate appointments brings both financial strain and logistical friction. 

Under standard Medicare Part B rules, beneficiaries are generally responsible for a 20 percent coinsurance after meeting their annual deductible, or they pay specific copayments depending on their supplemental coverage. When an evaluation and a minor treatment occur on the same day, patients typically navigate a single office charge. Splitting that care across two separate calendar days creates two separate service dates. 

If an office visit requires an out-of-pocket copayment, scheduling two separate appointments may double a beneficiary's total out-of-pocket charges.

| Clinic Visits Needed | 1 combined appointment | 2 separate appointments |
| Out-of-Pocket Expense | 1 copayment | 2 copayments (higher total) |

| Specialist Practice Response | Procedures handled during exam | ~60% of dermatologists & orthopedists require 2 visits |
| Treatment Timeline | Immediate same-day care | Minor treatment delayed by days or weeks |

Beyond the direct dollar impact, the logistical toll is substantial. Many retirees no longer drive and depend on family caregivers, specialized ride services, or municipal public transit to travel to medical appointments. Requiring two trips instead of one means:
- Coordinating twice as many rides and parking reservations.
- Enduring double the physical exertion, which can be exhausting for patients suffering from mobility limitations or joint pain.
- Waiting days or weeks for minor relief, such as an injection for arthritic knee pain or a diagnostic biopsy for a persistent rash.

## Navigating Scheduling Policies and Administrative Hurdles

Medical practices are beginning to communicate these operational updates well ahead of the 2027 rollout. Front-office schedulers and billing administrators will face the challenge of explaining why a physician who previously provided immediate treatments must now request a follow-up appointment.

Understanding your clinic's specific billing practices is essential to avoiding surprises. Because the fee adjustments apply directly to how physicians bill evaluation and management codes alongside procedural codes, practices have latitude in how they organize their daily templates. Some providers may elect to absorb the fee reductions to maintain patient convenience, while others will mandate strict separation between diagnostic visits and procedural care.

Patients should also stay vigilant by reviewing their Medicare documentation. When you receive services, your Medicare Summary Notice (MSN) outlines the specific services billed, the approved amounts, and the coinsurance responsibility assigned to you. Checking this notice ensures that procedures billed across multiple dates accurately reflect the medical care you received and that your Part B benefits are applied correctly.

## Practical Steps to Prepare for Upcoming Specialist Visits

Although the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule rule is slated for full effect in 2027, patients can protect their time and budgets by adopting proactive scheduling habits right now. Transparent communication with clinical teams ensures you can anticipate visit requirements before arriving at the office.

First, contact your specialist's front desk in advance of scheduled checkups. Ask directly whether the physician handles minor procedures during routine consultations or if their office requires separate bookings. Clarifying this policy before you arrive prevents unexpected return trips and allows you to budget your transportation and out-of-pocket funds accordingly.

Second, if scheduling two visits presents a significant hardship, share your circumstances with your physician. Doctors frequently exercise clinical discretion for patients who travel long distances or face severe mobility barriers. Explaining your physical or financial constraints can help the care team determine whether your evaluation and procedural needs can be accommodated in one trip.

Finally, independent assistance is available if you encounter complex billing issues or wish to understand how Part B coinsurance applies to upcoming procedures. You can verify formal coverage rules directly through Medicare.gov or by speaking with official customer service representatives. Additionally, state-level advocates can review your plan details and help you resolve billing disputes.

## Action Checklist for Upcoming Specialist Appointments

- ☐ Contact your doctor's front desk ahead of upcoming visits to ask if minor in-office procedures can be completed during your exam.
- ☐ Inquire whether treatments such as joint injections, rash evaluations, or skin biopsies require a second, separate booking.
- ☐ Confirm your Part B coinsurance or specialist copay requirements to anticipate out-of-pocket expenses if two visits are mandated.
- ☐ Review your Medicare Summary Notice (MSN) online at Medicare.gov or through your paper statements to verify billing accuracy.
- ☐ Contact 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) if you have direct questions about fee schedule coverage or billing code rules.
- ☐ Reach out to your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) at shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675 for free, objective guidance on managing specialist costs.
