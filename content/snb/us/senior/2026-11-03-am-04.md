How much does it actually cost to care for an aging parent or relative at home? For millions of households, the answer is no longer measured solely in personal hours and emotional dedication, but in thousands of dollars taken directly from personal savings. **According to national survey data, family caregivers spend an average of $11,508 each year out of their own pockets to support older relatives.**

This financial demand has escalated sharply across the country. Over the past five years, routine out-of-pocket obligations have increased substantially, shifting a major economic burden onto middle-aged adults, retirees, and multi-generational households. Understanding where these expenses originate, how they affect household stability, and what relief programs exist can help families protect their personal savings while maintaining essential care for older loved ones.

## By the Numbers: A 59 Percent Surge Since 2021

The financial reality of day-to-day eldercare was detailed in a national study conducted among 2,538 family caregivers. The findings demonstrate that personal financial contributions toward caregiving have grown rapidly over a short period.

In 2021, caregivers reported an average annual expenditure of $7,242. By 2026, that figure climbed to $11,508, reflecting a 59 percent increase in five years.

The scale of unpaid assistance across the United States is substantial. An estimated 63 million Americans currently provide unpaid eldercare services to relatives or friends. Together, these family members provide an economic contribution valued at approximately $1 trillion annually. Yet, because these caregivers absorb substantial out-of-pocket costs without regular institutional compensation, the financial toll remains focused directly on individual bank accounts.

## Where the Money Goes: Housing and Medical Needs

Family caregiving expenses encompass far more than occasional groceries or local transportation. The study breaks down out-of-pocket costs into distinct categories, showing that physical living space adaptations and health-related necessities account for the bulk of personal expenditures.

Housing and home adjustments make up the largest portion of out-of-pocket spending, accounting for 42 percent of total funds, or an average of $4,797 annually. Families routinely pay for physical home modifications such as accessibility ramps, bathroom grab bar installations, general home repairs, or direct support with mortgage and rent payments.

Direct medical necessities represent the second largest expense category, capturing 29 percent of spending, or $3,313 each year. These expenditures cover insurance copays, prescription drugs, and assistive medical equipment not fully absorbed by existing health plans.

The remaining spending is distributed between personal assistance and transit. Recipient personal care and meal provisions account for 18 percent of total spending ($2,115 per year), while caregiver travel and related personal support expenses make up 11 percent ($1,283 per year).

## The Strain on Household Income and Retirement Security

Allocating thousands of dollars each year to eldercare has lasting effects on household stability. On average, family caregiving expenditures absorb 31 percent of a caregiver's annual household income.

This burden falls unequally on lower-income households. For families earning under $40,000 annually, caregiving costs consume 76 percent of their annual income. Devoting such a high percentage of earnings to immediate care needs frequently forces individuals to alter their career paths, scale back working hours, or forgo salary advancements.

The diversion of income also impairs long-term retirement preparedness. Rather than contributing steadily to personal retirement accounts or building emergency reserves, caregivers are frequently required to draw down existing savings to cover recurring equipment costs, home alterations, and medical bills. Over several years, this dynamic can undermine personal independence for the caregiver later in life.

## International Comparison: Long-Term Care Supports in Germany

Other developed nations address the economic demands of home care through structured social insurance programs. In Germany, the statutory long-term care insurance framework (*Pflegeversicherung*) provides formal public benefits directly to family members delivering care at home, according to the German Federal Ministry of Health.

Under this system, families caring for loved ones receive monthly cash benefits (*Pflegegeld*) ranging from €332 to €947, depending on the level of care required. In addition to cash support, the program pays statutory pension contributions on behalf of the family caregiver and funds up to four weeks of annual substitute respite care (*Verhinderungspflege*). This public structure ensures that family members providing essential care maintain their own retirement credits and receive periodic respite relief without relying entirely on personal household income.

## Practical Steps to Reduce Out-of-Pocket Care Expenses

While the United States lacks a universal cash stipend for family care, specific federal tax provisions and regional community resources offer assistance to eligible households.

First, evaluate potential tax relief with a certified financial advisor or tax preparer; taxpayers supporting older family members may qualify for federal tax benefits.redit for Other Dependents, which offers a non-refundable credit of $500. Additionally, caregivers who itemize deductions on IRS Form 1040 Schedule A can deduct qualifying unreimbursed medical expenses that exceed 7.5 percent of their adjusted gross income.

Second, connect directly with regional aging networks. Local Area Agencies on Aging coordinate access to public assistance, including adult day-care subsidies, caregiver respite vouchers, and meal distribution programs. Families can locate community support options through the federal Eldercare Locator service, accessible online at eldercare.acl.gov or by calling 1-800-677-1116.

## Action Checklist for Family Caregivers

- ☐ Calculate your total yearly care expenses across housing modifications, medical copays, equipment, and travel.
- ☐ Keep all receipts for medical expenses and home safety equipment to assess if unreimbursed costs exceed 7.5 percent of adjusted gross income.
- ☐ Check IRS criteria with a tax professional regarding eligibility for the $500 Credit for Other Dependents.
- ☐ Contact your local Area Agency on Aging through eldercare.acl.gov or by phone at 1-800-677-1116 to ask about respite care stipends.
- ☐ Assess whether modifications such as accessibility ramps or grab bars qualify for local home-adaptation assistance grants.
- ☐ Review personal retirement contributions to avoid draining personal long-term savings for immediate short-term care expenses.
