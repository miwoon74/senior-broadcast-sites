Have you recently received an urgent phone call or text message claiming your Medicare coverage is about to end? Older Americans lose **over $1.6 billion** each year to imposter schemes, according to official reports filed with the Federal Trade Commission. With the annual open enrollment deadline arriving on December 7, 2026, con artists are intensifying high-pressure tactics aimed at stealing Medicare credentials, private banking details, and personal health benefits.

Understanding how legitimate federal programs operate—and recognizing the specific lies con artists tell—is the single most reliable way to protect your healthcare and finances.

## The Cost of Imposter Scams in Numbers

Consumer watchdogs, including AARP Michigan and the Kalamazoo County Area Agency on Aging, caution that the open enrollment window is peak season for healthcare fraud. During this period, beneficiaries regularly make decisions regarding prescription plans and supplemental coverage, giving fraudsters an opening to create artificial confusion.

When bad actors obtain an individual's Medicare number, the consequences reach far beyond unwanted phone solicitations. Healthcare identity theft allows criminals to submit fraudulent claims for medical procedures that never occurred, bill government programs for expensive hardware, disrupt accurate prescription histories, and potentially damage personal credit records. Resolving these issues involves filing extensive federal fraud reports, enduring administrative investigations, and completing a formal process to obtain a replacement identifier.

## Common Deceptions Used During Open Enrollment

Imposters routinely pose as representatives of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) or private insurance underwriters. They rely on fear, urgency, and technical-sounding jargon to pressure seniors into handing over confidential data.

| Scam Technique | Fraudulent Pitch / Claim | Legitimate Medicare Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plastic Chip Card Upgrade | Beneficiaries must verify identification to receive a new chipped plastic card. | Medicare never issues cards with embedded microchips or calls to confirm identity. |
| Coverage Cancellation Threat | Prescription drug coverage will expire immediately unless bank account details are verified. | Medicare never cancels benefits or demands banking numbers through sudden unsolicited phone calls. |
| Free Medical Equipment or Genetic Screenings | Offers for complimentary back braces, knee braces, or genetic lab tests in exchange for an ID number. | Legitimate medical equipment and diagnostic testing require direct orders from your personal physician. |
| Official Verification Demands | Callers claim to be federal agents needing Social Security or routing numbers to resolve billing errors. | Federal agencies do not contact beneficiaries by phone to solicit private banking numbers. |

### The "Chipped Card" Myth
One of the most widespread schemes involves callers claiming that government regulations require all Medicare beneficiaries to replace paper cards with plastic cards featuring embedded microchips. Callers state that coverage will be suspended unless the recipient verifies personal data. In reality, the federal government does not issue chipped Medicare cards, and officials will never place cold calls demanding identity verification.

### The Threat of Canceled Benefits
A second frequent tactic involves threats of sudden termination. Scammers claim a prescription drug plan has lapsed or an outstanding billing discrepancy exists. They demand routing numbers or credit card credentials immediately over the phone under the pretense of preserving active benefits. Medicare plans do not operate through abrupt telephone ultimatums.

### Unsolicited Medical Offers
Fraudsters also dangle free medical equipment, such as braces or specialized genetic test kits. When seniors agree to receive these items, the scammers use their Medicare numbers to submit fraudulent billing claims for thousands of dollars in unneeded or nonexistent services.

## Why Medicare Never Calls Unsolicited

Legitimate federal agencies and approved insurance administrators follow strict consumer protection protocols:

1. **No Cold Calling**: Genuine Medicare representatives will not place unexpected phone calls to beneficiaries. Unless you have initiated an inquiry and requested a callback, any incoming caller claiming to represent Medicare is an imposter.
2. **No Demands for Banking Numbers**: Official personnel will not ask for bank routing numbers, credit card numbers, or Social Security numbers over the phone to verify your status.
3. **Written Communication First**: If administrative or billing questions arise regarding your benefits, official correspondence is typically sent through formal postal mail or secure messages from your plan — check the latest at Medicare.gov.mail or accessible directly via secure online portals.

## Global Perspective: How Japan Fights Imposter Calls

Imposter schemes targeting older residents are an international challenge. In Japan, municipal offices and the National Police Agency have addressed phone-based fraud by deploying subsidized anti-fraud automated call recorders and filtering hardware directly to senior households. 

When an incoming call arrives, these residential devices automatically play an explicit warning informing the caller that the conversation is being recorded for security purposes. According to consumer crime reports from Japan's National Police Agency, this automated intervention deters criminals and results in over 80% of criminal phishing calls being immediately abandoned before the resident ever speaks.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your Coverage

Seniors and their families can insulate themselves against enrollment scams by adopting proactive defensive habits:

- **Adopt a Strict Hang-Up Policy**: If an unsolicited caller mentions Medicare, ends benefits, or demands identification numbers, disconnect the call immediately. Do not press buttons to be removed from a call list, as this confirms your phone number is active.
- **Inspect Quarterly Medicare Summary Notices (MSN)**: Review your printed statements or log in to your account at Medicare.gov to examine recent claims. Look closely for diagnostic exams, physician visits, prescription medications, or medical equipment you never received.
- **Verify Directly via Official Channels**: If you have questions about your coverage or changes to your plan before the December 7 deadline, bypass all incoming solicitations. Contact Medicare directly at 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) or consult your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP).
- **Report Suspected Fraud Promptly**: Contact the Senior Medicare Patrol (SMP) at 1-877-808-2468 or file a report with the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov. You can also contact the FTC consumer response center directly at 1-877-FTC-HELP.

## Enrollment Season Security Checklist

- ☐ Hang up immediately on any unexpected caller claiming to work for Medicare.
- ☐ Keep your Medicare card and number stored securely; never carry it unless visiting a doctor.
- ☐ Refuse offers of free medical supplies or screening kits that require your Medicare identifier.
- ☐ Never provide banking, credit card, or routing information over an unsolicited incoming phone call.
- ☐ Check your Medicare Summary Notices for unfamiliar services or medical billing entries.
- ☐ Finalize all legitimate plan adjustments prior to the December 7 open enrollment deadline.
- ☐ Report suspicious calls to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov or call 1-877-FTC-HELP.
