Have you looked out your window this week to find seawater creeping up your driveway or submerging local streets? **Santa Barbara recorded an all-time record water level of 8.21 feet in 2026**, as unprecedented king tides swept coastal neighborhoods across the country. These extreme high-water crests do not just flood sandy shores; they directly threaten the physical safety, home systems, and daily mobility of older residents living near bays, canals, and oceanfronts.

When seawater covers residential roadways, basic errands become serious hazards. Navigating these surges requires understanding why tides are cresting at historical highs and taking specific, proactive measures to safeguard your property and health.

## Historic Water Levels: What the Tidal Gauges Show

A powerful convergence of natural king tides and seasonal storm swells pushed ocean crests to some of their highest marks in over four decades. According to the National Weather Service, gauges along the California coastline documented historic benchmarks across multiple metropolitan regions.

The impact stretched far beyond Southern California. Ocean swells produced waves reaching up to 24 feet, collapsing a section of the public pier in Pacifica, California. In Washington State, tidal surges disrupted regional ferry operations serving an estimated 300,000 travelers, stranding commuters and cutting off key transit links. Forecasters point to the 2026 super El Niño pattern as a compounding factor, elevating baseline ocean heights and amplifying erosion hazards into December.

## Why Coastal Surges Present Unique Obstacles for Seniors

For older homeowners and community members, rising seawater creates challenges that extend far beyond inconvenience.

1. **Compromised Medical and Pharmacy Access:** When street gutters overflow with seawater, neighborhoods become temporarily isolated. Salt water pooling across access routes prevents family caregivers, home health aides, and emergency vehicles from reaching homes. For individuals reliant on daily prescription deliveries or scheduled outpatient care, even a temporary crest can disrupt medical routines.
2. **Severe Automotive Damage:** Driving through standing seawater is vastly different from driving through rainwater. Ocean water is highly corrosive. Submerging an automobile in coastal run-off destroys brake lines, erodes undercarriages, and ruins sensitive electronic modules. A car stranded in high water leaves an older driver without a reliable evacuation route.
3. **Property and Equipment Losses:** Ground-floor storage areas, low-lying carports, and crawl spaces are vulnerable to sudden inundation. Ground-level heating equipment, outdoor electrical panels, and backup medical machinery—such as home oxygen compressors—can be ruined if ocean water breaches the foundation. Standard homeowners insurance generally excludes damage caused by coastal flood surges, resulting in substantial personal repair bills.
4. **Physical Fall and Injury Risks:** Walking across flooded sidewalks or driveways presents severe fall hazards. Moving water as shallow as six inches can knock an adult down, while murky seawater hides displaced drain covers, shifting debris, and slippery algae.

## Actions to Protect Your Property and Mobility

Taking precautions ahead of peak tidal windows protects vital utilities and maintains personal independence during extreme coastal events.

- **Relocate Motor Vehicles in Advance:** Park your car on higher ground at least two hours before peak tide predictions. Avoid leaving vehicles in low carports, subterranean garages, or driveways near tidally influenced canals.
- **Elevate Essential Equipment:** Move ground-level appliances, backup medical machinery, outdoor power converters, and portable oxygen units off ground-level floors onto elevated platforms or upper stories.
- **Acquire Protective Barriers:** Connect with your local municipal public works department to locate designated distribution points for sandbags and protective barriers before water approaches your threshold.
- **Secure Medication and Critical Supplies:** Maintain several days of shelf-stable food, clean water, and vital prescription drugs in easily accessible containers so you can remain safely indoors while waters recede.
- **Never Drive Through Standing Water:** Turn around immediately if water covers the roadway. Obey all municipal road closure markers and never attempt to cross submerged intersections.

## Preparedness Checklist for High-Water Alerts

- ☐ Check your municipal flood map and local tide predictions at tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov before Monday
- ☐ Register your mobile phone for county emergency text notifications and coastal flood alerts via weather.gov
- ☐ Park all vehicles on elevated pavement at least two hours before posted high tides
- ☐ Charge all medical equipment, backup battery units, and phones while utility connections remain stable
- ☐ Raise ground-level outdoor electrical converters, heating systems, and oxygen machines away from crawl spaces
- ☐ Contact municipal public works for free community sandbag distribution locations
- ☐ Keep emergency contact numbers and non-perishable essentials readily accessible

For localized disaster preparedness guidance and community flood assistance, older Americans can contact their municipal emergency operations center or reach out to their regional Area Agency on Aging by visiting eldercare.acl.gov or calling 1-800-677-1116.
