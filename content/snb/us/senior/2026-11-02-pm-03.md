Are modern vehicle safety features making daily travel easier, or are complex dashboard alerts creating dangerous hesitation behind the wheel? According to recent federal crash analyses, **older adults aged 65 and older represent 23 percent of all licensed drivers in the United States, yet they accounted for 20 percent of nationwide traffic fatalities in 2024.** 

To address how advanced vehicle systems interact with older drivers, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) convened automotive leaders and safety officials for a national vehicle safety summit titled "Recalls Next: Technological Innovations & Safety Recalls." Federal regulators are focusing on modernizing safety recalls, upgrading vehicle lighting, and standardizing advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) to ensure critical warning systems protect rather than confuse motorists.

## By the Numbers: Crash Patterns and Older Drivers

Federal traffic data reveals that older motorists have distinctive driving patterns and risks on American roadways. In 2024, over 8,000 Americans aged 65 and older lost their lives in traffic crashes. While older adults made up 18 percent of the total U.S. population in 2024, they accounted for nearly a quarter of all licensed motorists.

A closer look at the circumstances of these collisions challenges several common assumptions about older driver safety:

- **Daylight Hours Present the Highest Risk:** A substantial 71 percent of fatal collisions involving older drivers occur during daylight hours, rather than late at night.
- **Multiple Vehicles Are Often Involved:** Two or more vehicles are involved in 66 percent of fatal crashes involving older motorists, frequently occurring at intersections where drivers must judge speed and distance.
- **High Seatbelt Compliance:** Older adults maintain strong basic safety habits, with 70 percent of older vehicle occupants killed in crashes having worn their seatbelts.

## Why Technology Alerts and Recalls Need Modernization

Modern vehicles are increasingly equipped with advanced driver assistance systems, such as blind-spot detection, lane departure warnings, and automatic emergency braking. For senior drivers, these systems can provide vital reaction support, helping maintain safe driving independence for years longer.

However, inconsistent dashboard warnings and complex user interfaces can sometimes work against driver safety. Inconsistent warning chimes, abrupt visual flashing, and complicated dashboard menus can create sensory overload, leading to dangerous hesitation at busy highway intersections.

Furthermore, federal regulators and the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) have raised concerns that millions of safety recalls and essential software updates remain unaddressed by motorists. When a vehicle's camera, radar, or safety software is out of date, driver assistance features may fail to operate as designed.

In response, ongoing federal regulatory proceedings under Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) No. 108 are reviewing vehicle lighting standards to mitigate dangerous nighttime headlight glare, while simultaneously working to standardize emergency audio-visual alert displays across automakers.

## International Standards: How Other Regions Address System Warnings

Sensory consistency in driver assistance technology is also being addressed internationally. In the European Union, the Vehicle General Safety Regulation (GSR II), enforced across member nations by the European Commission, establishes uniform human-machine interface requirements for intelligent speed assistance and lane-keeping alerts. 

By mandating standardized acoustic and visual warnings across all new vehicles regardless of make or model, the European regulation helps prevent driver confusion when switching between vehicles, offering a model for how consistent signals can support drivers of all ages.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your Safety on the Road

Staying safe behind the wheel involves both vehicle maintenance and personal skill review. Taking proactive steps ensures that automotive safety systems operate correctly:

1. **Verify Your Recall Status Online:** Automakers frequently issue free software updates and sensor recalibrations to improve driver assist functions. You can check if your vehicle has any outstanding safety notices by entering your 17-character Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).
2. **Contact the Vehicle Safety Hotline:** If you do not have internet access or prefer speaking with a representative, call the NHTSA Vehicle Safety Hotline at 1-888-327-4236 to check your vehicle's status and learn how to schedule free dealership repairs.
3. **Take a Refresher Safety Course:** The AARP Smart Driver course, offered in both classroom and online formats at [aarp.org/drive](https://www.aarp.org/drive), helps mature drivers familiarize themselves with modern ADAS features, adjust vehicle mirrors to minimize blind spots, and potentially qualify for multi-year automobile insurance discounts.

## Driver Safety Checklist for Older Motorists

- ☐ Locate your vehicle's 17-character VIN (printed on your registration card or driver's side dashboard).
- ☐ Check [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) or call 1-888-327-4236 to ensure all safety software updates are up to date.
- ☐ Schedule any outstanding dealership recall repairs, which are performed free of charge.
- ☐ Review your owner's manual to understand which alert sounds correspond to blind-spot warnings versus emergency braking.
- ☐ Consider enrolling in an AARP Smart Driver course to refresh roadway habits and learn about the newest vehicle assistance tools.
