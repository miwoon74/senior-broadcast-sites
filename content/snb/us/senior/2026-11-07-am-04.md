The IRS posted updated, inflation‑adjusted thresholds that increase the federal estate and gift tax exemptions for 2027, a change reported by CNBC on Nov. 6, 2026. Seniors and their families should check how higher 2027 exemption levels affect wills, lifetime gifts and the timing of transfers, and confirm that state rules do not change the picture.

## At a glance: what changed and why it matters

- The IRS adjusted the federal lifetime estate and gift tax exemption and related thresholds for tax year **2027** (announced and covered in reports dated **Nov. 6, 2026**).
- Media coverage noted the larger exemption for **2027**, which can reduce or eliminate federal estate tax for many households; state estate or inheritance taxes remain separate and unchanged by the federal adjustment.
- Practical impact: seniors who planned to shift property to heirs or make large lifetime gifts should reassess plans in light of the higher 2027 federal thresholds.

## What this means for seniors and heirs

- Estate tax exposure depends on the federal lifetime exemption in effect for the year of death or gift, and on each state's estate or inheritance tax rules. The IRS adjustment for **2027** may mean that estates which would previously have owed federal estate tax under earlier thresholds could now fall beneath the federal exemption.
- This federal change does not affect state estate or inheritance taxes. If you live in a state with its own estate or inheritance tax, your obligations may be unchanged.
- For many seniors, the practical choices to review now include: whether to accelerate or delay large gifts, whether to fund or amend trusts, and whether to revise wills and beneficiary designations.

## What to review now — a practical checklist

- ☐ Confirm the exact IRS dollar amounts and bracket adjustments for tax year **2027** on IRS.gov.  
- ☐ Review wills, revocable trusts and any irrevocable trust funding plans with an estate attorney in light of the 2027 thresholds.  
- ☐ Verify beneficiary designations on IRAs, 401(k)s, life insurance and annuities — these override wills in many cases.  
- ☐ Check whether your state imposes an estate or inheritance tax and how its thresholds compare to the new federal rules.  
- ☐ If considering large lifetime gifts, consult a tax advisor about timing (e.g., gifting in 2026 vs 2027) and reported gift records.  
- ☐ Keep documentation for appraisals, gifts and transfers, and update powers of attorney and health care directives as needed.

## Steps to take with professional help

1. Download the IRS guidance for estate and gift tax for tax year 2027 and bring it to your attorney or tax preparer.  
2. Ask your estate attorney whether any trusts should be funded or adjusted now versus waiting for 2027 figures to apply.  
3. Confirm that beneficiary forms at financial institutions match your current estate plan; update forms if necessary.  
4. If you expect to make significant gifts, coordinate with a CPA or tax advisor about gift‑tax reporting requirements.

## State taxes and the limits of the federal change

The IRS announcement and media coverage emphasize that federal exemption adjustments do not change state rules. Many states have their own estate or inheritance taxes with separate thresholds, so the federal increase for **2027** may not eliminate state tax exposure. Check your state's revenue or taxation agency for details.

## Summary — a practical takeaway for seniors

The IRS raised the inflation‑adjusted federal exemption for estate and gift taxes effective for **2027**, as reported on **Nov. 6, 2026**. That change can materially affect estate plans, gifting strategies and the need for federal estate tax planning for many households. However, state rules may still apply. Update your estate documents and speak with an estate attorney and tax advisor to understand the net effect for your family.

> As of Nov. 6, 2026: see IRS.gov for the exact 2027 exemption amounts and bracket adjustments, and consult state tax authorities for local rules.
