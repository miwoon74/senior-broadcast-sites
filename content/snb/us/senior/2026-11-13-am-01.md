Are you prepared for sudden cancellations and rising medical costs in your healthcare coverage? More than **60% of Minnesota Medicare Advantage enrollees**—roughly 390,000 seniors—are seeing their plans terminated or sharply restructured for the coming year. As major private insurers scale back regional coverage and tighten networks, older adults across the state are confronting unexpected premium spikes, lost provider networks, and steep out-of-pocket exposure. Understanding these changes before the federal open enrollment deadline is vital to protecting your health and household retirement budget.

## By the Numbers: How the Market Shift Impacts Beneficiaries

Major commercial insurers, including Blue Cross Blue Shield, are restructuring regional offerings due to mounting healthcare costs and tighter federal reimbursements. The impact is especially severe in rural areas, where private plan exits have left older residents with few coverage alternatives.

Beneficiaries in these communities are being forced to transition to supplemental policies, which can carry double the premiums of their prior coverage. Without careful planning, a household may absorb as much as $7,000 in unexpected annual out-of-pocket expenses and hospital deductibles.

## Why Private Insurers Are Scaling Back Advantage Plans

Medicare Advantage plans operate as private alternatives to Original Medicare, contracting with the federal government to provide Part A and Part B benefits, often bundled with Part D prescription drug coverage. When private carriers exit regional markets or drop specific products, several issues emerge:

- **Provider Network Disruptions:** Plan terminations immediately sever existing relationships with primary care doctors, regional specialists, and preferred hospital systems.
- **Lost Ancillary Benefits:** Supplemental coverage for dental, vision, hearing, and wellness programs is often sharply trimmed or eliminated entirely during restructuring.
- **Automatic Crosswalk Risks:** If a senior is crosswalked into an unfamiliar default plan without an active review, they may encounter high deductible structures and unexpected prescription copays on a fixed Social Security income.

## Global Comparison: Public Universal Healthcare Models

Cross-border comparisons illustrate how different national frameworks manage senior care. According to Health Canada, Canada's public Medicare system delivers universal coverage for medically necessary hospital and physician services funded directly through general taxation. Because standard healthcare is managed provincially as a single-payer model, Canadian seniors do not face private insurer market exits, shifting annual provider networks, or abrupt year-end plan terminations.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your Coverage Before December 7

Navigating an unexpected plan loss requires swift, proactive steps before the federal open enrollment deadline on December 7. Independent assistance is available to help beneficiaries compare alternatives and minimize out-of-pocket costs.

- ☐ **Examine your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC):** Review the notice sent by your current carrier to verify if your plan is being discontinued, restructured, or if premiums and copays are increasing.
- ☐ **Audit your doctors and specialists:** Contact your physicians directly or check updated provider directories to confirm they remain in-network for any prospective 2027 plan.
- ☐ **Run your prescriptions on Medicare.gov:** Enter your complete medication list and dosages into the official Plan Finder tool to evaluate real out-of-pocket drug expenses across competing options.
- ☐ **Confirm hospital deductible exposure:** Check whether alternative policies carry high out-of-pocket inpatient hospital deductibles or co-insurance requirements.
- ☐ **Schedule an unbiased counseling session:** Connect with trained advisors to evaluate supplemental policies and enrollment options without sales pressure.

State-funded programs provide neutral, free guidance to prevent costly enrollment errors. Beneficiaries can call Minnesota Aging Pathways at 1-800-333-2433, contact their local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) through shiphelp.org, or explore tools online at Medicare.gov.
