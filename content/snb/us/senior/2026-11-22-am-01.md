If you spent decades maintaining your lawn, garden, or driveway with Roundup weed killer, could past chemical exposure now qualify you for substantial legal compensation? Following years of protracted courtroom battles across state and federal jurisdictions, a Missouri state judge has issued final approval for a nationwide class-action settlement valued at up to **$7.25 billion**. The landmark decision creates a long-term claims structure specifically designed to compensate individuals who developed non-Hodgkin lymphoma after handling glyphosate-based herbicides.

For hundreds of thousands of older homeowners, retirees, and hobby gardeners across the United States, this ruling removes the painful uncertainty of prolonged personal litigation. Rather than facing exhausting individual depositions or waiting through multi-year trial dockets, qualifying individuals and surviving family members can now access direct financial compensation to help ease medical bills and care expenses.

## The Settlement by the Numbers: Resolving 65,000 Claims

On November 20, 2026, Judge Timothy Boyer of the Circuit Court of the City of St. Louis, Missouri, granted final approval to the agreement, finding the terms fair, adequate, and reasonable. The order effectively resolves approximately 65,000 active state-court lawsuits filed against Bayer and its subsidiary Monsanto.

The newly sanctioned framework establishes a rolling compensation reserve structured to operate over the next 21 years. This extended schedule ensures that financial resources remain available not only for current cancer patients but also for individuals who may receive a diagnosis in the coming years.

Agrochemical manufacturer Bayer acquired Monsanto in 2018 in a transaction valued at $63 billion. While the company settled an earlier wave of legal claims in 2020 for $10.9 billion, thousands of newer cases and unresolved liabilities lingered in state venues. Throughout the proceedings, Bayer has consistently maintained that glyphosate does not cause cancer, while moving forward with this comprehensive resolution to cap ongoing legal expenses and shield elderly claimants from repeated court appeals. Under the final order, $600 million is allocated toward legal representation and court costs, leaving the principal balance to fund eligible claimant awards.

## Individual Payout Ranges and Evaluation Criteria

Under the court order, qualifying claimants who received a formal medical diagnosis of non-Hodgkin lymphoma are eligible to receive awards spanning between $10,000 and $165,000. 

Because class members face widely differing personal situations, individual compensation is not distributed as a single flat payment. Instead, the court-supervised fund administrator applies an objective evaluation matrix based on documented records.

The court-established scoring system evaluates three primary criteria:

1. **Medical Severity and Diagnosis History:** Claimants must provide certified pathology records and clinical documentation confirming an active or previous diagnosis of non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Individuals with advanced disease stages or aggressive clinical regimens receive higher scoring weights.
2. **Documented Product Exposure:** The claims administrator reviews evidence demonstrating habitual residential, commercial, or agricultural use of glyphosate herbicides over an extended duration prior to diagnosis.
3. **Claimant Age at Diagnosis:** Because toxic tort resolutions prioritize recovery for vulnerable older adults, claimant age factors directly into payment calculations. For example, a 72-year-old home gardener with documented treatments could secure an award within the $10,000 to $165,000 range to directly offset out-of-pocket medical bills and caregiving costs.

## Why This Ruling Matters for Older Homeowners

For several decades, millions of American homeowners applied residential Roundup formulations across gardens, flowerbeds, fence lines, and driveways. Most consumer applications took place without personal protective gear such as chemical respirators, face shields, or specialized gloves. While Bayer removed glyphosate from consumer-facing residential lawn products in the United States several years ago, latency periods for non-Hodgkin lymphoma mean older individuals diagnosed in recent years trace their exposure back to regular seasonal yard maintenance conducted during the 1990s and 2000s.

Recent appellate decisions concerning federal pesticide labeling laws had created considerable legal ambiguity, leaving thousands of aging plaintiffs stranded in legal uncertainty. For elderly plaintiffs, going to trial often requires stressful multi-day cross-examinations and long delays during appellate reviews. The Missouri court's final approval creates a direct administrative alternative, allowing seniors to submit authenticated records online or through legal representatives and receive binding disbursements without entering a courtroom.

## Practical Steps to Prepare Your Records

Filing windows operate under strict administrative deadlines established by the court. If you or a loved one used residential Roundup regularly and received a medical diagnosis of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, taking prompt steps to organize records will protect your eligibility.

- ☐ **Locate Diagnostic and Pathology Records:** Contact your treating oncologist, hospital, or hematology clinic to retrieve formal diagnostic confirmation verifying non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
- ☐ **Gather Proof of Product Purchase and Use:** Inspect old garage or utility shed shelves, lawn-care logs, credit card archives, store receipts, or invoices showing historical purchases of glyphosate-based weed killers.
- ☐ **Log Exposure Timelines:** Prepare a clear, written chronology detailing approximate years, frequency of application, and specific residential or property addresses where product spraying occurred.
- ☐ **Verify Information on the Official Portal:** Access the court-approved settlement claims administrator portal online or contact retained legal counsel to confirm submission deadlines and review claim forms.
- ☐ **Audit Current Shed Chemicals:** Dispose of any lingering legacy residential weed killers containing glyphosate at an authorized local hazardous waste collection site and switch to EPA-registered bio-based alternatives.
- ☐ **Seek Independent Clinical Resources:** If you need independent cancer support, call the National Cancer Institute's Cancer Information Service at 1-800-422-6237 for treatment guidance and clinical trial information.
- ☐ **Access Caregiver and Family Support:** Older adults or caregivers seeking local supportive services can reach the federal Eldercare Locator online at eldercare.acl.gov or by calling 1-800-677-1116.
