Are you wondering what to give your teenage or young adult grandchildren this holiday season without having to decipher complicated electronics or buy another impersonal gift card? According to consumer research, **78 percent of Generation Z young adults are asking for screen-free, analog gifts** like board games, puzzles, and art supplies. 

For older adults who often worry that glowing smartphone screens create an insurmountable barrier between generations, this emerging shift represents a welcome change. Rather than chasing the latest digital gadgets, younger adults are actively looking for tactile objects and authentic, face-to-face interactions. Understanding this trend can help you plan meaningful gatherings and choose gifts that your grandchildren will genuinely treasure.

## By the Numbers: The Analog Shift Among Young Adults

The data from retail and consumer surveys points to an unexpected divergence between age groups. Generation Z (young adults ages 12 to 27) grew up in an entirely digital world, yet they are expressing an outsized desire to log off during the holidays.

The PwC 2026 Holiday Outlook reveals how strongly younger consumers are leaning away from screens when compared to the broader shopping public:



As the survey illustrates, 78 percent of Gen Z consumers desire screen-free analog gifts, compared to 64 percent of consumers overall. Furthermore, 81 percent of Gen Z respondents say they prioritize in-person activities over digital gadgets this season—representing the strongest generational preference recorded. 

Retail market trackers are seeing the exact same pattern in purchasing behavior. Wholesale marketplace platform Faire reports that retail demand for analog crafts, vintage board games, and tactile items is up by more than 60 percent heading into the Thanksgiving holiday.

## Understanding Digital Fatigue and the Craving for Connection

Why are young adults turning away from the digital devices that defined their teenage years? Researchers and sociologists point to widespread digital fatigue. Constant smartphone notifications, video feeds, and remote interactions have left many young people feeling disconnected from tangible reality.

Instead of seeking more digital input, younger consumers are embracing vintage aesthetics and nostalgic traditions. They are searching for authentic social moments that provide comfort, calm, and genuine connection. A staggering 63 percent of Gen Z respondents stated that sharing a home-cooked family meal holds far greater importance than receiving an expensive commercial present.

This cultural movement plays directly to the strengths of grandparents. You do not need technical expertise or familiarity with modern video games to connect with your grandchildren. The skills, stories, and slow-paced traditions you have practiced for decades are precisely what younger family members are looking for today.

## Practical Ways to Connect Across Generations

Planning an offline holiday gathering does not need to be complicated or costly. A few simple, hands-on activities can turn an ordinary Thanksgiving or holiday afternoon into an unforgettable bonding experience.

### 1. Set Up an Analog Game Table
Bring out classic, cooperative tabletop games that encourage conversation. Favorites like Scrabble, Rummikub, trivia games, or a large jigsaw puzzle allow everyone to gather around the table at their own pace without anyone feeling pressured.

### 2. Share Cherished Kitchen Recipes
Cooking together is one of the most natural ways to bridge generational divides. Invite your grandchild into the kitchen to bake a favorite holiday pie or prepare a family side dish. Along the way, share the handwritten cooking techniques and personal family stories behind the food.

### 3. Gift Tactile, Creative Tools
If you want to wrap a physical present, choose items that invite hands-on creativity rather than screen time:
- An instant or analog film camera (such as a classic Polaroid)
- A high-quality leather journal with analog stationery
- A beginner knitting, crochet, or craft kit
- Vintage board games or card sets

### 4. Assemble a Handwritten Family Recipe Book
Take time to transcribe your signature family recipes into a notebook or binder by hand. Passing down these culinary secrets gives younger relatives a physical keepsake they can reference for years to come.

## Family Holiday Planning Checklist

Use this quick checklist to prepare an inviting, screen-free atmosphere for your upcoming family gatherings:

- ☐ Designate a dedicated table space for a jigsaw puzzle, cards, or a board game tournament
- ☐ Select two or three family recipes to cook or bake together with teenage or college-age grandchildren
- ☐ Gather any needed craft supplies, art materials, or baking ingredients ahead of the holiday weekend
- ☐ Choose one or two tactile, screen-free gifts (such as a journal, craft kit, or analog camera)
- ☐ Review intergenerational activity ideas and community toolkits on AARP.org or gu.org
