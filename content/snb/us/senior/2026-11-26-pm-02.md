The IRS has reminded retirees that required minimum distributions (RMDs) for the 2026 tax year generally must be taken by **December 31, 2026**. Missing an RMD can trigger an IRS excise tax or other penalties, so check accounts and plan now.

## Numbers to know at a glance

- RMD deadline for 2026: December 31, 2026.
- Example account used by the IRS guidance: $200,000 (account balance as of Dec. 31, 2025).
- Example life‑expectancy factor: 25.6.
- Resulting example 2026 RMD: about $7,813.
- Penalty for missed RMDs: described in 2026 guidance as up to 25% in historical changes and reduced excise tax rules (see IRS pages for details).

## How the RMD is calculated (simple explanation)

RMDs for 2026 are generally calculated using the retirement account balance as of December 31 of the prior year (for example, Dec. 31, 2025) divided by an IRS life‑expectancy factor for the owner (an example factor given is 25.6). The broadcast example shows:

- Account balance on Dec. 31, 2025: $200,000
- Life‑expectancy factor: 25.6
- 2026 RMD ≈ $200,000 ÷ 25.6 = $7,813

This is the IRS approach described in their 2026 RMD guidance; use your actual year‑end balance and the IRS table or worksheet for your exact factor.

## Why meeting the Dec. 31 deadline matters

1. Tax penalty risk: The IRS guidance explains that missed RMDs can trigger excise taxes or other penalties. Recent law changes reduced earlier, harsher excise tax rates and set out correction procedures, but the IRS still emphasizes timely withdrawals.
2. Impact on taxable income: An RMD increases taxable income for the year it is received. A large, late withdrawal can raise your overall tax bill for 2026 and could affect means‑tested programs tied to income.
3. Administrative complications: Plan custodians, IRA trustees, and employers process distributions on schedules. Delaying contact with them increases the risk a distribution is not completed by Dec. 31.

## Practical steps to take this week

- ☐ Check whether you are subject to an RMD for 2026. If you hold a traditional IRA, SEP IRA, SIMPLE IRA, or employer retirement plan, review your account statements and eligibility rules.
- ☐ Get your Dec. 31, 2025 year‑end account balance from each retirement plan that requires an RMD.
- ☐ Use the IRS RMD worksheet or calculator on IRS.gov to find your life‑expectancy factor and compute the exact 2026 RMD.
- ☐ Contact your plan custodian now to request the distribution or set up an automatic RMD if they offer it; confirm processing time to meet the Dec. 31 deadline.
- ☐ If you missed an earlier RMD, review the IRS relief procedures and consider whether Form 5329 is necessary; consult a tax pro if unsure.
- ☐ Keep records of the distribution amount, calculation, and communications with the custodian in case the IRS asks for documentation.

## Who should contact a professional

- Anyone unsure how the IRS life‑expectancy factor applies to their situation.
- Those who have multiple accounts and want to coordinate RMDs across them.
- People who may face a large tax consequence from adding an RMD to other income sources.
- Those who missed a required distribution in a prior year and need correction help.

For low‑cost assistance, IRS guidance mentions tools on IRS.gov, and the broadcast noted that programs such as AARP Tax‑Aide and VITA may offer local help (see local program pages).

## How the IRS presents corrections and penalties

The IRS guidance for 2026 explains procedures to correct missed RMDs and describes how penalty provisions were changed in recent years (the guidance includes exceptions and calculation methods). If you discover a missed RMD, follow the IRS correction steps and keep documentation. If in doubt, consult the IRS pages or a qualified tax advisor.
