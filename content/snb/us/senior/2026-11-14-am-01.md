When arranging in-home nursing or palliative support for an aging parent, spouse, or yourself, how can you be sure the business knocking on your door is a legitimate clinical provider? On November 12, 2026, federal health officials took decisive action to safeguard older Americans by announcing a **6-month extension of the nationwide freeze on new Medicare home health and hospice enrollments, keeping restrictions in place through May 12, 2027.**

The federal government first enacted this emergency freeze on May 13, 2026, after watchdogs and investigators discovered widespread schemes targeting vulnerable seniors. The extension announced by Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz ensures bad actors cannot enter the Medicare system. CMS said it would consider limited adjustments to enrollment rules to allow certain certified agencies to expand operations; check the latest at cms.gov.

## Understanding the Federal Freeze: What the Moratorium Means

The nationwide moratorium targets newly forming hospice and home health startups seeking Medicare billing privileges. Under normal circumstances, any newly formed business meeting baseline licensing guidelines could apply to bill Medicare for skilled nursing, physical therapy, aide assistance, and end-of-life care. However, rampant exploitation forced regulators to halt the influx of unvetted enterprises.

## Why CMS Stepped In: Halting Deceptive Schemes

Federal authorities enacted the moratorium in response to rapid rises in fraudulent operations known as "churn and burn" enterprises. In these deceptive schemes, bad actors established shell agencies designed solely to bill federal healthcare programs for care that never happened.

These dishonest operators frequently recruited older adults without their knowledge or consent. In severe instances, unscrupulous businesses falsely certified non-terminal patients for hospice care. Hospice care is intended for individuals facing terminal conditions, and signing someone up under false pretenses strips away access to curative medical treatments and routine home health therapies.

When a bogus provider ties up your Medicare record, you face serious complications:

- **Interrupted Care:** Medicare may reject legitimate claims submitted by genuine physical therapists or nurses because a fraudulent agency has already billed under your Medicare number.
- **Compromised Benefits:** Falsely listing a patient under hospice benefits can lead to the cancellation of necessary curative medications, standard physician consultations, and hospital-based rehabilitation.
- **Lack of Clinical Oversight:** Families believe their loved one is under active medical supervision, only to discover that promised clinical visits, safety evaluations, and pain management are never delivered.

By barring new startups, CMS blocks fraudulent operators from cycling through corporate names and collecting taxpayer funds while abandoning beneficiaries.

## How Established Agencies Can Expand Under the Modified Rule

While freezing new entrants protects consumers from fraud, healthcare advocates raised concerns about service shortages in rapidly growing senior communities. Without additional capacity, older adults recovering from surgery or managing chronic conditions could face delays in receiving in-home nursing or therapy.

To resolve this issue, CMS modified the moratorium rules alongside the 6-month extension announced in November 2026. Under the revised framework:

- **Brand-New Startups Remain Barred:** Unaffiliated corporate entities and newly created businesses cannot enroll in Medicare or obtain new billing numbers.
- **Satellite Branch Expansions Permitted:** Established home health and hospice agencies that maintain clean compliance histories and full Medicare certification are authorized to open new branch locations.

This modification allows proven, high-performing agencies to bring certified nurses, aides, and therapists directly into underserved neighborhoods. Families gain access to expanding local services backed by agencies with transparent track records.

## Global Oversight: How Other Nations Tackle In-Home Care Billing

The struggle to police home-based care is not unique to the United States. Across developed nations, aging populations rely heavily on home care, prompting international regulators to tighten oversight.

In the United Kingdom, the National Health Service and the Care Quality Commission faced comparable challenges involving private home-care contractors overbilling local social councils. In 2025, British authorities responded by implementing automated registry audits. Under these digital licensing systems, domiciliary care providers must pass unannounced operational checks and ongoing digital verification before qualifying to receive public funds. 

The coordinated efforts in both the United States and the United Kingdom highlight a shared global objective: ensuring public healthcare resources flow exclusively to legitimate clinicians who deliver verifiable, compassionate care.

## How Seniors and Families Can Verify Providers

Federal regulations provide essential guardrails, but family members and beneficiaries should take active measures when choosing an in-home care agency. Before signing intake paperwork for physical therapy, occupational therapy, skilled nursing, or hospice support, verify that the agency is fully credentialed and reputable.

Use these practical steps to safeguard your benefits:

- ☐ **Verify Agency Standing on Medicare Care Compare:** Visit **Medicare.gov/care-compare** to inspect an agency's quality ratings, inspection reports, and patient safety records.
- ☐ **Call Medicare Directly for Guidance:** If you have questions about whether a provider is authorized, contact **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227)** to confirm certification status before receiving services.
- ☐ **Scrutinize Intake Paperwork:** Never sign blank documents or agreements presented by unsolicited salespeople offering free household items or unsolicited medical equipment in exchange for your Medicare number.
- ☐ **Confirm Hospice Eligibility with Your Own Doctor:** Never accept end-of-life or palliative care enrollment unless your trusted, independent physician evaluates your health condition and discusses the diagnosis directly with you.
- ☐ **Inspect Quarterly Medicare Summary Notices:** Carefully review every Medicare Summary Notice (MSN) you receive in the mail. Check that all listed home health visits, therapy dates, and hospice claims match the actual care provided in your home.
- ☐ **Report Unauthorized Billing Immediately:** If an unfamiliar agency appears on your billing notices, contact Medicare fraud hotlines immediately to protect your benefits from unauthorized claims.
