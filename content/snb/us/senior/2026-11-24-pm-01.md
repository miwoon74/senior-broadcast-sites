Are you preparing to travel across the country to join family for Thanksgiving dinner this week? If you are packing your bags or fueling your car, you will be sharing the nation's transit routes with record-breaking crowds under volatile weather conditions. According to holiday travel forecasts released by the American Automobile Association (AAA), an estimated **81.5 million** Americans are projected to journey 50 miles or more during the 2026 Thanksgiving travel period. At the very same moment, severe dual storm systems are battering both the Atlantic and Pacific coastlines. 

For older adults, severe weather travel is not simply an inconvenience or an annoying scheduling delay. Heavy downpours, wind gusts, freezing mountain passes, and congested airport terminals introduce substantial physical stress and safety risks. Navigating slick interstates increases crash hazards, while sudden gate changes or missed flight connections can leave travelers stranded without access to necessary prescriptions. Taking a proactive approach to monitoring weather advisories, packing medical essentials, and adjusting departure times can help you reach your loved ones safely and comfortably.

## Tracking the Weather: Dual Storm Systems by the Numbers

Weather forecasts from the National Weather Service indicate that dual coastal storm systems are simultaneously disrupting transit across the United States. In the West, a powerful atmospheric river system is colliding with king tides along the Pacific Northwest, pushing heavy precipitation and runoff inland. Meanwhile, a developing coastal low-pressure system off Florida and the Carolinas is tracking upward along the Atlantic Seaboard, generating strong coastal storm surges and dangerous localized flooding.

## Why Severe Weather Creates Unique Hazards for Older Travelers

When bad weather hits during peak holiday volume, the challenges compound rapidly for older motorists and airline passengers. Understanding these risks before leaving home is the best way to guard against preventable accidents and health complications.

### 1. Highway Flooding and Diminished Road Visibility
Intense precipitation from atmospheric rivers reduces tire traction and severely limits visibility. When highway water pools on high-speed routes like Interstate 5, the risk of hydroplaning increases dramatically. For drivers coping with changing vision or slower reaction times, driving through torrential rain at dusk or at night strains the eyes and heightens fatigue. In higher elevations along Interstate 80 and Interstate 90, rain quickly transitions into slick mountain pass winter conditions, requiring tire chains and extreme caution. If you encounter flooded roads, trip times can easily double, turning a routine afternoon drive into an exhausting ordeal.

### 2. Airport Delays and Physical Fatigue
Air travel presents its own set of complications during severe weather. High winds exceeding 40 miles per hour cause air traffic control holds and missed flight connections, especially in dense connecting hubs like San Francisco or New York. For older travelers, delayed flights often mean miles of walking between distant gates, navigating crowded terminals with heavy baggage, or facing unexpected overnight stranding. Most critically, when checked luggage is separated from passengers during unexpected delays, vital daily medications stored in suitcases become completely inaccessible.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your Safety and Health

Careful planning can turn a potentially dangerous trip into a manageable, low-stress journey. Older travelers should implement several key precautions before heading out this week:

- **Protect Your Prescription Medications**: Never pack daily prescription medications in checked luggage or leave them in the trunk of your car. Keep a minimum of seven days' worth of essential prescription drugs, duplicate doctor documentation, and non-perishable snacks in your personal carry-on bag or within arm's reach inside the passenger cabin. If an afternoon flight delay forces an overnight stay, or highway flooding leaves you stranded in traffic, your vital health needs remain uninterrupted.
- **Adjust Your Travel Timing**: If your route along Interstate 5 or coastal highways is under a flash flood watch on Wednesday, consider postponing your departure until Thursday morning. Leaving after heavy rainfall subsides gives road crews time to clear debris, lowers traffic density, and allows you to travel entirely during daylight hours.
- **Monitor Real-Time Road Conditions**: Before getting behind the wheel, check your state's Department of Transportation travel information (many states provide 511 services) or visit your state DOT website — check the latest at your state DOT website.site. These official portals provide live camera feeds, road closure alerts, and detour maps for corridors like Interstate 5, Interstate 80, and Interstate 90.
- **Track Flight Delays via Official Channels**: If you are flying, download your airline's official smartphone application and enroll in automated text notifications for departure gates and flight times. You can also view broader national airspace operations and system delays through the Federal Aviation Administration operations center website.
- **Store Emergency Roadside Contacts**: Program vital assistance numbers into your mobile phone before departure. Keep the national AAA Roadside Assistance dispatch line (1-800-222-4357) saved for rapid towing, battery, or tire service. If you need local community transportation support or regional roadside safety resources, contact your area agency dispatch via the Eldercare Locator directory (1-800-677-1116).

## Pre-Departure Travel Safety Checklist

Review this checklist before locking your front door:

- [ ] I have packed at least seven days' worth of daily prescription medications and medical documents in my carry-on bag.
- [ ] I have verified real-time weather advisories and road closure maps on my state 511 Department of Transportation website.
- [ ] I have checked the flight operations status at my departure, arrival, and connecting airports via my carrier or the Federal Aviation Administration website.
- [ ] I have stored emergency roadside assistance numbers, including AAA (1-800-222-4357), in my mobile phone contacts.
- [ ] I have evaluated my departure schedule and considered delaying Wednesday travel to Thursday morning if flood warnings impact my route.
- [ ] My vehicle phone charger is accessible inside the passenger cabin, and my mobile phone is fully charged.
- [ ] I have shared my planned travel route, rest stops, and estimated arrival time with family members.

Arriving at your destination safely is far more important than arriving strictly on schedule. Give yourself permission to slow down, pull off the highway at safe rest areas during blinding downpours, and adjust your itinerary whenever safety demands it.
