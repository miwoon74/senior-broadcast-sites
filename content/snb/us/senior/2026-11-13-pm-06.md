Have you found yourself wondering whether your grandchildren would rather spend their holiday season staring into a smartphone or visiting crowded shopping centers? Retail research published in November 2026 reveals a striking shift in consumer behavior: **78 percent of Generation Z shoppers prefer in-person holiday shopping alongside family members or friends rather than buying gifts alone on a screen.** 

Rather than seeking out detached digital gadgets or isolated e-commerce browsing, younger consumers are embracing traditional holiday customs. This trend opens an exceptional door for grandparents and older relatives to step forward, share classic family rituals, and create genuine holiday memories together.

## The Numbers Behind the Real-World Comeback

For years, headlines predicted that digital storefronts would completely replace neighborhood shopping outings. However, comprehensive consumer analysis conducted with over 10,000 young consumers—specifically Generation Z, representing individuals aged 14 to 29—shows that youth culture is moving in the exact opposite direction during the holiday season.

According to the International Council of Shopping Centers (ICSC) 2026 Holiday Intentions Survey, 97 percent of Generation Z shoppers plan to visit physical brick-and-mortar storefronts this season. The tactile atmosphere of stores, holiday window displays, and shared social outings have replaced the appeal of solo web browsing.

## Why the "Grannycore" Aesthetic Is Surging

Retail tracking analysts and youth culture researchers have documented a sharp rise in late 2026 around what the industry terms "Grannycore." After enduring years of constant screen exposure and ambient digital chaos, younger Americans are experiencing genuine digital fatigue. 

Young adults are seeking out items and activities that evoke comfort, security, and nostalgia:
- **Homemade Vintage Treats:** Interest in classic, slow-baked heritage cookies and breads has outpaced automated delivery options.
- **Physical Family Games:** Rather than video games or online entertainment, young family members are requesting board games that facilitate conversation.
- **Heirloom Tableware and Decor:** Vintage ornaments, handcrafted seasonal decor, and traditional dinner table setups are favored over disposable modern designs.

This generational pivot represents an emotional craving for authenticity. Generation Z is looking for roots, tangible textures, and personal family lore. Older family members already possess the skills, memories, and recipes that the younger generation considers deeply meaningful.

## An International Shift Toward Handcrafted Traditions

This yearning for genuine, slow holiday customs is not limited to the United States. Similar cultural movements are taking place across European markets.

In France, youth retail monitoring by the Chamber of Commerce reveals that over 60 percent of Generation Z shoppers intentionally patronize traditional artisan winter street fairs (*Marchés de Noël*) and antique flea markets (*brocantes*). French youth are deliberately choosing handcrafted traditional goods to support sustainable family gifting, sidestepping mass-produced online inventory in favor of local community roots.

Across borders, younger generations are realizing that digital convenience cannot replace the sensory warmth of holiday markets, smell of winter spices, and warmth of generational storytelling.

## Practical Ways to Connect with Your Grandchildren

Because younger generations are deliberately seeking screen-free holiday experiences, you do not need an elaborate or costly plan to engage them. Small, classic activities carry far more emotional weight than tech gadgets or generic gift cards.

### 1. Host a Heritage Baking Afternoon
Invite your grandchildren into your kitchen for an afternoon dedicated to making your family's traditional holiday cookies, breads, or pies. Teach them the hands-on techniques that cannot be learned from a quick web search—such as judging dough consistency by touch. Write down the family recipe by hand on an index card so they have a keepsake to take home.

### 2. Plan an In-Person Shopping Excursion
Instead of asking for an online link, arrange an outing to explore your local downtown shops, craft fairs, or seasonal markets together. Offer to stroll through festive storefronts, stop for a hot drink, and let them help you choose gifts for other family members. The excursion itself is the gift.

### 3. Introduce Analog Family Traditions
Dust off traditional board games, bring out family photo albums, or teach them a holiday craft you enjoyed at their age. Ask your grandchildren which family stories and seasonal customs they would most like to carry forward.

## Checklist for Multigenerational Holiday Planning

- ☐ Reach out to your grandchildren to schedule a specific date for a shared holiday outing or kitchen afternoon.
- ☐ Select one or two heritage recipes that carry personal family history.
- ☐ Prepare handwritten recipe cards or printed copies that younger relatives can save.
- ☐ Map out an easy walking route through a local shopping district, holiday market, or main street.
- ☐ Set aside physical board games or classic activities that encourage conversation without smartphones.
- ☐ Ask younger relatives what traditional customs they would most like to learn from you this year.
