Could your daily blood pressure medicine contain hidden manufacturing debris? Federal health regulators announced an urgent voluntary nationwide recall of **60 mg extended-release propranolol hydrochloride capsules** after identifying intermediate materials suspected of introducing microscopic metal fragments into the finished medication. 

Propranolol is a widely used beta-blocker taken by millions of older adults for hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, and migraine prevention. Discovering a foreign material alert on an essential heart pill creates immediate worry. However, abruptly stopping cardiovascular medication carries its own severe risks. Understanding the exact lot affected, the biological risks involved, and the steps to secure a safe replacement ensures you protect your health without disrupting your blood pressure management.

## The Specific Drug Lot Involved in the 2026 Recall

The recall focuses on a single production run of generic propranolol hydrochloride extended-release capsules distributed across the United States. Manufactured by Adare Pharma Solutions and distributed by Breckenridge Pharmaceutical, the affected units were shipped to wholesalers and retail pharmacies over a concise window in the autumn of 2026.

| Recall Parameter | Official Notice Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Product Name** | Propranolol Hydrochloride Extended-Release Capsules |
| **Dosage Strength** | 60 mg |
| **Recalled Lot Number** | PK25110035 |
| **Expiration Date** | May 2029 |
| **Distribution Window** | October 26 – November 4, 2026 |
| **Primary Health Risk** | Soft-tissue lacerations (mouth, throat, esophagus, digestive tract) |

Pharmacies nationwide received these bottles between October 26 and November 4, 2026. If your prescription was refilled outside of this window, or if your dosage is different (such as 80 mg, 120 mg, or immediate-release tablets), your bottle is not part of this specific recall action.

## Understanding the Physical and Medical Hazards

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) enforcement alert highlights two distinct health concerns: the physical hazard posed by the contaminated pills, and the medical hazard of stopping heart medication suddenly.

### 1. Physical Tissue Damage from Metal Shavings
The manufacturer discovered that intermediate materials used during capsule production were suspected of harboring metal shavings. Ingesting microscopic or small metal fragments poses acute hazards of physical soft-tissue lacerations to the:
- Mouth and oral mucosa
- Throat and esophagus
- Stomach lining
- Intestinal tract

While no adverse injury events had been confirmed prior to the recall publication, regulators treat internal laceration risks as serious safety threats requiring immediate market withdrawal.

### 2. The Danger of Abrupt Beta-Blocker Withdrawal
Cardiologists and community health nurses warn that patients must never stop taking beta-blockers cold turkey. Propranolol regulates heart rate and arterial pressure. Abruptly quitting the medication can trigger dangerous rebound spikes in blood pressure, hypertensive crises, or irregular heart rhythms (cardiac arrhythmias). 

If you discover your bottle matches the recalled lot, you must continue your blood pressure therapy using an untainted replacement bottle arranged through your pharmacy rather than simply throwing your pills away.

## Step-by-Step: How to Inspect Your Bottle and Secure a Replacement

Follow these concrete steps to verify your medicine cabinet today:

1. **Locate the Lot Code on the Pharmacy Label:** Look closely at the printed white prescription label or the manufacturer bottle. Look for the letters "LOT" followed by numbers. Check whether it reads **PK25110035** with an expiration date of **May 2029**.
2. **Do Not Throw the Bottle Out:** Keep the capsules inside the bottle so your pharmacist can verify the lot number and issue an exchange.
3. **Contact Your Dispensing Pharmacy Immediately:** Call the drugstore or mail-order facility that filled your prescription. Under official FDA recall procedures, dispensing pharmacies replace recalled lots at no cost to the patient.
4. **Speak With the Manufacturer for Returns:** Breckenridge Pharmaceutical provides a dedicated medical affairs help line at **1-800-367-3395** for reimbursement information and return-packaging guidelines.
5. **Report Any Adverse Reactions:** If you suspect you experienced physical discomfort or injury from a defective capsule, submit an official report to the FDA — check the latest at www.fda.gov MedWatch Safety Information program online or by calling **1-800-332-1088**.

- ☐ Locate your current bottle of 60 mg propranolol hydrochloride extended-release.
- ☐ Inspect the printed label for lot number PK25110035 and May 2029 expiration.
- ☐ Verify whether your prescription was filled or refilled between October 26 and November 4, 2026.
