Have you ever considered taking a vacation planned entirely by your teenage or young-adult grandchild? Across the United States, a growing number of families are doing exactly that. According to travel industry research released in 2026, **28% of Generation Z respondents report planning vacations specifically with their grandparents.** 

This multigenerational travel style, increasingly known as "gramping," is redefining how families spend their leisure time. Rather than opting for crowded resort corridors or solo trips with peers, young adults born between 1997 and 2012 are intentionally choosing relaxed getaways with their elders. For older adults, this trend opens up a wonderful opportunity to build lasting memories, explore the outdoors, and connect deeply across generational lines without the stress of managing every single detail alone.

## By the Numbers: How Younger Generations Travel

Consumer travel research from Skyscanner and national booking platforms highlights how central multigenerational experiences have become to modern vacation habits. Young travelers are not simply tagging along on traditional family vacations; they are actively seeking out time with older relatives and choosing destinations that encourage meaningful conversation.

The survey numbers indicate a profound shift away from fast-paced, high-stress tourism toward thoughtful, shared experiences:

- **28% of Gen Z travelers plan trips with grandparents:** More than one in four young adults surveyed specifically prioritize traveling with their grandparents.
- **37% engage in three-generation trips:** More than a third participate in extended family getaways that unite grandparents, adult children, and grandchildren.
- **58% prefer nature and mountain escapes:** Rather than bustling amusement parks or noisy urban commercial strips, a clear majority favor quiet mountain retreats, national parks, and scenic landscapes that offer calm surroundings.
- **72% utilize smart digital tools to plan:** Nearly three-quarters of young travelers actively use digital applications and artificial intelligence to map out custom itineraries tailored to their older relatives.

## Why Nature Escapes and Shared Planning Connect Generations

What is driving this desire among young adults to travel with their grandparents? After years dominated by smartphone screens, virtual interactions, and social media pressures, young people are seeking genuine, real-world connections. A quiet cabin, a scenic mountain overlook, or a lakeside trail offers an environment free from everyday distractions, allowing family members to engage in authentic conversation.

This dynamic also rebalances the responsibilities of family travel. Historically, grandparents or parents carried the full burden of organizing schedules, booking rooms, and navigating unfamiliar roads. Today, the collaboration looks very different:

1. **Digital Logistics Handled by Grandchildren:** Because 72% of Gen Z travelers utilize digital tools and artificial intelligence to organize travel, they can take pride in managing mobile boarding passes, researching scenic rest stops, and locating restaurants that cater to specific dietary preferences.
2. **Accessible and Comfortable Accommodations:** Tech-savvy grandchildren are adept at filtering lodging options for step-free access, convenient walk-in showers, and quiet ground-floor rooms, ensuring that older relatives stay comfortable throughout the trip.
3. **Space for Stories and Wisdom:** With logistics settled digitally, grandparents are free from travel-related anxiety and can focus on sharing family history and life lessons.ry, personal stories, and life perspectives, creating an emotionally rich environment that grandchildren genuinely value.

## International Perspective: The UK's Three-Generation Boom

The desire to travel across generations is not unique to the United States. In the United Kingdom, domestic tourism providers report a parallel transformation in family vacation habits. 

Between 2024 and 2026, bookings for three-generation—or "3G"—holidays in the British countryside surged by 35%, making it one of the country's fastest-growing domestic travel sectors. British holiday parks and self-catering cottage operators have adapted their facilities to accommodate this demand. Modern accommodations frequently combine accessible ground-floor living suites for older adults with nearby outdoor recreation, such as walking trails and cycling routes, alongside high-speed internet connectivity for younger guests. This balance ensures that every generation feels catered to without compromise.

## How to Co-Design an Enjoyable Getaway

If you are interested in planning a gramping trip with your grandchild, the most effective approach is a cooperative partnership. Letting your grandchild take the lead on research while you establish reasonable physical boundaries creates an enjoyable dynamic for both of you.

Consider these practical steps when discussing your next getaway:

- **Propose a Co-Designed Road Trip:** Invite your grandchild to sit down with you and explore potential destinations. Ask them to research unique local diners, scenic overlooks, or historic landmarks using navigation apps. In turn, you can provide the vehicle, manage the fuel, and ensure that driving distances remain manageable.
- **Utilize the Federal Lifetime Senior Pass:** If you plan to explore nature, take advantage of the United States National Park Service Lifetime Senior Pass. Available through NPS.gov and Recreation.gov for $80, this lifetime pass provides the pass holder and all passengers in a single non-commercial vehicle free entry into more than 2,000 federal recreation sites and national parks across the nation.
- **Maintain a Balanced Daily Schedule:** While younger travelers often have boundless energy, the best trips leave plenty of unscheduled downtime. Agree in advance on a relaxed morning routine, set aside time for quiet afternoon reading or resting, and prioritize early dinners.

## Planning Checklist for Grandparents and Grandkids

- ☐ Sit down together to review vacation ideas and select a quiet destination, such as a state or national park.
- ☐ Check your eligibility for the federal $80 Lifetime Senior Pass on NPS.gov to access more than 2,000 public recreation lands.
- ☐ Let your grandchild use mobile apps to verify that lodgings feature step-free entries, accessible bathrooms, and ground-floor access.
- ☐ Build an itinerary with built-in rest periods rather than packing every hour with activities.
- ☐ Confirm driving routes and scenic rest stops ahead of time to keep travel days smooth and unhurried.
- ☐ Select casual dining locations together that offer comfortable seating and relaxed dining atmospheres.
