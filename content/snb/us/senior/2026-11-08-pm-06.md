## Why this matters to grandparents
Gen Z activism is often values-driven and community-oriented. That creates natural openings for older relatives to connect without taking over. For grandparents, the value is twofold:
- Relationship building: engaging with what grandchildren care about fosters trust and conversation.  
- Practical support: older adults can offer time, local knowledge, or help with simple logistics while respecting young people’s leadership.

## Ways grandparents can support — concrete, practical steps
Below are actions you can take that match the way Gen Z organizes in 2026.

- ☐ Ask open, nonjudgmental questions about your grandchild’s cause and offer to listen.  
- ☐ Attend a school event, club meeting, or community activity your grandchild is involved in (one attendance can communicate support).  
- ☐ Volunteer together on a local cause: help make posters, assist with a safe fundraiser, or offer a car ride to an event.  
- ☐ Set curious boundaries about online activity: ask how funds are raised and whether the online activity feels safe for them. Use an age-appropriate guide when needed.  
- ☐ Offer practical skills: writing a polite letter, making a phone call to a local organization, or helping with small logistics can be very helpful.

Numbered, step-by-step example:
1. Start a conversation: “Tell me about the project you care about.”  
2. Offer one concrete help item: attend one event, help with one task, or review a short message before it’s posted.  
3. Check for safety: ask whether they want adult involvement online and suggest resources if they ask.

## What to watch for and boundaries to keep
- Respect that many Gen Z organizers lead projects online and value peer leadership. Offer help without taking over.  
- Balance encouragement with questions about safety and privacy if online fundraising or campaigning is involved. If your grandchild asks for guidance, point them to trusted resources rather than imposing rules.
