Every holiday season, many grandparents face the same puzzling dilemma: what do you buy for a tech-savvy grandchild who seems glued to a smartphone? You might assume that younger generations only want the newest electronic gizmos or video games. However, recent consumer research shows a very different picture. **A striking 78% of Generation Z shoppers actively want nostalgic, screen-free gifts this holiday season.** 

While younger adults are turning to artificial intelligence to handle their holiday logistics, their genuine personal wish lists are surprisingly grounded in tradition, personal connection, and real-world experiences. Here is a closer look at what the numbers reveal, why younger family members are leaning into analog life, and how grandparents can create lasting holiday memories without breaking the bank or deciphering complex technology.

## By the Numbers: High-Tech Planning, Low-Tech Desires

The 2026 Mastercard Shopper Snapshot, conducted in partnership with The Harris Poll across 9,034 global adults, uncovers an interesting contrast in how Generation Z navigates the holidays. On one hand, younger Americans embrace cutting-edge digital tools to navigate holiday shopping stress. On the other hand, screen fatigue is driving an overwhelming preference for offline, tangible gifts and personal quality time.

Key findings from the survey data illustrate this balance:

- **AI as a trusted shopping assistant:** Four in ten (40%) Gen Z shoppers report trusting artificial intelligence tools to curate gift ideas and draft holiday cards, relying on technology to streamline holiday planning.
- **Strong hunger for screen-free connection:** More than three-quarters (78%) of Gen Z respondents express a desire for nostalgic, screen-free physical gifts rather than purely digital items.
- **Family meals top physical presents:** Sixty-three percent (63%) of Gen Z respondents state that an intentional, shared family holiday meal matters more to them than physical retail items.
- **The rise of experiential gifting:** According to data from the National Retail Federation (NRF), 27% of all holiday shoppers are specifically choosing experiential gifts over manufactured goods, a shift led largely by younger demographics.

## Why Younger Generations Are Looking Backward

For grandparents worried about keeping up with rapid technological changes, these trends offer welcome news. You do not need to study the latest video game consoles, smartphone specifications, or digital subscription services to connect with your grandchildren.

Younger Americans spend vast portions of their work, school, and social lives in front of screens. Retail researchers note that this constant connectivity has sparked a powerful counter-trend. Young people are actively seeking emotional anchors that ground them in family history, tangible textures, and authentic human interaction. 

Physical photo albums, handwritten recipe cards, vintage keepsakes, and uninterrupted conversations hold a unique emotional value in an increasingly digitized world. When younger people say they prefer a family dinner or an experiential gift, they are looking for an escape from screen fatigue and an opportunity to build genuine traditions.

## A Global Perspective: Japan's "Showa Retro" Wave

This longing for nostalgic, hands-on connection is not unique to the United States. In Japan, cultural observatories and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government report a major youth trend known as "Showa Retro," named after Japan's Showa era (1926–1989). 

Teenagers and young adults in bustling districts like Shibuya and Harajuku are actively seeking out vintage cassette players, instant film cameras, and traditional hand-dripped tea sets. Community centers across Tokyo have even launched popular "intergenerational cafes," where local seniors spend weekend afternoons teaching high school students traditional Japanese calligraphy and artisanal woodworking. Just like their peers in America, younger people in Japan find comfort and creativity in learning time-tested, analog skills from older generations.

## Practical Ways Grandparents Can Connect This Season

Instead of feeling pressured to hunt down expensive merchandise, consider using the holidays to share your time, wisdom, and family history. Here are simple, practical steps grandparents can take to bridge the generational divide:

- ☐ **Start a relaxed conversation about modern tools:** Ask your grandchild: *"I read that a lot of people your age are using AI to pick gifts this year—have you tried that, and what's the coolest thing it suggested?"* This opens a comfortable, non-judgmental dialogue about their tech habits without making them feel defensive.
- ☐ **Create an "Experience Coupon":** Instead of buying clothes or tech accessories, give a handwritten card offering an afternoon together. Offer to visit a local diner, attend a sporting event, or spend an afternoon exploring a museum of their choice.
- ☐ **Pass down an heirloom skill:** Invite your grandchild to join you in the kitchen to bake a signature family recipe, assemble a physical photo album, or hear stories about family ancestry.
- ☐ **Select tangible, screen-free gifts:** Consider physical books, board games for the entire family, puzzles, or creative craft supplies that invite shared participation around the kitchen table.
