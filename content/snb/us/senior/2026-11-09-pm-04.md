How can families afford the rising costs of helping older relatives remain safely in their own homes? Across Nebraska, **300,000 unpaid family caregivers provide an estimated $4.5 billion worth of care every year**, often paying directly out of pocket for essential equipment and daily assistance. To help offset these significant personal expenses, state leaders and advocates are highlighting a tax credit that provides direct financial relief to family members supporting aging loved ones.

During National Family Caregivers Month this November, AARP Nebraska and state revenue officials launched a major outreach campaign to inform eligible households about the Caregiver Tax Credit Act. By reimbursing a portion of qualifying out-of-pocket expenses—such as wheelchair ramps, assistive medical devices, and respite care aides—the program provides meaningful financial support designed to make aging in place sustainable.

## The Growing Economic Value of Family Caregiving

Family caregiving forms the backbone of long-term care across the United States. Most older adults prefer to remain living independently in their own homes and familiar neighborhoods for as long as possible. However, private caregiving costs, structural home modifications, and daily support can quickly deplete personal retirement savings and strained household budgets.

In Nebraska alone, family caregivers contribute 210 million hours of unpaid support each year. When quantified, this labor represents $4.5 billion in economic value annually. Without family members stepping in to handle daily care tasks, many seniors would be forced into costlier institutional settings. The state tax credit acknowledges this contribution by returning cash directly to families who invest their own financial resources into safe, home-based care.

## How the Credit Works: Caps, Percentages, and Eligibility

The Nebraska Caregiver Tax Credit functions as a nonrefundable state tax credit covering 50 percent of qualified out-of-pocket caregiving expenditures paid during the tax year. 

Under standard guidelines, eligible family caregivers can claim up to $2,000 annually. However, if the person receiving care is a military veteran or has a confirmed medical diagnosis of dementia, the maximum annual benefit increases to $3,000. 

To ensure the relief reaches low- and middle-income families who face the greatest strain, the program enforces specific income thresholds based on federal adjusted gross income (AGI):

- **Married Couples Filing Jointly:** Adjusted gross income must be under $100,000.
- **Single Filers:** Adjusted gross income must be under $50,000.

Because the credit is nonrefundable, it directly reduces the amount of state income tax owed dollar-for-dollar up to the taxpayer's total liability, helping caregivers keep more of their hard-earned earnings.

## Qualified Expenditures: What Home Care Expenses Count?

The program specifically targets expenses that help an older adult remain safely in a private residence rather than moving into a residential facility. Many family members pay for these modifications and services out of pocket without realizing that state relief exists.

Eligible expenses covered under the 50 percent reimbursement framework include:

1. **Home Accessibility Modifications:** Structural updates designed to eliminate fall hazards and barriers, such as installing wheelchair ramps, widening doorways, and retrofitting bathrooms with walk-in showers or grab bars.
2. **Assistive Medical Equipment:** Durable medical equipment and specialized devices that support daily mobility and safety inside the home.
3. **Respite Services and Adult Day Care:** Payments for professional adult day programs and temporary respite care aides to support primary family caregivers.rs necessary breaks while ensuring loved ones receive continuous supervision.

Because the credit covers half of eligible spending, claiming the full $2,000 standard credit requires $4,000 in qualifying receipts. Reaching the higher $3,000 cap for veterans or individuals with dementia requires $6,000 in documented expenditures.

## International Comparison: How the United Kingdom Supports Family Carers

Support systems for family eldercare vary significantly across developed nations. While Nebraska uses targeted state tax credits to offset documented home care expenses, other countries rely on direct statutory cash payments.

In the United Kingdom, family-based eldercare is supported through direct cash welfare administered by the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP). Under British rules in 2026, the statutory Carer's Allowance pays £81.90 per week to individuals who provide at least 35 hours of home care weekly to an older or disabled relative. 

Unlike a tax credit—which requires upfront family spending, detailed receipt tracking, and sufficient tax liability at year-end—the UK model provides an ongoing weekly stipend to supplement income. Both models reflect a shared recognition: supporting family caregivers directly at home is vital to keeping national eldercare systems functioning.

## Practical Steps to Claim the Nebraska Caregiver Tax Credit

Navigating tax relief requires organization and timely documentation. Families caring for an aging relative should establish an organized recordkeeping system throughout the year to ensure they can substantiate every dollar spent when filing state returns.

### Action Checklist for Family Caregivers

- ☐ Maintain a dedicated folder or digital ledger of receipts for home accessibility renovations, including ramp installation and grab bar additions.
- ☐ Save all payment records and invoices for durable medical equipment, adult day care centers, and qualified respite care aides.
- ☐ Verify that household adjusted gross income falls below the statutory ceiling ($100,000 for married couples filing jointly; $50,000 for single filers).
- ☐ Obtain formal medical or military documentation if your loved one is a veteran or has a diagnosed dementia condition to claim the elevated $3,000 cap.
- ☐ Visit the Nebraska Department of Revenue online at revenue.nebraska.gov to download the applicable tax schedule forms and instructions.
- ☐ Review eligibility criteria and resource materials through AARP Nebraska at aarp.org/necaregiving.
- ☐ Contact the Nebraska Department of Revenue taxpayer assistance helpline at 1-800-742-7474 if you have questions regarding specific qualifying expense categories.

Taking these straightforward administrative steps ensures that eligible households receive the full tax benefit they have earned while supporting their loved ones at home.
