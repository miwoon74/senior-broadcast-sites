The Social Security Administration announced on November 6, 2026 that beneficiaries will receive a **3.6% cost‑of‑living adjustment (COLA) for 2027**. The agency also notes that roughly **67 million** people received Social Security benefits in 2026. These two figures matter because the COLA adjusts monthly checks for tens of millions of retirees, survivors and disabled beneficiaries, while changes to Medicare premiums can reduce the net gain.

## At a glance: the key numbers



- COLA for 2027: 3.6% (SSA announcement, Nov. 6, 2026).  
- Beneficiaries: about 67 million people received Social Security benefits in 2026 (SSA, 2026).  
- SSA contact: 1‑800‑772‑1213 (official SSA number listed by the agency).

## What the 3.6% COLA means in plain terms

A COLA raises the dollar amount of monthly Social Security benefits to help keep pace with inflation measured by the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (as used by SSA). A 3.6% COLA means the SSA will increase the dollar benefit amounts by 3.6% effective in 2027 compared with the 2026 benefit level. For millions of recipients, the headline percent is useful for budgeting, but the actual change to take‑home dollars depends on other program adjustments that often happen in the same cycle — most importantly Medicare Part B and Part D premiums.

Important points to bear in mind:
- The COLA is a percentage increase in benefit amounts for 2027 (3.6%).  
- Approximately 67 million people were receiving benefits in 2026; many of those will see the 2027 COLA applied.  
- Medicare premiums, which are commonly deducted from Social Security checks, may change and reduce the net increase you receive.

## How Medicare premiums can affect your net increase

When SSA raises benefit amounts, Medicare Part B and Part D premiums are often adjusted in the same annual cycle. If Part B or Part D premiums increase, the actual increase deposited or mailed to a beneficiary could be smaller than 3.6% after those deductions. The SSA announcement and routine Medicare communications are the official sources to confirm how premiums will interact with the COLA.

## What you should do now (practical steps)

- Visit your SSA account at ssa.gov to view your personalized benefit information and the SSA’s bulletins about the COLA and effective dates.  
- Call SSA at 1‑800‑772‑1213 if you prefer phone help or cannot access your online account.  
- Watch for official Medicare communications (Part B and D) about premium changes that may be deducted from your Social Security check; factor these into your budget.  
- If you receive other means-tested benefits or pay for Medicare premiums out-of-pocket, consider reviewing your overall monthly budget soon to see how the COLA and any premium changes affect spending for essentials such as food, medicine and utilities.

## Checklist — before 2027 benefit payments change

- ☐ Log into your SSA account at ssa.gov and check your estimated benefit amount for 2027.  
- ☐ Confirm whether your Medicare Part B or D premiums will be deducted from your Social Security check in 2027.  
- ☐ Review automatic payments, prescriptions and essential monthly expenses to see whether the net increase meets your needs.  
- ☐ If you expect a shortfall, contact local aging services or benefits counselors for help with budgeting and benefit options.

## How policymakers and a large beneficiary population are affected

The COLA applies broadly: SSA reported about 67 million beneficiaries in 2026. For policy and program planning, that number is a reminder of the scale of monthly benefit adjustments and the fiscal and practical impact of even modest percentage changes. For individual beneficiaries, the main takeaway is that a headline COLA of 3.6% is helpful but not a guarantee of a full 3.6% increase in disposable monthly income once Medicare and other deductions are applied.
