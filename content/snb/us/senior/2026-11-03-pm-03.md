When you pick your vehicle up after a routine maintenance job, such as replacing a cracked windshield or getting a standard wheel alignment, do you assume your automated safety systems are working properly? Many drivers do, but automotive safety specialists and engineers meeting this week in Las Vegas issued an urgent warning: common maintenance work can throw delicate sensors out of alignment, causing collision avoidance technology to fail silently without triggering any warning lights on the dashboard. According to official data from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), **8,019 adults aged 65 and older were killed in traffic crashes in 2024**, representing a substantial share of national roadway fatalities.

Modern vehicles increasingly rely on Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS)—including automatic emergency braking, forward collision alerts, and blind-spot monitoring—to protect motorists. However, these systems provide protection only when their optical and radar components are aligned with pinpoint accuracy. For older motorists who depend on these electronic safeguards, understanding sensor calibration is now an essential part of routine vehicle ownership.

## The Data Behind Senior Roadway Risks

Federal traffic records highlight the vulnerability of older motorists and the critical role that functioning driver assistance tools play on modern roads. The NHTSA safety statistics show that older drivers represent a significant portion of fatal incidents, especially during everyday driving routines.

## The Silent Threat: How Everyday Repairs Misalign Sensors

Automotive safety specialists at the annual SEMA automotive engineering showcase in Las Vegas highlighted a growing problem across repair shops. When a car undergoes routine maintenance, the delicate radar and optical units mounted behind windshields, grilles, and bumpers can easily shift out of position.

The physical tolerances of these digital components are exceptionally narrow. Diagnostic engineers revealed that physical shifts as small as one millimeter, or an angular deviation of just fractions of a degree, can severely impair the system. A one-degree sensor deviation causes a forward-facing camera to misjudge distances entirely. When an optical unit is misaligned by even that single degree, the vehicle's computer may miss stopped vehicles ahead or misinterpret standard highway lane markings.

What makes this issue particularly dangerous is that it happens silently:
- **No Dashboard Warnings:** A mechanically misaligned camera or radar module can pass internal electronic health checks, leaving the driver unaware that the system is impaired.
- **Phantom Braking:** Misaligned sensors may mistakenly detect roadside signs or adjacent barriers as direct obstacles, causing the car to brake violently at highway speeds without warning.
- **Failure to Stop:** When traffic halts suddenly, a misaligned camera can miscalculate closing distance, failing to initiate automatic emergency braking in time to avoid an impact.

## Federal Standards and International Oversight

The reliance on driver assistance systems is set to expand rapidly across the United States. Federal safety rules have mandated that Automatic Emergency Braking (AEB) systems become standard equipment on nearly all light passenger vehicles by September 2029. As these systems transition from luxury options to standard safety equipment, proper calibration becomes an urgent mechanical necessity rather than an optional add-on.

Many older motorists rely on blind-spot detection and forward collision alarms to compensate for natural physical changes, such as slower reaction times or diminished peripheral vision. If these systems are compromised, senior drivers lose the critical safety buffer they depend on.

Other nations are already establishing mandatory regulatory standards to address this problem. In New Zealand, the national transport agency introduced mandatory Warrant of Fitness (WoF) inspection rules. Under this framework, registered vehicle inspectors are required to formally examine and test ADAS steering and emergency braking components during routine roadworthiness audits. New Zealand inspectors must confirm that no diagnostic trouble codes or fault indicators exist across the assistance systems before issuing a roadworthy certificate.

## How to Protect Yourself at the Repair Shop

In the United States, vehicle owners must be proactive when taking cars in for service. Anytime your vehicle undergoes bodywork, bumper repair, windshield replacement, or suspension and steering alignment, the physical orientation of your safety sensors may change.

Automotive technicians emphasize that vehicle owners should never leave a repair facility without verifying that both static and dynamic ADAS recalibrations were conducted. Static calibration uses precision optical targets inside the shop bay, while dynamic calibration requires driving the vehicle under specific road conditions with diagnostic computers plugged into the system.

Before accepting your car back from any service facility, take the following actions:
1. **Request Calibration Records:** Ask the service advisor for an official, itemized calibration certificate verifying that all radar sensors and forward cameras were recalibrated with diagnostic tools.
2. **Review Post-Scan Reports:** Demand a diagnostic post-scan printout showing that the forward collision warning (FCW) and blind-spot detection (BSD) cameras have cleared all diagnostic tests with zero remaining error codes.
3. **Check Recalls Regularly:** You can look up active safety recalls for your vehicle model by visiting NHTSA.gov/recalls — check the latest at NHTSA.gov/recalls. at 1-888-327-4236.
4. **Maintain Sensor Cleanliness:** Regularly clean your front windshield and areas around sensors (grille/bumper). Road grime, salt, and dirt can interfere with sensor optics and should be removed.rd-facing sensors can block optical inputs and degrade system response.

## Service Checklist for Senior Drivers

- ☐ Inquire before scheduling: "Does your shop perform certified dynamic and static ADAS recalibrations in-house?"
- ☐ Request a written ADAS calibration certificate following any windshield, bumper, or suspension repair
- ☐ Confirm that an electronic post-scan was completed with zero error codes on forward collision and blind-spot systems
- ☐ Inspect the windshield area directly in front of the rearview mirror camera to ensure it is clean and free of haze
- ☐ Check for open safety recalls on your vehicle make and model at NHTSA.gov/recalls or by calling 1-888-327-4236
