Is wrestling with a heavy roast turkey truly necessary for a successful Thanksgiving gathering? For generations, American families treated a giant golden bird as the non-negotiable centerpiece of the holiday dinner table. Yet recent consumer findings show that younger family members are quietly rewriting the holiday rulebook. According to nationwide survey research, **65% of Gen Z diners say they would happily skip the holiday turkey entirely in favor of an all-side-dish meal.** 

For older hosts and grandparents, this cultural shift brings unexpected relief. Rather than spending hours managing a demanding roast, seniors now have an open invitation from their grandchildren to focus on what matters most: comforting family casseroles, handwritten recipes, and relaxed time together around the dining table.

## By the Numbers: The Rise of the Holiday Side Dish

The preference for savory sides is not merely a passing fad; it represents a broad generational trend documented across the country. Campbell Soup Company surveyed over 5,000 holiday celebrators nationwide for its 2026 State of the Sides Report. The findings highlight a clear evolution in what guests look forward to when they sit down to eat.



While 65% of Gen Z diners favor an all-side-dish dinner, they are not alone. A solid 56% of Millennials share the same sentiment. Perhaps even more telling is the perspective of the cooks themselves: 80% of holiday hosts admitted they enjoy preparing savory side dishes far more than roasting a turkey. 

Furthermore, younger generations are not looking for sterile, store-bought novelties. The survey confirmed that 77% of Americans view recipes handed down from their grandparents as carrying deep personal meaning and nostalgic value. The appetite among youth is centered on authentic culinary heritage, creating a perfect bridge between older family cooks and younger diners.

## Lighter Kitchen Duties: Less Oven Stress and More Comfort

Roasting a very large turkey presents serious physical and logistical challenges. Lifting a scalding, heavy roasting pan out of a low oven can strain the lower back and wrists. Monitoring internal temperatures, basting, and carving can monopolize hours of kitchen time, isolating the host from family conversations in the living room.

When younger relatives prefer an all-side feast, menu planning instantly transforms. Instead of coordinating complex cooking times around an enormous bird, grandparents can design a flexible buffet of comforting classics:

- **Baked Macaroni and Cheese:** A crowd favorite that can be prepped early and reheated evenly.
- **Savory Stuffing:** A versatile heritage staple that stands on its own without needing to be stuffed inside poultry.
- **Roasted Sweet Potatoes:** A colorful, nutrient-rich dish that accommodates various dietary preferences.
- **Seasonal Casseroles:** Dishes that can be assembled the afternoon before, reducing holiday morning kitchen chaos.

Focusing on casseroles and vegetable dishes frees up oven rack space and dramatically reduces grocery costs. More importantly, it shifts the holiday atmosphere from high-pressure culinary performance to a collaborative, relaxed family gathering.

Young adults are looking for more than just comforting food when they visit older family members. National retail data indicates a parallel hunger for genuine, low-screen connection. Figures from the wholesale platform Faire demonstrate that young adults are actively seeking vintage home activities to accompany holiday meals.

Pairing a table full of grandmother-style sides with an after-dinner jigsaw puzzle or board game matches exactly what younger guests hope to experience when visiting family.

## Practical Steps: Passing Down the Apron

If you want to simplify your holiday cooking while strengthening bonds with your grandchildren, proactive planning can make the transition smooth and memorable. You do not need to abandon tradition entirely; simply invite the younger generation to share the responsibility.

1. **Appoint Side Dish Captains:** Contact your grandchildren before Thanksgiving Day. Assign each of them a favorite side dish to make alongside you or bring from their own kitchen.
2. **Share Handwritten Heritage Recipes:** Bring out your original recipe cards. Showing grandchildren your notes, adjustments, and kitchen secrets gives them a tangible link to family history.
3. **Blend the Old with the New:** Encourage grandchildren to introduce a contemporary side dish twist alongside your heritage casseroles, demonstrating mutual respect for each other's culinary tastes.
4. **Prepare an Analog Entertainment Corner:** Clear a small side table for a puzzle, craft project, or classic board game where family members of all ages can gather naturally after dinner.

## Family Holiday Planning Checklist

- ☐ Call grandchildren ahead of the holiday to discuss their favorite side dishes
- ☐ Choose two or three heritage casserole recipes that can be prepared in advance
- ☐ Designate younger family members as official Side Dish Captains
- ☐ Locate handwritten family recipe cards to display or copy for younger cooks
- ☐ Set out a jigsaw puzzle or vintage board game in the living room for screen-free connection
- ☐ Review intergenerational cooking resources online through community support organizations

If you are looking for additional inspiration, national organizations offer helpful toolkits for bridging the generation gap. You can explore intergenerational activity ideas and recipe exchange guides through Generations United at gu.org, or access family lifestyle guides via AARP's family portal at aarp.org/home-family. Local university extension programs and 4-H youth offices through the USDA National Institute of Food and Agriculture (nifa.usda.gov) also provide regional culinary heritage materials.

A warm and memorable holiday does not depend on a picture-perfect roasted bird. By embracing the simple comfort of traditional side dishes, older adults can eliminate holiday exhaustion and build lasting kitchen traditions that your grandchildren will treasure for decades to come.
