## By the Numbers: A 57% Surge in Elder Prize Fraud

Official law enforcement data reveals a sharp escalation in predatory prize schemes targeting older adults. Reports tracked by the Federal Bureau of Investigation's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) document significant financial losses concentrated among retirees.



In 2025 alone, recorded losses to lottery, sweepstakes, and inheritance schemes reached $138 million, representing a 57 percent annual surge compared to previous levels. The FBI's IC3 formally documented 3,837 older victims across the nation during the year.

## Anatomy of the Scam: The Upfront "Tax" Trap

Criminal rings frequently mine public records and solicitation lists, zeroing in on older adults who donate to civic charities or participate in legitimate commercial sweepstakes. 

The swindle usually follows an identical pattern:

1. **The Grand Announcement:** Imposters reach out through automated robocalls, high-grade personalized mailings, or urgent text alerts. They declare that you have been chosen as the winner of a massive cash prize, a lottery drawing, or an imported luxury vehicle.
2. **The Fake Processing Hurdle:** Once you express interest, the narrative immediately shifts. An "agent" informs you that your payout is ready, but federal taxes, customs clearance charges, or administrative processing fees must be paid before funds can be released.
3. **Untraceable Payment Demands:** Con artists insist that you must urgently settle these obligations using cashier's checks, bank wire transfers, retail gift cards, or cryptocurrency. 
4. **Disappearing Funds:** As soon as funds leave your checking account, recovering the stolen money is nearly impossible. If you comply, the scammers rarely stop—they return with claims of additional unexpected customs duties or state tax levies until your savings are exhausted.

## What It Means for You: Legitimate Prizes Cost Zero

The distinction between legal prize distributions and illegal fraud rests on a clear legal boundary: **In the United States, legitimate sweepstakes never ask winners to pay money, buy retail gift cards, or surrender banking details to receive a payout.**

Under United States federal law, requiring any advance fee to claim sweepstakes winnings is strictly illegal. If an entity tells you that you won a $10,000 lottery drawing but instructs you to send $500 in advance processing fees, you are dealing with a criminal. Any demand for an upfront fee—no matter how official the caller sounds or how authentic the paperwork looks—means the prize does not exist.

## Action Steps: How to Guard Your Financial Accounts

Retirees can prevent catastrophic financial losses by establishing firm communication boundaries and setting up security guardrails with financial institutions.

- ☐ **Hang up immediately:** If an aggressive caller presses you for an administrative fee, simply state: *"I never pay to collect a prize,"* and terminate the call.
- ☐ **Refuse unverified payment methods:** Never wire funds, purchase gift cards, or disclose bank account numbers to any unverified solicitor.
- ☐ **Set bank verification limits:** Instruct your local branch manager to place a two-party verification notice on large outgoing wire transfers or withdrawals from your savings accounts.
- ☐ Block unsolicited solicitations: Consider registering landline and mobile numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry at donotcall.gov.lling **1-888-382-1222**.
- ☐ **File an official federal complaint:** Report deceptive communications directly to the Federal Trade Commission at **reportfraud.ftc.gov**.
- ☐ **Access free advocate counseling:** If you or a family member shared personal financial details, contact the AARP Fraud Watch Network helpline at **1-877-908-3360** for specialized guidance.
