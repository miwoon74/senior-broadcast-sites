Are you wondering what to buy or plan for your teenage or young adult grandchildren this holiday season? While high-tech devices and electronic entertainment often dominate holiday advertising, consumer market data reveals a surprising shift in youthful priorities. **According to research from PwC, 78 percent of Gen Z shoppers are deliberately prioritizing screen-free gifts over electronics and video games this holiday season.** 

Instead of searching for virtual experiences, America's youngest adults are increasingly setting aside their smartphones to embrace classic, hands-on activities. From traditional needlework and baking to vintage parlor games, the pastimes that older adults have practiced for decades are suddenly at the center of youth culture. Understanding this cultural pivot can help older generations connect deeply with visiting family without spending money on expensive gadgets.

## The Numbers Behind the Analog Holiday Surge

Retail figures for the 2026 holiday shopping season demonstrate that the shift toward tactile, screen-free pastimes is substantial. A comprehensive holiday retail analysis conducted by the merchant platform Faire revealed that Gen Z and Millennials accounted for half of all seasonal retail demand. Rather than spending their budgets entirely on modern digital products, these younger demographics drove dramatic spikes across multiple heritage and home hobby categories.

Orders for classic craft and sewing hobby kits surged 58 percent compared to 2025, while purchases of classic board games jumped 55 percent and jigsaw puzzles increased by 40 percent. At the same time, retro decorative styles saw an extraordinary revival: retail orders for tartan and plaid home textiles climbed 93 percent, and crochet decor rose 70 percent.

## Understanding Digital Fatigue and the 'Grannycore' Movement

Why are young adults turning toward pastimes commonly associated with their grandparents? Market researchers and psychologists attribute this behavioral shift to acute digital exhaustion. Having grown up in an era dominated by automated notifications, video streams, and algorithm-driven feeds, many teens and twentysomethings report feeling overwhelmed by relentless screen time. 

In response, young people are seeking genuine, physical experiences that ground them in the real world. In youth culture, this phenomenon is widely referred to as the "grannycore" aesthetic—a lifestyle trend that celebrates cozy, vintage, and domestic arts. The appeal lies in tangible creation. When young people bake bread from scratch, stitch an ornament, or play a physical card game, they experience a sense of calm, accomplishment, and genuine presence that digital feeds cannot provide.

For older generations, this shift represents a welcoming change. For years, parents and grandparents have worried that a digital divide was pulling families apart. Today, the very activities older adults know best—sewing, woodcraft, baking heritage recipes, and tabletop games—are precisely what younger generations are eager to explore.

## Bridging Generations at the Holiday Table

Because younger adults are actively seeking offline experiences, you do not need to decipher viral internet slang or invest in complex electronics to host an engaging holiday gathering. Your lifelong hobbies, domestic skills, and personal memories are valuable assets that your grandchildren are often genuinely excited to learn about.

Consider the kitchen table. Preparing holiday meals has long been an intergenerational activity, but framing it as an educational, screen-free experience gives it fresh meaning. Guiding a grandchild through the steps of mixing bread dough or decorating traditional holiday cookies provides hands-on mentorship. It naturally opens the door for storytelling, allowing you to share the history of family recipes and memories from your own youth.

Similarly, living room entertainment can shift away from television screens and mobile devices—for example, by dusting off a board game or setting up a card tournament.starting a 1,000-piece jigsaw puzzle creates an inviting space where family members can sit together for hours. Because these activities require focus without demanding continuous, high-stress conversation, they offer an easy, low-pressure way for different age groups to bond.

## Practical Ways to Share Traditions This Season

Engaging your grandchildren with analog traditions does not require elaborate planning. Simple, purposeful invitations are often the most effective. Here are several practical ideas you can put into practice during family visits:

1. **Host an Unplugged Holiday Bake-Off**: Invite your teenage or college-age grandchild into the kitchen to learn a traditional family recipe. Focus on tactile steps, such as rolling dough, crimping pie crusts, or measuring spices, and share the family history behind the dish.
2. **Set Up a Permanent Game or Puzzle Table**: Dedicate a side table in the living room to a jigsaw puzzle or classic games like Scrabble, Mahjong, or dominoes. Leaving the game accessible throughout the visit invites casual, spontaneous participation whenever someone walks by.
3. **Plan an Outing to a Vintage or Antique Shop**: Take a trip together to browse local thrift stores or antique markets. Exploring vintage holiday ornaments, historic home goods, or retro vinyl records can spark conversations about design, history, and how everyday items have evolved.
4. **Explore Community Intergenerational Programs**: If your family lives far away, you can still participate in the analog revival. Call 2-1-1 to ask about local intergenerational mentoring circles, craft workshops, or volunteer programs connecting seniors with youth in your community.

## Checklist for an Unplugged Holiday Gathering

- ☐ Select one or two classic board games, card decks, or puzzles from the closet and set them out where guests can easily see them.
- ☐ Gather the ingredients and handwritten recipe cards for a traditional holiday bread, pie, or cookie you would like to teach someone to make.
- ☐ Prepare a basket or dedicated spot where family members can voluntarily place their mobile phones during meals or game sessions.
- ☐ Locate old photo albums, vintage holiday ornaments, or classic vinyl records that have meaningful family stories attached to them.
- ☐ Check with local senior centers or community organizations via 2-1-1 to learn about neighborhood intergenerational programs and craft circles.
