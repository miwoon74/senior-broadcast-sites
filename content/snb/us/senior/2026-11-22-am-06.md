If you are wondering how to connect with teenage or college-age grandchildren during holiday gatherings, the answer may already be waiting inside your living room closet. While many assume younger generations are entirely absorbed by smartphones and video games, recent research points in the opposite direction: **74% of young adults aged 16 to 25 prioritize in-person, real-world entertainment over digital alternatives.**

Instead of spending hours streaming movies or scrolling through feeds in separate rooms, young adults across the country are returning to the physical dining table. For older adults, this cultural pivot represents an ideal opportunity to share meaningful, low-stress time with family members across generations without needing to navigate internet culture or digital devices.

## By the Numbers: The Analog Resurgence Among Young Adults

Industry data published in late 2026 highlights an unmistakable surge in analog gaming. Retailers and manufacturers are seeing unprecedented demand driven largely by younger shoppers who want physical, tactile hobbies.

A comprehensive international study conducted by Asmodee and research firm Kantar confirmed that this growth is powered directly by Gen Z consumers looking for real-life community.

Major tabletop conventions like Gen Con alongside neighborhood board game cafés documented record-setting attendance in the autumn of 2026. Rather than treating board games as an outdated pastime, college campuses and young adult social groups have embraced them as staple weekend activities.

## Why Younger Generations Are Stepping Away From Screens

The sudden migration toward analog games is not accidental. Young adults are navigating unprecedented levels of digital exhaustion. Between screen-based college coursework, remote job responsibilities, and constant mobile notifications, younger Americans report widespread digital fatigue. 

Tabletop games solve several social challenges that screen-based interactions cannot address:

1. **A Natural Antidote to Digital Burnout** — Rolling physical dice, shuffling card decks, and moving wooden pieces across a cardboard map provides a sensory break from flat glass screens.
2. **Structured Conversation Without Awkward Small Talk** — Family gatherings can sometimes feel tense when relatives run out of casual conversation topics or struggle to find common ground. A board game provides clear rules, shared goals, and natural talking points, making socialization effortless.
3. **Equal Footing Across Generations** — In digital video games, younger players often hold an insurmountable advantage due to fast reflexes and technical familiarity. Tabletop strategy games, classic card games, and tile-placement challenges level the playing field, allowing grandparents and grandchildren to compete or cooperate as equals.

Modern titles like Catan, Ticket to Ride, and Carcassonne have joined traditional card decks as standard social currency for young adults. These games emphasize trade, route-building, and light strategy rather than lightning-fast reaction times, making them accessible to players of all ages.

## Bridging the Living Room Divide This Holiday Season

For older adults preparing for family visits, welcoming grandchildren into an analog gaming session requires very little preparation. You do not need to purchase expensive equipment or master complicated rules ahead of time.

Many of the games popular on college campuses share fundamental mechanics with traditional favorites. If your family enjoys tile games like Rummikub or Scrabble, or tile-laying systems like Mexican Train Dominoes, you already have the building blocks for an engaging afternoon. 

Inviting a grandchild to teach you their favorite game can be just as rewarding as introducing them to yours. Role reversals—where the younger generation explains game setup and strategic choices—create an empowering environment that fosters mutual respect. Whether gathering after Thanksgiving dinner or setting aside a quiet Sunday afternoon, setting up a dedicated game table encourages visitors to leave their phones in another room voluntarily.

## Actionable Steps for Setting Up Family Game Sessions

Connecting over a tabletop game is straightforward when you create an inviting atmosphere. Use this practical checklist to prepare before your next family gathering:

- ☐ Check your storage closet for well-preserved family classics such as Scrabble, Rummikub, or Mexican Train Dominoes.
- ☐ Verify that all essential game components, rulebooks, and scorepads are intact before guests arrive.
- ☐ Ask your teenage or college-age grandchildren ahead of time which tabletop or card games they enjoy playing with friends.
- ☐ Invite them to bring one of their preferred games—such as Catan, Carcassonne, or Ticket to Ride—and ask them to teach the rules.
- ☐ Set aside a clean, well-lit dining table or coffee table with comfortable seating away from television noise.
- ☐ Locate an independent neighborhood game store using the retailer locator provided by the American Specialty Toy Retailing Association at astratoy.org.
- ☐ Check with local senior community centers or public libraries through the Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov (or by calling 1-800-677-1116) to explore community game clubs and intergenerational game nights.

Gathering around a board game provides a structured, joyful way to slow down, build memories, and bridge the years between generations without a screen in sight.
