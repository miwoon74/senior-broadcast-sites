Are you looking for an accessible, festive evening stroll to celebrate the start of the winter holiday season? As winter daylight shrinks, staying socially active and spending comfortable time outdoors remains essential for health and well-being. Today, November 20, 2026, the Missouri Botanical Garden in St. Louis opens its public season for the annual Garden Glow, welcoming visitors with **over two million** dazzling LED lights set across its historical 79-acre grounds.

Running on select evenings through January 2, 2027, the seasonal tradition combines festive holiday lighting with accessible, paved walking trails, heated indoor stops, and plan-ahead ticket options that help retirees and families visit comfortably without breaking the budget.

## Highlights Across the 79-Acre Grounds

The event transforms the botanical institution's 79-acre landscape into an illuminated winter walk. Thousands of visitors wander past radiant botanical sculptures, the iconic Climatron conservatory, the Frosted Forest, and the historical Tower Grove House.

Along the pathway, guests can purchase festive snacks, stop by outdoor fire pits to make s'mores, and step inside heated indoor pavilions when they want a warm break from the night air. Weeknight outings typically feature lighter crowds and simpler parking, offering an unhurried, gentle pace that is ideal for older adults walking with friends or family.

## Accessibility and Comfort Features for Older Adults

Walking long distances in the dark can be intimidating, but Garden Glow is structured to support visitors managing joint pain or limited mobility:

- **Flat, Paved Walkways:** The main route follows ADA-accessible paved surfaces, avoiding uneven dirt paths or steep terrain.
- **Motorized Cart and Wheelchair Rentals:** The garden provides motorized scooter rentals and manual wheelchairs, making the vast grounds manageable. It is recommended to contact visitor services in advance to arrange equipment.
- **Sensory-Friendly Nights:** For guests who experience sensory overload or prefer a quieter environment, the garden has scheduled a dedicated sensory-friendly evening on November 30 with softer background music and subdued lighting effects.
- **Heated Rest Areas:** Climate-controlled indoor spaces and fire pit locations give visitors frequent opportunities to sit down, rest, and stay warm along the route.

## How to Book Official Tickets and Avoid Reseller Scams

Third-party ticket reselling scams frequently target popular holiday events, charging inflated prices or delivering fake entry barcodes. All visitors should purchase timed passes directly from the official Missouri Botanical Garden web portal at mobot.org. 

Booking in advance not only avoids potential late-day walk-up price increases, but it also ensures you lock in an entry time that fits your dinner and travel schedule.

## Practical Checklist for Your Evening Visit

- ☐ Reserve timed-entry tickets online in advance at mobot.org to secure the best rate.
- ☐ Call visitor services at 314-577-5100 if you need to reserve a motorized scooter or accessibility assistance.
- ☐ Choose a weeknight or the sensory-friendly evening (November 30) for smaller crowds and easier parking.
- ☐ Dress warmly in breathable layers, wearing comfortable, flat walking shoes with good traction.
- ☐ Review public and community transportation options through the Eldercare Locator (eldercare.acl.gov or 1-800-677-1116) if you prefer not to drive at night.
- ☐ Explore national outdoor recreation opportunities by looking into the lifetime America the Beautiful Senior Pass for federal parks at store.usgs.gov or nps.gov (1-888-275-8747).
