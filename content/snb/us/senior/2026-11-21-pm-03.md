Could your bones be thinning without you feeling any difference at all? For millions of older adults, the answer is yes. According to federal public health data, many adults aged 50 and older live with osteoporosis, a condition that quietly erodes skeletal strength until a minor slip leads to a serious fracture.

Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, commonly known as a DEXA or DXA scan, serves as the clinical gold standard for measuring bone thickness at the hip and lumbar spine. Despite its safety and simplicity, many seniors skip this vital preventive test simply because bone loss causes no symptoms in its early stages. Understanding your screening options, risk factors, and Medicare benefits can help you safeguard your mobility and independence for years to come.

## By the Numbers: The Hidden Toll of Bone Loss

Osteoporosis is often called a silent condition because bones lose mineral content gradually, causing no everyday ache or visible symptom. Many retirees only discover their skeleton has weakened after tripping over an unexpected doorstep or a loose throw rug.

Surveillance figures from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the National Center for Health Statistics reveal the broad scope of bone loss among American seniors:

- **12.6 percent of all adults aged 50 and older** have osteoporosis at the femur neck or lumbar spine.
- Among adults aged 65 and older, that figure rises to **17.7 percent**.
- **19.6 percent of women aged 50 and older** live with osteoporosis, compared to **4.4 percent of men** in the same age group.
- An additional **43.1 percent of adults aged 50 and older** show low bone mass, a precursor condition known as osteopenia. That includes **51.5 percent of women** and **33.5 percent of men**.
- Each year, many older adults are hospitalized nationwide due to severe hip fractures, with most of these injuries triggered by falls.

The data shows that more than half of women over 50 have already begun losing critical bone density. While women face significantly higher rates due to hormonal changes, men are far from immune: more than one-third of men over 50 exhibit osteopenia, placing them at heightened risk of fractures as they grow older.

A broken hip often leads to an abrupt loss of independence, frequently requiring lengthy rehabilitation or admission to a skilled nursing facility. Identifying weak bones early allows you and your physician to take protective measures before a fall results in hospital care.

## What to Expect During a DEXA Scan

Many people put off bone density testing because they worry about pain, injections, or radiation. In practice, a DEXA scan is one of the quickest and easiest noninvasive tests in routine healthcare.

- **Fast procedure:** The scan is usually brief and noninvasive. You remain fully clothed, provided your garments do not have heavy metal zippers or buttons.
- **Very low radiation:** A DEXA scan involves less radiation exposure than a standard cross-country commercial airplane flight.
- **No needles:** The test requires no contrast dye, injections, or blood draws.
- **Standardized results:** The scan generates a numerical T-score, which compares your measured bone density directly to that of an average healthy young adult. A low score alerts your doctor whether you fall into the osteopenia or osteoporosis range.

## Medicare Coverage: When Is a Bone Density Scan Free?

Original Medicare Part B may cover screening DEXA scans for qualified beneficiaries under specific conditions; coverage details and timing can vary, so check the latest at Medicare.gov.ovider accepts Medicare assignment.

Medicare covers bone mass measurement if you meet at least one of the following criteria:

1. **Women aged 65 and older** whose healthcare provider orders a screening test.
2. **Estrogen-deficient women** who are clinically determined to be at risk for osteoporosis based on medical history.
3. **Individuals with vertebral abnormalities** shown on an X-ray that suggest osteoporosis or fracture.
4. **Individuals taking steroid medications** (such as prednisone) or planning to begin long-term glucocorticoid therapy.
5. **Individuals diagnosed with primary hyperparathyroidism**.
6. **Individuals being monitored** to assess the response to an FDA-approved osteoporosis drug therapy.

Eligible men who demonstrate clinical risk factors can also receive Medicare Part B coverage with an order from their treating physician.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your Bones and Prevent Falls

Early screening gives you the information you need, but daily habits and home safety keep you on your feet. Public health specialists recommend combining medical guidance with environmental precautions to reduce your overall fracture risk.

- ☐ Call your primary care physician to request a Medicare-covered bone density order if appropriate; coverage timing can vary, so check the latest at Medicare.gov.
- ☐ Clear all loose throw rugs and floor runners from hallways, bathrooms, and main living areas.
- ☐ Incorporate regular weight-bearing physical activities, such as daily walking, to help preserve bone mass and joint function.
- ☐ Review your daily calcium intake recommendations and bone nutrition guidelines with your doctor or through the Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation.
- ☐ Explore local fall prevention resources, such as community-based Tai Chi for Arthritis or balance classes offered through your Area Agency on Aging.
- ☐ Download and review the CDC STEADI (Stopping Elderly Accidents, Deaths, & Injuries) fall prevention toolkit to spot trip hazards around your home.
