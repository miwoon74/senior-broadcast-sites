The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention published updated adult immunization data on Nov. 25, 2026, showing that pneumococcal vaccine coverage for adults aged **65+** remains below national targets in 2024–2026. The CDC also reports wide variation by state and by race and ethnicity, and urges clinicians, pharmacists and older adults to check vaccine records and follow the CDC adult immunization schedule.

## At a glance: What the CDC is reporting (numbers you should note)

- The CDC briefing covers adult vaccination data spanning **2024–2026** and was released on **Nov. 25, 2026**.
- Key age groups mentioned by the CDC include adults aged **50–64** and adults aged **65+**.
- The agency says pneumococcal coverage for adults **65+** is “below national target” and shows significant state-level variation for **2024–2026**.

## Why this matters for older adults

Pneumococcal disease and pneumonia cause higher hospitalization and death rates among older adults, which is why the CDC emphasizes improving pneumococcal vaccine coverage in the **65+** age group in its 2024–2026 reporting. Staying up to date with recommended pneumococcal and seasonal vaccines lowers the risk of severe illness and helps protect family members and caregivers.

## What the CDC specifically says and what it does not give in this briefing

- The CDC statement released on **Nov. 25, 2026** indicates that pneumococcal vaccine coverage for adults **65+** remains below the agency's national targets for **2024–2026**, and that coverage varies substantially by state and by race/ethnicity.
- The briefing notes improvements in some groups linked to new vaccines and outreach, but overall gaps persist across 2024–2026.
- The CDC refers readers to its adult immunization schedule (2026) for detailed recommendations about which pneumococcal vaccine is appropriate depending on prior vaccine history.
- The bulletin does not provide precise nationwide percentage values or a single national coverage percentage in the text cited here; for exact numeric percentages and state-by-state tables, consult the CDC data release and QuickStats page directly.

## Practical steps for seniors and caregivers

- ☐ Ask your primary care clinician or pharmacist to review your adult vaccine record against the CDC adult immunization schedule (2026).
- ☐ If you are aged **65+**, ask whether you are current for the pneumococcal vaccine recommended for your previous vaccine history.
- ☐ Bring your Medicare card when you get vaccinated; many recommended adult vaccines are covered by Medicare in specific situations (see Medicare.gov).
- ☐ If transportation is an issue, contact your state or local health department, your Area Agency on Aging, or use the Eldercare Locator for local help (eldercare.acl.gov).

## Medicare and paying for vaccines

The CDC briefing and related guidance note that Medicare covers many adult vaccines. The CDC points readers to Medicare.gov for coverage details and to pharmacies and clinicians that can bill Medicare where applicable. If you need help understanding coverage, your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) counselor can assist.

## Living with the data: how to use CDC materials

- Use the CDC adult immunization schedule (2026) to identify which pneumococcal shot is recommended for adults **65+** based on prior vaccination.
- If you or a family member are not sure about prior pneumococcal vaccines, request an immunization-history check from your clinician or pharmacist; documentation can change which shot is recommended.
- Check state-level data if you want local context: the CDC materials for **2024–2026** report significant differences across states.
