AARP issued a public scam alert on **Nov. 7, 2026**, warning that fraudsters are increasingly targeting veterans and their families with impostor benefit notices, fake charity solicitations, and urgent-payment demands. If you or a loved one receives an unsolicited contact about benefits, the AARP alert recommends verifying by calling the Department of Veterans Affairs at **1‑800‑827‑1000** and reporting fraud to the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov (or by the FTC’s help line referenced as 1‑877‑FTC‑HELP).

## What the alert says: common scam methods

According to the AARP alert summarized in recent reporting, scammers targeting veterans typically use a few consistent tactics:
- Impersonation: callers or messages claiming to be VA officials or benefit administrators.
- Fake charities: solicitations tied to military holidays or veterans’ causes to gain trust.
- Urgent payment demands: requests to pay immediately, often by gift card, wire transfer, or other non-reversible methods.
- Pressure and deception: callers pushing for quick action or personal/financial details.

The AARP notice emphasizes that these approaches are designed to exploit trust around military service and benefit contacts.

## Why veterans and older adults are at special risk

The alert explains that veterans and older adults may expect official outreach about benefits and may be more likely to trust callers who reference service history or benefits. That combination makes impostor approaches effective. Financial losses from scams can have outsized impacts on retirement savings and access to care.

## How to verify and where to report

Practical verification and reporting steps the alert and reporting recommend:
1. Hang up on unsolicited calls about benefits. Do not provide personal or financial details.
2. Verify independently by calling official numbers — for VA-related contacts, call 1‑800‑827‑1000 (VA main number listed in the alert).
3. Report scams to the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov. The alert also references the FTC assistance line shown as 1‑877‑FTC‑HELP.
4. Notify family members, caregivers, and local law enforcement if you suspect a scam.

If you get a suspicious charity solicitation tied to veterans’ causes, check the charity’s registration and ask for written information before donating.

## Checklist: steps to protect a veteran you care about

- ☐ If a caller mentions benefits, hang up and call the VA at 1‑800‑827‑1000 to verify.
- ☐ Never pay by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency for a supposed benefit or charity request.
- ☐ Use reportfraud.ftc.gov to report the attempt and follow local police guidance if you experienced a loss.
- ☐ Keep a list of trusted contacts (family, VA regional contact, local veterans service organizations).
- ☐ Consider placing a fraud alert on credit reports and reviewing account statements regularly.

## International note

Reporting about similar targeting of veterans appears in other countries’ consumer-protection efforts (for example, UK agencies run seasonal campaigns around commemorative dates). The AARP alert highlights this as a shared international pattern of scams tied to veterans and pension themes.
