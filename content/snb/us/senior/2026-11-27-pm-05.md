How can older adults keep up with rapidly changing devices and applications without paying expensive fees or feeling overwhelmed by computer jargon? The answer increasingly lies in barrier-free, community-based education. Across the United States, **over 181,000 participants** have completed technology workshops at zero personal cost through Senior Planet, a charitable initiative run by Older Adults Technology Services (OATS), an affiliate of AARP. Marking ten years of expanding local learning opportunities, the organization has officially broadened its community footprint to more than 400 partner centers, libraries, and neighborhood hubs nationwide. 

Whether learning to manage a patient portal, connect with distant grandchildren over video, or set up a smart doorbell, older adults are discovering that practical digital literacy is one of the most effective tools for living independently.

## Measuring a Decade of Community Reach

Navigating modern technology can often feel intimidating, especially when user manuals are replaced by touchscreens and software updates change familiar layouts overnight. To address this challenge, Older Adults Technology Services created a comprehensive curriculum tailored specifically to how mature adults learn. 

Over the past ten years, the initiative has transitioned from regional hubs into a widespread national network. The program now coordinates with public libraries, senior centers, and community organizations to offer courses in both physical classrooms and interactive online environments.

## Why Digital Literacy Is Essential for Aging in Place

Digital skills are no longer limited to leisure or entertainment. For older adults, technological independence is closely tied to daily safety, health management, and emotional well-being. Demographic tracking has identified persistent digital literacy gaps, particularly among adults aged 75 and older, which can significantly heighten the risk of social isolation.

When older adults acquire modern digital skills, everyday living becomes safer and more manageable:

- **Telehealth and Medical Care:** Routine medical care increasingly relies on electronic patient portals for scheduling appointments, viewing lab results, and refilling prescription medications. Telemedicine visits allow seniors to consult specialists from home, avoiding demanding travel.
- **Home Safety and Automation:** Setting up smart speakers, pairing medical monitoring devices, and automating video doorbell alerts allow retirees to supervise home security and communicate immediately during emergencies.
- **Essential Home Deliveries:** Ordering household groceries and everyday necessities online protects older adults during periods of illness, severe winter weather, or limited mobility.
- **Family and Community Ties:** Mastering video calling platforms like Zoom enables regular, face-to-face contact with family members and community peers, directly reducing the physical and psychological toll of isolation.

## Inside the Classroom: Practical, Jargon-Free Instruction

A core strength of the Senior Planet learning model is its classroom culture. Training sessions are led by patient instructors and volunteers who teach in plain English, avoiding confusing technical terms that often alienate beginner students. Participants are encouraged to ask basic questions without embarrassment, whether they need help finding an app on a tablet screen or configuring device volume.

Workshops cover a wide spectrum of everyday tasks:

1. **Foundational Device Handling:** Beginners start with iPad basics, tablet navigation, and touchscreen gestures, helping individuals build muscle memory and confidence.
2. **Everyday Online Banking and Cybersecurity:** Instructors guide participants through creating strong passwords, identifying common internet scams, and recognizing online fraud, ensuring seniors can manage financial accounts securely.
3. **Smart Home Integration:** Learners discover how to configure smart home devices and pair health monitors with their smartphones.

To ensure broad access, instruction is provided through multiple flexible formats. Older adults can participate in daily live interactive workshops over Zoom, receive telephone-based instruction, or visit bilingual in-person learning labs operating in major metropolitan areas, including New York, Denver, and Miami, alongside regional operations in Maryland.

## How to Connect with Free Training and Tech Support

Accessing these learning opportunities requires no prior technical experience. Older adults and family caregivers can engage through several direct community pathways:

- **Interactive Online Classes:** Anyone with an internet connection can explore upcoming courses and join live virtual sessions by visiting seniorplanet.org or seniorplanet.org/classes.
- **Toll-Free Device Troubleshooting:** For assistance with specific hardware issues, password problems, or application setup, seniors can call the Senior Planet National Tech Hotline directly at 1-888-713-3495 for free, individual guidance.
- **Local Aging Networks:** Retirees seeking neighborhood-based workshops can reach the federal Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov or call 1-800-677-1116 to identify nearby Area Agency on Aging offices and participating community centers.
- **Self-Paced Learning:** Older adults who prefer learning at their own speed can access tutorials covering digital banking, telehealth portals, and basic computer skills through digitallearn.org, maintained by the Public Library Association.

- ☐ Check with your local library or senior center to see if they are among the 400 partner sites offering in-person assistance
