Are you searching for a relaxing, accessible holiday outing that combines spectacular horticultural design with festive cheer? Longwood Gardens in Kennett Square, Pennsylvania, opens its renowned winter showcase, "A Longwood Christmas," on Friday, November 20, 2026, illuminating the grounds with **more than 500,000 sparkling lights**. Featuring celestial installations inspired by deep-space imagery from the James Webb space telescope alongside heated conservatories and paved trails, the exhibition provides an ideal setting for older adults and multi-generational families to celebrate the season comfortably.

Navigating popular seasonal exhibits can sometimes present hurdles for older visitors, especially regarding walking distances, cold weather, and crowds. With thoughtful planning, understanding the admission guidelines, and taking advantage of on-site mobility accommodations, you can enjoy a calm and memorable stroll through one of the country's premier botanical displays.

## The 2026 Exhibition by the Numbers

Spanning 1,000 acres, Longwood Gardens transforms its sprawling landscape into a cosmic wonderland running from November 20, 2026, through January 10, 2027. Curators have organized this year's displays around a cosmic theme, pairing traditional plant collections with celestial elements.

Inside the heated Conservatory, guests can view a centerpiece 22-foot rotating Fraser fir tree embellished with metallic ornaments set along the Center Walk. Complementing the grand evergreen are hanging baskets filled with Guzmania bromeliads and illuminated botanical arrangements. Outside, over 500,000 lights adorn trees, walkways, and open-air spaces, complemented by illuminated fountain performances.

To preserve visitor comfort and prevent overcrowding along the paths, Longwood uses a strict timed-entry ticketing system. General adult admission ranges from $45 to $52 for visitors aged 16 to 64, while children under 4 receive free admission. Because attendance caps are enforced for every entry time, tickets regularly sell out weeks in advance.

## Accessibility and Mobility Across the Grounds

For older adults and visitors managing physical limitations or joint pain, walking through a massive outdoor estate in late autumn and winter can feel daunting. Fortunately, Longwood Gardens has engineered the experience with extensive mobility accommodations:

- **Paved, Level Pathways:** The primary walking paths through both the outdoor light corridors and the indoor exhibits are fully paved and ADA-compliant, accommodating walkers, strollers, and wheelchairs smoothly.
- **Climate-Controlled Sanctuaries:** When chilly outdoor winds pick up, visitors can step inside the extensive, heated glass Conservatories to warm up without missing the horticultural displays.
- **Mobility Equipment Availability:** Visitors can request complimentary manual wheelchairs or reserve electric motorized scooters to navigate the expansive grounds without fatigue. Reservations and requests can be coordinated at the Visitor Center accessibility desk or arranged in advance by contacting guest services at 610-388-1000.
- **Rest Stops and Restrooms:** Accessible restrooms and comfortable rest benches are placed along the pathways, allowing visitors to pause and take in the glowing scenery at their own pace.

## Timing Your Visit to Avoid Crowds and Walking Strain

Weekend evenings during the holiday season often draw large crowds, which can lead to longer waits along narrow display corridors and increased congestion in parking areas. For retirees and older adults with flexible schedules, strategically choosing your arrival time can make a significant difference.

Booking a timed entry for a Wednesday or Thursday afternoon provides a much calmer environment. Arriving during the mid-afternoon—prior to twilight—allows you to examine the indoor botanical installations and the 22-foot rotating Fraser fir while natural daylight pours through the glass ceiling. As dusk sets in, you can effortlessly step outdoors to watch the half a million lights turn on against the evening sky.

Planning your trip on a weekday before peak holiday dates offers the double benefit of quieter pathways and a wider selection of available time slots.itors holding an eligible Pennsylvania ACCESS or SNAP card can inquire at the on-site ticketing desk on qualifying days for reduced-cost community access admission opportunities.

## Practical Steps for a Smooth Experience

Before heading out to the gardens, taking a few preparatory measures will help ensure your outing remains comfortable from arrival to departure.

- ☐ **Book your timed passes early:** Go to Longwoodgardens.org and reserve your date and entry window well ahead of time.
- ☐ **Select an off-peak slot:** Choose a weekday afternoon before peak holiday dates to avoid crowded pathways and peak pricing.
- ☐ **Arrange mobility assistance:** Call guest services at 610-388-1000 or stop by the Visitor Center desk to request a wheelchair or arrange an electric scooter.
- ☐ **Dress in layers:** Wear warm outdoor winter clothing that can be adjusted or unzipped when entering the heated glass conservatories.
- ☐ **Wear sensible footwear:** Put on supportive, slip-resistant walking shoes suited for extensive strolling across paved trails.
- ☐ **Bring documentation if applicable:** If you participate in state community access programs, bring your Pennsylvania ACCESS or SNAP card to the on-site desk on eligible days.
- ☐ **Check local senior recreational resources:** If you are seeking broader regional park passes or recreational discounts, contact your local Area Agency on Aging through eldercare.acl.gov or call 1-800-677-1116.

Taking the time to coordinate your tickets, transportation, and walking accommodations in advance allows you to focus on the sights, seasonal music, and horticultural artistry throughout the 2026 holiday season.
