Will high-tech automated routing smooth out your holiday flight, or will it create unexpected hurdles when navigating crowded terminals? **The Federal Aviation Administration has completed the nationwide rollout of its artificial intelligence platform across all 22 air route traffic control centers under an $875 million contract.** As millions of older Americans prepare to fly across the country for family gatherings, understanding how this system reshapes schedules and connecting gates is essential for a stress-free trip.

## The Numbers Behind the System: Fuel, Time, and Scale

The initiative, known as the Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes, and Trajectories (SMART) program, was developed over a 12-year contract with Air Space Intelligence. To anticipate airspace gridlock before planes ever push back from the gate, the cloud-based platform continuously analyzes approximately 200 data streams, ranging from incoming weather patterns and overall traffic volume to regional staffing levels.

During initial operational testing in the Washington, D.C., corridor in autumn 2026, federal officials evaluated how predictive rerouting handled real-world congestion:

## Why Automated Routing Matters for Senior Travelers

While saving 10 minutes in the air is a positive step for airline schedules, automated adjustments introduce new challenges on the ground. When computer-assisted models recalculate flight paths to bypass storms or busy corridors, arrival times and terminal gate assignments can shift rapidly.

For younger, unencumbered travelers, a sudden gate swap across a sprawling airport hub may simply mean walking faster. For older adults, especially those managing joint pain, heart conditions, or carrying heavy luggage, tight connections present serious physical strain. Walking between distant gates at major hubs like Chicago or Dallas can turn a planned 40-minute layover into an impossible sprint, raising the risk of missed flights and lost luggage.

Federal officials also addressed public anxiety following a five-hour nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, 2026. According to the Department of Transportation, that disruption stemmed from legacy telecommunications switch failures and had zero connection to the SMART software. Furthermore, the FAA emphasized that certified air traffic controllers maintain final human authority over every automated rerouting proposal.

## Protecting Your Connection: Practical Steps for Older Flyers

Navigating high-tech airport changes requires giving yourself more breathing room. Rushing through security or sprinting between concourses can ruin a family reunion before it starts. Consider these practical adjustments when planning and taking your flight:

- ☐ **Schedule wider layovers:** When booking holiday travel, avoid 40-minute connections. Aim for layovers between 90 and 120 minutes to absorb unexpected runway holds or concourse changes.
- ☐ **Book mobility assistance early:** Contact your airline's accessibility desk at least 48 hours before departure to arrange electric cart or wheelchair transfers between connecting gates.
- ☐ **Call TSA Cares:** Travelers with medical conditions or physical limitations can request dedicated screening assistance by calling TSA Cares at 1-855-787-2227 prior to travel.
- ☐ **Monitor airspace conditions independently:** Check real-time national airport delay programs and weather disruptions on fly.faa.gov before leaving home.
- ☐ **Confirm gates upon landing:** Check carrier smartphone apps or terminal departure monitors immediately after deplaning, as automated trajectory adjustments can alter connecting gates while you are airborne.

For questions regarding airline accessibility rights, consumer protections, or service complaints during flight delays, passengers can contact the U.S. Department of Transportation Aviation Consumer Protection Division at 202-366-2220 or visit transportation.gov/airconsumer.
