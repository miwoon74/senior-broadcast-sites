Are you preparing to travel across the state or across the country to spend Thanksgiving with family this week? If so, you will be sharing the journey with record-level crowds amidst challenging seasonal conditions. According to national travel projections from AAA, an estimated **81.5 million Americans** are expected to travel at least 50 miles away from home during the Thanksgiving holiday corridor. 

At the same time, major weather systems are moving across both the Pacific Northwest and the East Coast, bringing rain, freezing drizzle, and gusty winds right as holiday traffic surges. For older drivers, the combination of heavy traffic volume, adverse road surfaces, and early darkness presents unique challenges. Taking the time to understand the highway conditions, mechanical risks, and travel patterns can make the difference between a stressful ordeal and a safe, peaceful holiday reunion.

## By the Numbers: Record-Level Crowds and Rising Fuel Costs

Despite economic pressures, Thanksgiving travel volume remains near historic peaks. AAA projects that 81.5 million people will travel between Tuesday, November 24, and Monday, November 30. While this represents a modest dip of 0.1 percent compared to the 81.6 million travelers recorded in 2025, the highways will experience virtually the same intense bumper-to-bumper congestion seen during peak years.

The vast majority of travelers plan to drive rather than fly. AAA estimates that 72.9 million motorists will take to the roads, making up nearly 90 percent of all holiday travel.

## Severe Coastal Storms and Hazards for Older Drivers

Forecast updates from The Weather Channel and the National Weather Service indicate that severe storm systems are sweeping into both coasts. The Pacific Northwest and the East Coast are facing wet weather patterns that include steady rain, freezing drizzle, and strong wind gusts. 

For older motorists, winter driving hazards demand heightened attention. Wet pavement and sudden patches of freezing drizzle dramatically reduce tire traction, lengthening stopping distances. In addition, late autumn brings early dusk, meaning a substantial portion of afternoon and evening driving occurs in darkness or low-light conditions. Glare from oncoming headlights on wet roads can cause severe eye fatigue and temporarily obscure lane markings.

Reaction times naturally slow in dense, stop-and-go highway traffic. Safety analysts note that high traffic volumes paired with widespread driver distraction increase crash risk. If you are embarking on a long trip to visit children or grandchildren, transportation analysts warn that you should expect travel times to increase during peak periods. Congestion is expected to reach its highest intensity on Wednesday afternoon and Sunday evening.

Being stranded on an icy shoulder or in an unmoving traffic backup is particularly hazardous in freezing weather. Exposure to cold temperatures inside an unheated car can quickly compromise the health of older adults, especially those managing chronic medical conditions or circulation difficulties.

## Vehicle Preparedness: Preventing Roadside Breakdowns

Highway breakdowns can turn an uncomfortable trip into a life-threatening emergency. AAA notes that dead batteries and flat tires account for the vast majority of calls for emergency roadside assistance during the holiday season.

Cold temperatures diminish a vehicle battery's cranking power. A battery that functions adequately in mild weather may fail completely when overnight temperatures drop. Similarly, fluctuating autumn temperatures cause tire pressure to fall, creating uneven tread wear, reduced handling capability, and a greater risk of tire failure on high-speed interstate corridors.

Motorists should have a qualified mechanic inspect their vehicle battery and verify tire pressure before departing. Completing these checks prior to Wednesday, November 25, helps drivers avoid long wait times at service stations and minimizes the danger of experiencing a mechanical failure during the height of holiday gridlock.

## Practical Strategies for Navigating Holiday Traffic

Planning ahead and making minor adjustments to departure schedules can significantly reduce road-related stress:

1. **Avoid Peak Congestion Windows:** Because highway gridlock concentrates heavily on Wednesday afternoon and Sunday evening, consider scheduling your driving segments during off-peak hours. Planning to drive during daylight hours helps you avoid the combined hazards of darkness, dropping temperatures, and visual glare.
2. **Pack Critical Essentials in the Passenger Cabin:** Never place vital necessities in the trunk. If a highway collision or weather closure halts traffic for extended periods, you must have access to your supplies without leaving the vehicle. Keep prescription medications, drinking water, shelf-stable snacks, warm blankets, and mobile phone chargers inside the passenger cabin.
3. **Monitor Live Highway Feeds Before Leaving:** Check state Department of Transportation live camera feeds, active road closure reports, and official travel bulletins before pulling out of your driveway. Online traveler resources and regional 511 traveler lines provide up-to-the-minute updates on highway conditions.
4. **Know Your Emergency Contacts:** Keep roadside assistance numbers easily accessible. AAA roadside dispatch can be contacted nationwide by calling 1-800-222-4357.

## Pre-Departure Travel Checklist

Before embarking on your holiday drive, review this practical readiness checklist:

- ☐ Have your car battery tested and tire pressure inspected by a mechanic prior to Wednesday, November 25.
- ☐ Pack essential daily medications and extra doses directly inside the passenger cabin where they can be reached easily.
- ☐ Place warm blankets, drinking water, and shelf-stable snacks on the back seat in case of unexpected delays.
- ☐ Verify that your mobile phone is fully charged and that you have a working vehicle charging cord packed.
- ☐ Check live highway conditions, state traffic cameras, and weather hazard updates via Weather.gov and your state 511 service.
- ☐ Save the AAA emergency dispatch telephone number (1-800-222-4357) in your mobile phone contacts.
- ☐ Plan your route to avoid peak travel periods on Wednesday afternoon and Sunday evening.
