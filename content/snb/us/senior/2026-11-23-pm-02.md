Are you relying solely on Original Medicare for your retirement healthcare? Many retirees are surprised to learn that **Original Medicare Part B pays only 80 percent of approved outpatient expenses, leaving beneficiaries responsible for an uncapped 20 percent coinsurance.** Unlike many commercial employer plans, Original Medicare has no annual ceiling on what you can be forced to pay out of your own pocket. A single major outpatient procedure or serious diagnosis can lead to thousands of dollars in sudden medical bills.

Understanding how private Medicare Supplement Insurance—widely known as Medigap—operates can help you protect your hard-earned retirement savings against severe healthcare debt.

## By the Numbers: The 20% Out-of-Pocket Gap

When you receive physician services, surgical procedures, diagnostic imaging, or outpatient clinical therapies under Original Medicare Part B, Medicare covers 80 percent of the approved cost once you satisfy your annual deductible. 

The remaining 20 percent falls entirely on the patient. Because Original Medicare lacks an annual out-of-pocket maximum, there is no limit to your financial exposure over the course of a calendar year.

Consider a concrete retirement budget scenario. If an older adult receives an average Social Security check of $1,900 per month and requires an outpatient knee replacement totaling $10,000 in clinical charges:

- **Original Medicare pays:** 80%, or $8,000.
- **The patient owes:** 20%, or $2,000.

In this single instance, the beneficiary's $2,000 bill surpasses an entire month of Social Security income ($1,900). With a standardized policy such as Medigap Plan G, that full $2,000 coinsurance bill is paid by the supplement policy after the annual Part B deductible has been met.

## How Standardized Medigap Plans Safeguard Retirement Budgets

Medigap policies are sold by private insurance companies but are standardized federally across states from Plan A through Plan N. This standardization means that the basic medical benefits of a specific plan letter—such as Plan G or Plan N—are identical regardless of which insurance company issues the policy.

The primary function of these plans is to pay Medicare's cost-sharing requirements, including copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles.

| Coverage Component | Original Medicare Alone | With Medigap Plan G |
|---|---|---|
| Part B Coinsurance | Patient Pays 20% Uncapped | $0 Copay (Covered 100%) |
| Annual Out-of-Pocket Cap | None (Unlimited Risk) | Predictable Premium Only |
| Doctor Network Freedom | Any Doctor Accepting Medicare | Nationwide Access |
| Worked $10,000 Procedure Cost | Patient Pays $2,000 Out-of-Pocket | Patient Pays $0 (After Deductible) |

By stepping in to absorb the 20 percent Part B coinsurance, Medigap converts unpredictable, catastrophic medical liability into a predictable monthly insurance premium. In addition, because Medigap works directly alongside Original Medicare, beneficiaries maintain nationwide access to any physician or healthcare facility that accepts Medicare patients without restrictive network requirements.

## Important Enrollment Timelines and Rights

Timing is critical when considering a Medigap policy. Beneficiaries have a six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period. This window begins automatically the first month an individual is both 65 or older and enrolled in Medicare Part B.

During this specific six-month period:
- You have guaranteed issue rights.
- Private insurers cannot deny you coverage due to past or current health conditions.
- Private insurers cannot charge you higher premiums based on medical underwriting or pre-existing conditions.

If you miss this enrollment window and attempt to purchase a Medigap plan later, insurers in most states may use medical underwriting, charge higher rates, or deny coverage entirely.

## Step-by-Step Action Plan for Beneficiaries

Reviewing your coverage statements helps verify whether your household is shielded against uncapped cost sharing:

- ☐ Check your official Part B enrollment date to track your six-month guaranteed issue open enrollment rights.
- ☐ Examine your quarterly Medicare Summary Notice to calculate whether your cumulative 20 percent coinsurance expenses exceed the annual cost of a Medigap premium.
- ☐ Visit Medicare.gov to utilize the official Medigap Plan Finder tool and compare standardized Plan G and Plan N options and premiums in your ZIP code.
- ☐ Review your healthcare statements and consult a benefits counselor before December 7.
- ☐ Call your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) toll-free at 1-877-839-2675 for free, unbiased, one-on-one counseling.

> Official plan rules and cost-sharing guidelines are established by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Verify standardized plan availability and local premium quotes directly through Medicare.gov or your state counseling agency.
