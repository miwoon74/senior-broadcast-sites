Prescription medications account for one of the largest ongoing expenses for older households, leaving many retirees to wonder when relief will reach their monthly pharmacy receipts. Federal health officials at the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalized landmark discounts on 13 costly brand-name medications used to treat serious chronic illnesses, including diabetes, cancer, and arthritis. Across the entire program, these newly negotiated maximum fair prices will yield **$464 million in direct out-of-pocket savings** for enrollees while reducing federal healthcare spending by billions of dollars.

Understanding how these price reductions work, which therapies are included, and what concrete steps you should take during open enrollment will help you make the most informed choices for your coverage.

## The Scope of the Reductions: A 45 Percent Net Savings

Under federal drug price negotiation statutes, regulators concluded the third cycle of price negotiations targeting high-spend medications. The newly established prices will generate an estimated 45 percent net savings for the Medicare program, amounting to $9.2 billion in annual spending reductions based on 2025 healthcare spending records.

The 13 therapies selected under this round represent significant financial burdens for both the government and individual participants. In 2025 alone, approximately 1.4 million Medicare Part D enrollees filled prescriptions for the selected oral therapies, while roughly 120,000 clinic patients under Part B received physician-administered treatments from the negotiated group.

Among the largest reductions are treatments for type 2 diabetes. Trulicity received an 84 percent discount, lowering its monthly cost from $965 down to $150. Tradjenta, another widely prescribed diabetes medication, saw an 83 percent reduction, dropping from $476 to $81 per month. For individuals managing autoimmune and respiratory conditions, Anoro Ellipta received a 72 percent cut, while the immunosuppressive therapy Cosentyx experienced a 67 percent decrease, bringing its monthly expense down from $7,878 to $2,600.

## Why These Price Cuts Matter for Chronic Illness Management

Living on a fixed retirement budget often means balancing essential living expenses against recurring medical care. The 13 medications included in this announcement treat long-term, complex conditions such as diabetes, Crohn's disease, psoriatic arthritis, and cancer. Because these chronic conditions require consistent adherence, steep drug prices have forced many households to shoulder heavy financial burdens year after year.

When manufacturer prices drop dramatically, the structural savings ripple through the entire Medicare system:

- **Lower direct cost-sharing:** When you pick up a prescription subject to coinsurance—where you pay a percentage of the total drug price rather than a flat fee—a lower baseline price means a significantly smaller charge at the register.
- **Protection against premium spikes:** High gross spending by Part D plans exerts upward pressure on monthly premiums across the board. Lowering manufacturer prices by $9.2 billion helps protect Part D plan stability and limits premium surges in future coverage years.
- **Improved access for clinic-based treatments:** For the 120,000 enrollees receiving physician-administered therapies under Part B, reduced baseline pricing directly curbs out-of-pocket exposure during outpatient clinic visits.

## Navigating Formularies and Plan Selections

A lower negotiated price creates the framework for savings, but individual out-of-pocket costs still depend on how private insurers structure their specific plan options. Medicare Advantage plans and stand-alone Part D prescription drug plans place medications onto distinct formulary tiers. Each tier carries its own copayment or coinsurance schedule.

As insurers update their 2027 drug formularies to incorporate the new pricing agreements, beneficiaries must examine how their specific plans categorize each medication. A drug with a substantially lower negotiated price may shift tiers, potentially lowering a standard copayment or altering prior authorization rules.

Reviewing your Annual Notice of Change ensures that your current dosages and brand-name therapies remain covered without administrative interruptions. Enrollees taking any of these 13 therapies should verify that their chosen plan offers favorable formulary placement before finalizing their coverage choices.

## Action Steps Before the Open Enrollment Deadline

Medicare open enrollment provides a limited annual window to adjust coverage. Taking organized steps now ensures your medications remain affordable and accessible:

- ☐ Check the official list of 13 negotiated medications by reviewing announcement details on the official CMS website.
- ☐ Log into your personal account on Medicare.gov before the December 7 open enrollment deadline.
- ☐ Enter your exact prescription names and dosage amounts into the Medicare Plan Finder tool to calculate your projected 2027 out-of-pocket expenses.
- ☐ Review your plan's Annual Notice of Change to verify that your prescribed brand-name treatments remain on the formulary.
- ☐ Connect with a trained counselor through your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) by calling 1-877-839-2675 or visiting shiphelp.org for personalized guidance.
