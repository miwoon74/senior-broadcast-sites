Every holiday season, grandparents face the familiar challenge of trying to pick the right holiday gifts for their teenage and young adult grandchildren. If you are wondering whether to buy another sweater, electronic gadget, or store-bought present, recent holiday consumer research offers clear guidance: **over 70 percent of Generation Z respondents report that they prefer shared experiential gifts over physical items.**

Rather than unwrapping a boxed present, today's young adults are looking for quality time, personal companionship, and activities shared with family members. Understanding this generational shift can relieve the pressure of holiday shopping while opening the door to deeper, more memorable connections with your grandchildren.

## By the Numbers: How Young Adults View Holiday Gifting

Recent consumer sentiment research published in November 2026 by organizations including PwC, CivicScience, and Forbes highlights a noticeable evolution in how younger generations celebrate and exchange gifts. Rather than collecting material possessions, younger adults are prioritizing communal connection and memorable outings.

The findings reveal several key dynamics in how young people approach the holiday season:

- **Experiential preference:** More than 70 percent of Generation Z and young Millennial adults express a clear preference for receiving experiences—such as concert tickets, cooking workshops, museum admissions, or weekend trips—over material items.
- **Communal holiday activities:** According to consumer research from CivicScience in November 2026, 38 percent of Gen Z adults participated in or hosted Friendsgiving dinners, showing an appetite for shared meals and peer-to-peer traditions alongside family gatherings.
- **Shared living arrangements:** Housing affordability figures from Realtor.com in November 2026 indicate that 25.2 million adults between the ages of 18 and 34 reside in multigenerational households. This physical closeness provides daily opportunities for grandparents and young adult grandchildren to connect through shared routines and activities.

## Why Young Adults Crave Time Over Material Possessions

The desire for experiences rather than physical presents stems from both practical realities and emotional needs. Many young adults today live in compact apartments or shared housing where storage space is limited, meaning extra gadgets and boxed household goods often translate into unwanted apartment clutter.

At the same time, behavioral psychologists point to the emotional value of shared anticipation. When you plan an event or outing, the excitement begins long before the day arrives. Looking forward to a shared concert, workshop, or road trip builds interpersonal engagement in a way that unwrapping a manufactured product cannot. While material items quickly lose their novelty after being opened, shared experiences create lasting family memories that both generations carry forward.

Younger adults also express a growing desire for authentic connection free from the distraction of digital screens. Sitting side by side at a play, sharing a meal, or learning a practical culinary skill allows for natural conversation, laughter, and personal storytelling.

## Comparing Material Gifts With Experiential Bonding

Planning an experiential gift does not require spending more money than you would on a traditional boxed gift. In many cases, tickets to an afternoon theater matinee, a local sporting event, or a community workshop cost the same as a retail item, yet deliver significantly higher emotional value.

## Practical Steps to Plan an Experiential Holiday Gift

If you want to move away from physical shopping lists and offer an experience this season, several accessible strategies can help you tailor the gift to your grandchild's unique interests:

1. **Design a personalized "Experience Coupon":** Create a handwritten certificate or decorative card promising an outing of their choice. Ideas can include an afternoon at a local art museum, a walk through a botanical garden, tickets to a regional sporting match, or seats at a local comedy performance.
2. **Host a hands-on culinary session:** With 38 percent of young adults participating in communal holiday gatherings like Friendsgiving, young people are eager to learn authentic cooking skills. Invite your grandchild into your kitchen to teach them a cherished family holiday recipe, complete with a handwritten recipe card they can keep.
3. **Take advantage of combined ticketing discounts:** Many community playhouses, regional symphony orchestras, and state park systems offer discounted admission rates for seniors and students. Look into paired ticketing options to reduce the cost of an outing for both of you.
4. **Leverage public library and civic passes:** Many municipal library networks and local cultural affairs departments maintain pass-lending programs that provide free or steeply discounted admission to local science centers, historical landmarks, and art galleries.
5. **Connect with community multigenerational programs:** Contact your local Area Agency on Aging through the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 or visit eldercare.acl.gov to learn about local multigenerational community programs, senior-youth volunteer projects, and regional cultural passes.

## Planning Checklist for Grandparents

- ☐ Ask your grandchild what local food spots, museum exhibits, or performing artists they enjoy following.
- ☐ Use upcoming family gatherings, such as Thanksgiving dinner, to discuss potential December outing dates together.
- ☐ Select an activity that allows comfortable conversation without loud distractions or rushing.
- ☐ Check municipal portals, public library systems, or state park services for senior and student discount passes.
- ☐ Mark the event on your calendars well in advance so both of you can enjoy the anticipation of spending time together.
