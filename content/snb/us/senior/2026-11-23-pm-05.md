Are you spending a growing portion of your monthly income or retirement savings to help care for an aging spouse, parent, or relative? According to research from the AARP Public Policy Institute, family caregivers in the United States now spend an average of **$11,508 out of pocket each year** to support aging loved ones at home. This spending represents a 59 percent surge over a five-year span, placing severe financial pressure on millions of American households who provide essential daily assistance.

Understanding where this money goes and knowing which community support services exist can help prevent personal burnout and protect your family budget.

## By the Numbers: Out-of-Pocket Caregiving Costs Jump 59 Percent

The 2026 AARP study surveyed 2,538 family caregivers across the country to measure personal financial contributions to loved ones. The findings demonstrate that caregiving expenses have climbed sharply across every category, outpacing typical household cost increases.

In 2021, the average annual out-of-pocket spending reported by caregivers stood at $7,242. By 2026, that figure reached $11,508. This jump of nearly 60 percent reflects compounding pressures from inflation, rising healthcare costs, and expanding needs as loved ones age in place.

The burden is even more pronounced for families managing cognitive decline. Caring for an individual with Alzheimer's disease or related dementias requires specialized support, supervision, and safety equipment, pushing average annual out-of-pocket spending to $15,846, up 31.5 percent from $12,050 in 2021.

## How Caregiving Expenses Strain Household Incomes

Caregiving expenses do not affect every family equally. In 2021, out-of-pocket costs absorbed approximately 26 percent of a caregiver's annual household income. In 2026, that proportion climbed to an average of 31 percent.

For families with lower annual earnings, the financial impact is acute. Caregivers in households earning under $40,000 dedicate an alarming 76 percent of their annual income to care-related expenses. 

Consider a typical fixed-income scenario: a senior receiving a monthly retirement pension of $3,000 who spends nearly $1,000 each month on groceries, home accessibility ramps, and prescription copayments for an aging spouse is surrendering a third of their living budget to care duties. Nearly nine out of ten caregivers now cover regular daily living expenses for their loved ones. To keep up with these obligations, caregivers frequently withdraw money from retirement accounts, reduce their working hours, or carry personal debt, putting their own long-term security at risk.

Nationwide, approximately 59 million family caregivers deliver unpaid care, providing an estimated economic contribution surpassing $1 trillion annually. Yet individual caregivers shoulder these burdens with little structured financial compensation.

## Where the Money Goes: Housing and Healthcare Lead Costs

The study shows that 71 percent of all out-of-pocket caregiving expenditures fall into two main categories: housing adaptations and medical care.

1. **Housing Adaptations and Utilities (42 percent of total):** Caregivers spend an average of $4,797 annually to keep homes safe and functional. This includes installing grab bars, building wheelchair ramps, widening doorways, and covering elevated utility expenses associated with running medical devices or maintaining home temperature controls.
2. **Medical Services and Prescriptions (29 percent of total):** Caregivers spend an average of $3,313 each year on out-of-pocket healthcare needs. This includes prescription copayments, durable medical equipment, incontinence supplies, and physical therapy sessions not fully reimbursed by health insurance.
3. **Daily Living and Transportation (Remaining 29 percent):** The rest of caregiver spending covers specialized dietary groceries, assistive transport services, clothing adaptations, and supplemental in-home supervision.

Because housing and medical bills make up the majority of caregiver spending, finding public programs that subsidize these specific categories can deliver immediate relief.

## Programs and Steps to Reduce Out-of-Pocket Expenses

Family caregivers do not have to absorb these escalating costs alone. Federal, state, and local agencies maintain programs designed to subsidize modifications, provide in-home respite breaks, and offset direct living costs.

### 1. Apply for Area Agency on Aging Respite Grants
Every community is served by a local Area Agency on Aging (AAA). Through the Title III-E National Family Caregiver Support Program, AAAs distribute respite grants to help pay for temporary companion care, adult day care, and basic home modifications. You can find your regional agency online through the Administration for Community Living at Eldercare.acl.gov or by calling the Eldercare Locator toll-free at 1-800-677-1116.

### 2. Explore State Medicaid Self-Directed Care Waivers
Many states allow Medicaid recipients to self-direct their personal care funds. Under these waiver options, an aging parent or relative may be permitted to hire and compensate a family member directly for home care assistance, replacing lost wages and helping offset direct household outlays. Contact your state Medicaid office or local aging agency to evaluate eligibility guidelines.

### 3. Screen for Core Benefits
Programs exist to reduce everyday costs for seniors living on fixed incomes, freeing up household cash for medical and dietary necessities. Use free screening resources such as BenefitsCheckUp.org to determine whether your loved one qualifies for:
- Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) food benefits
- Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) heating and cooling subsidies
- Medicare Extra Help or state pharmaceutical assistance programs to reduce medication copayments

### 4. Connect With Community Helplines
Dialing 2-1-1 connects families to local United Way community specialists who can locate nearby food pantries, volunteer-led home repair groups, and non-emergency medical transportation services.

## Action Checklist for Family Caregivers

- ☐ Log your caregiving expenses for one month to identify whether housing modifications or prescription copays drive your spending.
- ☐ Visit Eldercare.acl.gov or call 1-800-677-1116 to locate your local Area Agency on Aging and inquire about Title III-E caregiver respite funds.
- ☐ Check BenefitsCheckUp.org to verify whether your care recipient qualifies for SNAP, LIHEAP utility assistance, or Medicare prescription subsidies.
- ☐ Ask your state Medicaid agency whether a self-directed personal care waiver is available to reimburse caregiving hours.
- ☐ Dial 2-1-1 to ask about local volunteer home repair initiatives and medical transit programs in your municipality.
