The holiday shopping season brings warmth, family gatherings, and gift-giving, but it also marks the most active period of the year for cybercriminals and organized fraud rings. According to recent research from the AARP Fraud Watch Network, **84% of American adults were targeted by or experienced at least one prevalent form of scam in 2026.** Scammers rely heavily on artificial urgency, holiday distractions, and sophisticated digital messages to catch consumers off guard. 

Understanding the specific mechanics of these cons is the most dependable way to keep your household finances and hard-earned retirement savings secure throughout the festive period.

## The Numbers Behind Holiday Scams in 2026

To examine how fraud rings operate during the peak retail rush, ANR Market Research conducted a nationwide representative survey of 1,801 U.S. adults on behalf of AARP. The study revealed an intense wave of fraudulent shopping, shipping, and payment solicitations reaching consumers across multiple channels, including smartphone text messages, email inboxes, and retail shelves.

The findings highlight that deceptive shipping alerts, counterfeit road toll notices, and compromised gift cards top the list of threats this season.

The scale of these numbers underscores that encountering a con artist is no longer an isolated event. Nearly half of all surveyed adults reported receiving deceptive delivery tracking texts, while more than two in five encountered bogus unpaid road toll alerts. In addition, more than a third of consumers bought or received gift cards whose balances had been drained before checkout.

## How Common Holiday Deceptions Operate

Scammers design their operations around seasonal behaviors. During late autumn and early winter, households across the country track dozens of incoming packages and travel along regional highways to visit loved ones. Fraud rings take advantage of this high-volume activity to blend deceptive solicitations into everyday life.

### 1. Fake Delivery Tracking Notifications (47%)

Nearly half of all adults surveyed (47%) reported receiving fraudulent text messages claiming a package could not be delivered due to an incorrect address, unpaid customs fee, or missing delivery instruction. These short messages almost always contain an embedded hyperlink directing users to a counterfeit postal or parcel service webpage.

When an unsuspecting recipient enters a credit card number or personal identity details to pay a minor redelivery fee, scammers capture the credentials. In minutes, criminals can use those banking details to compromise existing financial accounts or conduct unauthorized transactions.

### 2. Bogus Unpaid Road Toll Alerts (44%)

Another widespread con involves urgent text messages claiming the recipient owes an immediate payment for an unpaid highway or bridge toll. The survey showed that 44% of adults received these fraudulent alerts in 2026. 

These notifications frequently threaten steep administrative fines, immediate license suspension, or legal action if payment is not remitted through an included web link. Because millions of motorists drive through automated electronic toll booths without stopping at physical cash lanes, many drivers assume the notification might be authentic.

### 3. Skimmed and Drained Gift Cards (35%)

Gift cards remain one of the most convenient presents for grandchildren and family members, yet retail store racks have become prime targets for tampering. The survey found that 35% of consumers bought or received store gift cards that had a zero remaining balance when the recipient attempted to redeem them.

Criminals visit physical store aisles, scan or copy barcode numbers from exposed display racks, and replace the scratch-off protective security strips. Software programs then monitor the card accounts until an unsuspecting shopper loads money at the cash register. The instant the card is activated, the fraudsters siphon off the funds online before the intended recipient can ever open the gift.

## International Approaches to Scam Prevention: Lessons from Japan

Consumer fraud during major retail periods is not unique to the United States. Looking overseas reveals how other nations coordinate community defenses against similar retail scams.

In Japan, the National Police Agency and the Consumer Affairs Agency collaborate closely with national convenience store chains to shield older residents from financial exploitation. Because fraudsters frequently instruct victims over the phone to purchase large amounts of prepaid electronic money cards, Japanese retail stores implement rigorous safeguards at the cash register:

- Convenience store clerks receive formal training to recognize older shoppers purchasing elevated volumes of prepaid cards or digital gift vouchers.
- Store registers display automated, mandatory bilingual fraud warning screens before any prepaid card transaction can be completed.
- Retail employees are empowered and prompted by point-of-sale systems to gently intervene and ask questions if an older patron appears to be following live phone instructions while purchasing digital gift cards.

These proactive counter interventions create a vital physical barrier between coordinated fraud rings and vulnerable consumer savings.

## Action Checklist: How to Keep Your Holiday Shopping Safe

Adopting practical habits can dramatically reduce your exposure to seasonal fraud. Review this checklist before completing your shopping or responding to automated messages:

- ☐ **Verify parcel shipments directly:** Never click on an embedded link sent via an unsolicited text message. If you expect a package, open a web browser and check the tracking number directly on the official carrier site, such as usps.com.
- ☐ **Forward suspicious text messages:** When you receive a questionable package notification or unpaid road toll demand, forward the text message directly to **7726** (SPAM) to notify your mobile carrier.
- ☐ **Inspect gift cards before buying:** Avoid picking cards from open revolving racks in high-traffic aisles. Ask the retail cashier to retrieve an untouched card from behind the checkout register, and check the packaging to confirm the protective PIN film has not been scratched off or altered.
- ☐ **Ignore urgent toll payment warnings:** State and regional turnpike authorities do not issue final debt collection notices via urgent text messages with shortened web links. Log into your established toll account directly or call the toll service agency using the customer service number on your physical transponder.
- ☐ **Keep fraud assistance contacts accessible:** If you suspect an unauthorized transaction or encounter a con artist, call the AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline toll-free at **1-877-908-3360** to speak with a trained fraud specialist.

Taking a deliberate pause before clicking unexpected links or paying unfamiliar invoices is the most dependable way to protect your finances and enjoy a secure, stress-free holiday season.
