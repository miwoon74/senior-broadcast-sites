How will your monthly healthcare costs change when the new calendar year begins? The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has announced the official cost-sharing rates for 2027, setting the standard monthly Medicare Part B premium at **$209.40**. Because Part B premiums are deducted straight from monthly Social Security benefits, every retiree needs to understand how this upward adjustment interacts with upcoming cost-of-living increases. While costs are going up across multiple parts of the program, the net result for the average beneficiary remains positive.

Understanding these updated baseline figures allows you to plan your household budget, review your current plan choices, and avoid unexpected financial surprises when January benefit payments arrive.

## The Official 2027 Medicare Numbers at a Glance

The standard monthly Part B premium covers doctor visits, outpatient hospital services, durable medical equipment, and preventive care. For 2027, the standard monthly rate climbs from $202.90 to $209.40. This reflects an increase of $6.50 per month, or 3.2 percent. According to federal officials, the premium adjustment is driven by projected increases in outpatient hospital spending and broader health care utilization.

Along with the monthly premium, the annual Part B deductible is also shifting. In 2027, beneficiaries will pay an annual Part B deductible of $292.00 before Medicare begins paying its share, marking an increase of $9.00 from the 2026 deductible of $283.00.

Inpatient care under Medicare Part A is experiencing adjustments as well. While most older adults receive Part A premium-free based on their work history, hospital stays still involve deductibles and copayments. For 2027, the Part A inpatient hospital deductible rises to $1,776.00, an increase of $40.00 from the 2026 level of $1,736.00.



The table below outlines the primary cost adjustments across both Part A and Part B between 2026 and 2027.

| Medicare Cost Element | 2026 Level | 2027 Official Level | Change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Standard Monthly Part B Premium | $202.90 | $209.40 | +$6.50 (+3.2%) |
| Annual Part B Deductible | $283.00 | $292.00 | +$9.00 |
| Part A Inpatient Hospital Deductible | $1,736.00 | $1,776.00 | +$40.00 |

## How the Part B Increase Interacts with Your Social Security Check

For the vast majority of beneficiaries, Medicare Part B premiums are paid automatically through a direct deduction from their monthly Social Security check. Therefore, any rise in Part B premiums directly offsets the annual Social Security cost-of-living adjustment (COLA).

The Social Security Administration announced a COLA for 2027; check the latest at ssa.gov/cola.

When you factor in the healthcare adjustment:
- **Gross Monthly Increase:** The average retiree receives an additional $75.00 per month from the 3.6 percent COLA.
- **Medicare Part B Deduction:** The standard monthly premium increases by $6.50 (from $202.90 to $209.40).
- **Net Monthly Increase:** The Part B increase absorbs about 9 percent of the average gross raise, leaving the typical recipient with a net gain of roughly $68.50 per month.

This means that despite the rising cost of outpatient healthcare coverage, typical retirees will still see their net monthly bank deposits grow rather than shrink in January 2027.

## Higher-Income Surcharges: Who Pays IRMAA in 2027?

Not every beneficiary pays the standard $209.40 monthly rate. Beneficiaries with higher reported incomes are subject to an additional fee known as the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA). 

Federal records indicate that approximately 9 percent of Medicare beneficiaries pay these income-related surcharges. For the 2027 plan year, IRMAA surcharges begin for individual tax filers with income levels starting above $112,000.00. Those subject to IRMAA will pay additional amounts on top of the standard $209.40 base premium for Part B, as well as an extra surcharge on their Part D prescription drug coverage.

If your income has recently decreased because of a major life change—such as retirement, a reduction in work hours, or a change in marital status—you do not necessarily have to accept the initial IRMAA determination. Beneficiaries can file Form SSA-44 with the Social Security Administration at Ssa.gov to request an IRMAA surcharge waiver based on qualifying life-changing events.

## Practical Steps to Protect Your 2027 Healthcare Budget

Because Medicare open enrollment runs through the December 7 deadline, the announcement of the 2027 cost structures provides an immediate opportunity to review your overall coverage. Taking a few proactive administrative steps before plan selections lock in can help you maximize your net monthly income.

### 1. Check Your Personal Benefit Portal
You do not have to wait until your January bank statement arrives to see your exact net benefit. You can view your personalized 2027 COLA increase and specific Medicare premium deductions by logging into your personal "my Social Security" account online at Ssa.gov/myaccount.

### 2. Compare Part D Drug Tiers and Advantage Plans
Even though standard Part B premiums are set at the federal level, prescription drug plans and Medicare Advantage offerings vary widely. Beneficiaries can review their medications across different prescription Part D tiers and evaluate Medicare Advantage options at Medicare.gov. You can also explore coverage options and get answers to plan questions by calling 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).

### 3. Seek Free, Unbiased Local Guidance
Evaluating health plans can feel overwhelming. Older adults can connect with free, objective counseling through their state State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP). SHIP counselors provide unbiased guidance on plan comparisons, Medicare Advantage, and premium assistance programs. You can find your local program through shiphelp.org or by calling 1-877-839-2675.

## Action Checklist for the 2027 Benefit Year

- ☐ Log into your personal account at Ssa.gov/myaccount to review your official 2027 COLA notice and net monthly benefit payment.
- ☐ Check your prescription drugs against plan formularies at Medicare.gov before the December 7 open enrollment deadline.
- ☐ Determine whether your modified adjusted gross income exceeds current IRS/SSA thresholds to see if you are subject to IRMAA surcharges; check the latest at ssa.gov/medicare.
- ☐ If your income dropped due to retirement, marriage changes, or reduced work, submit Form SSA-44 at Ssa.gov to request an IRMAA review.
- ☐ Contact a local SHIP counselor at 1-877-839-2675 or shiphelp.org if you need one-on-one help reviewing premium assistance or plan tiers.
- ☐ Update your household monthly spending plan to account for the $209.40 Part B premium and the $292.00 annual outpatient deductible.
