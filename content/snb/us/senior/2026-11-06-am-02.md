## Why this matters for older adults

- Respiratory infections often pose greater risk for older adults, especially those with chronic lung disease or weakened immune systems. While human cases of avian influenza remain uncommon, the reported death raises concern because it shows spillover from infected birds to people can have serious outcomes.
- Many seniors live in or visit rural areas where poultry or wild birds are more common; people who handle birds (professionally or informally) face higher exposure risk.

## What public health reports say (based on this week’s coverage)

- News outlets report the recent Washington death as the third confirmed U.S. human death tied to avian influenza in 2026.
- Local media reported confirmed detections at a commercial poultry facility in Pierce County, Washington, in early November 2026.
- The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention maintains publicly available guidance for avian influenza at cdc.gov/avianflu (refer to state and local health department notices for area-specific recommendations).

## Practical steps seniors and families can take

- If you handle poultry or wild birds:
  - Use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE): N95 or equivalent mask, gloves, and eye protection when in close contact with birds or bird environments.
  - Avoid direct contact with sick or dead birds; report them to your state veterinarian or local animal health authority.
- Health precautions:
  - Get your annual seasonal influenza vaccine; follow your doctor’s advice about pneumococcal vaccination if you have chronic lung disease or other risk factors.
  - Call your healthcare provider promptly if you develop fever, cough, shortness of breath, or other respiratory symptoms after contact with birds.
- Stay informed:
  - Check the CDC avian influenza page (cdc.gov/avianflu) and your state health department for up-to-date advisories and local detection reports.

## Checklist for seniors and caregivers

- ☐ Do I or a household member have regular contact with poultry or other birds?  
- ☐ Do we have N95 (or equivalent) masks, gloves, and eye protection available for bird-handling tasks?  
- ☐ Are seasonal flu and any recommended vaccines up to date for household members at higher risk?  
- ☐ Do we know how to report sick or dead birds to local authorities?  
- ☐ Do we have a plan to contact our healthcare provider quickly if respiratory symptoms develop after bird exposure?
