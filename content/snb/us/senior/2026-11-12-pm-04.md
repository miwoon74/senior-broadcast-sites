When an older adult prepares to leave a hospital bed after surgery, illness, or an unexpected fall, the next question is often daunting: where will recovery happen? For millions of families, the default answer has long been an institutional nursing home. Yet federal records reveal that while approximately **40% of older adults** discharged from acute hospital wards require post-acute rehabilitation, an overwhelming majority want nothing to do with institutional stays. 

On November 12, 2026, a bipartisan group of federal lawmakers took a major step toward changing that dynamic. Representatives Adrian Smith, a Republican from Nebraska, and Debbie Dingell, a Democrat from Michigan, formally introduced the Bringing Rehabilitation, Integrated Delivery, and Greater Extended Care at Home Act—known as the BRIDGE Care at Home Act. 

The proposed legislation would create a five-year Medicare demonstration program designed to bring intensive skilled nursing, physical therapy, and post-acute recovery services directly into patients' living rooms. For older adults and family caregivers navigating a complex post-hospital healthcare system, this measure could reshape how recovery works across the United States.

## The Gap Between Patient Wishes and Facility Care

Under current traditional Medicare guidelines, seniors who need extended physical therapy, specialized rehabilitation, or daily wound management after an inpatient hospital stay are generally directed to a brick-and-mortar skilled nursing facility. To qualify for this coverage, patients typically must log an inpatient hospital stay of at least three days.

Once discharged, however, many seniors encounter serious obstacles. Across the country, severe staffing constraints and bed shortages at institutional nursing homes leave vulnerable patients stuck in acute hospital rooms far longer than medically necessary. Even when a bed is available, entering a facility takes individuals away from their personal routines, their families, and the supportive comfort of their own homes.

Consumer surveys cited by lawmakers indicate that **85% of recovering patients** strongly favor home-based care over admission to a nursing home. When patients have direct experience with home health services, that preference remains clear: recovering in familiar surroundings supports mental well-being and allows families to stay closely involved in day-to-day care.

## How the BRIDGE Care at Home Act Would Work

The BRIDGE Care at Home Act is structured as a nationwide five-year Medicare pilot model. Rather than forcing a binary choice between an institutional nursing home and unassisted discharge, the bill instructs Medicare to fund skilled nursing facility services inside private residences following an inpatient stay.

Under the demonstration program, Medicare beneficiaries who meet clinical criteria could return straight home from the hospital while receiving the exact clinical care they would otherwise get in a specialized facility. Key elements outlined in the bill include:

- **Registered Nurse Home Visits:** Daily or frequent visits from registered nurses to handle clinical tasks such as wound dressings, intravenous medications, and vital sign assessments.
- **Physical and Occupational Therapy:** In-home sessions with licensed physical therapists to rebuild mobility, strength, and balance, helping seniors navigate the actual hallways, stairs, and furniture of their own living spaces.
- **Continuous Medical Monitoring:** Clinical check-ins and remote monitoring to catch complications early and prevent preventable trips back to the emergency department.
- **Rigorous Federal Oversight:** The bill requires the Department of Health and Human Services to evaluate patient outcomes, readmission rates, and overall Medicare cost savings throughout the 5-year study.

If the five-year data demonstrates that home-based rehabilitation delivers equal or superior clinical recovery while keeping costs manageable, the findings could clear the path for permanent Medicare adoption nationwide.

## International Precedents: Lessons from Canada

The concept of bringing intensive post-acute clinical services into the home is already operating successfully beyond U.S. borders. In Ontario, Canada, the Ministry of Health oversees a publicly funded home and community care network that provides post-acute restorative care directly in the home.

Under Ontario's model, patients leaving the hospital can receive comprehensive home-based rehabilitation for up to 12 weeks following discharge. Specialized clinical teams visit patients to provide daily physical therapy, assist with medication administration, and track biometric markers. 

The results have been significant: health authorities report that this home restorative model keeps **over 80% of recovering seniors** out of long-term care institutions. By treating the home as a primary center of rehabilitation, Ontario's health system helps older individuals regain independence while easing the pressure on regional hospital beds and residential nursing homes.

## Practical Steps for Seniors and Families Facing Hospital Discharge

While federal lawmakers consider the BRIDGE Care at Home Act, seniors facing an upcoming planned surgery or an unexpected hospitalization still have to navigate the system as it stands today. Fortunately, there are actionable steps patients and family caregivers can take to advocate for the best possible post-acute arrangement:

- ☐ **Request an early meeting with the hospital discharge planner:** As soon as an inpatient stay begins, ask to speak with the hospital case manager or discharge coordinator to explore all post-acute options.
- ☐ **Inquire about existing waiver programs:** Ask whether the hospital participates in current CMS Acute Hospital Care at Home initiatives or transitional home-health therapy programs that provide intensive home visits.
- ☐ **Check eligibility requirements:** Clarify whether your hospital stay is classified as formal inpatient admission meeting the traditional 3-day stay rule or as outpatient observation status, which affects rehabilitation coverage.
- ☐ **Contact SHIP for independent guidance:** Call the State Health Insurance Assistance Program at **1-877-839-2675** or visit Shipac.org for free, personalized counseling on Medicare coverage rules and supplemental policy benefits.
- ☐ **Assess home safety before discharge:** Identify potential tripping hazards, review whether family members can assist between nurse visits, and ask the hospital therapy team what assistive devices will be delivered to the home.

Navigating post-hospital care can feel overwhelming, but staying informed about both current Medicare coverage rules and proposed legislative reforms empowers seniors and their families to make decisions that prioritize safety, recovery, and personal dignity.
