Medicare finalized a change that reduces reimbursement for certain outpatient drugs purchased through the 340B safety‑net program, a policy shift reported by the Kaiser Family Foundation on **Nov. 7, 2026**. If you or a family member get care at community, rural or other safety‑net hospitals that use 340B discounts, ask your provider what this could mean for local services and prescription access, and call **1‑800‑MEDICARE** with coverage questions.

## Numbers and quick view

- KFF report date: **Nov. 7, 2026** (Kaiser Family Foundation).
- Medicare consumer helpline: **1‑800‑MEDICARE**.
- Eldercare/Area Agency on Aging contact mentioned for finding alternatives: **1‑800‑677‑1116**.
- Official 340B program overview: HRSA 340B Program (no dollar figures in this brief).

## What changed (plain language)

- The rule reduces how much Medicare reimburses for some outpatient drugs when hospitals purchase those drugs using 340B discounted prices, according to the Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF, Nov. 7, 2026).
- The 340B program itself allows eligible hospitals and clinics to buy outpatient drugs at discounted prices so they can stretch federal resources to serve low‑income and uninsured patients (Health Resources & Services Administration, HRSA overview).
- KFF reports the change shifts reimbursement in ways that are likely to reduce financial benefits for some non‑profit safety‑net providers and to benefit other entities not participating in 340B. The KFF piece frames potential downstream effects on services to patients but does not give national totals or cut percentages in the items cited here.

## Why this matters to seniors

- Many older Americans receive outpatient care, prescriptions and community supports through hospitals and clinics that participate in the 340B program. If those providers face reduced reimbursement, they may need to reassess budgets for outpatient programs, charity care, transportation or other services seniors use.
- The policy change may affect local availability of patient assistance programs and clinic support services. The exact local impact depends on each hospital or clinic’s finances and decisions; official statements from local providers will give the most reliable, specific information.

## What to ask your provider or pharmacist

When you contact your clinic, hospital or pharmacy, consider asking:

- Will this Medicare change affect how my outpatient drugs are billed or priced at this facility?
- Are any patient assistance programs, free medication programs, or transportation services at risk of reduction?
- If my clinic changes services, what alternatives do you recommend for prescriptions and outpatient care?

## Practical steps for seniors and families

- Call your provider or pharmacist and ask the questions above.
- For Medicare questions, call 1‑800‑MEDICARE.
- If local services are reduced, contact your state health department or your Area Agency on Aging for alternatives. The national Eldercare/Area Agency on Aging contact is 1‑800‑677‑1116.
- Monitor statements from your usual clinic or hospital for notices about changes to patient assistance, pharmacy services, or clinic hours.

## Local impact will vary — what to watch for in the coming weeks

- Notices from your clinic, hospital or pharmacy about changes to patient assistance programs or prescription access.
- Public statements or press releases from local safety‑net hospitals and community health centers explaining how they will respond.
- Guidance and FAQs posted by Medicare (Medicare.gov) and HRSA about implementation and protections for patients.

## Checklist — actions to take now

- ☐ Call your regular clinic or pharmacy and ask whether the Medicare reimbursement change will affect you.  
- ☐ If you use a safety‑net hospital for care or prescriptions, ask about any planned changes to patient assistance programs.  
- ☐ Call 1‑800‑MEDICARE for coverage or billing questions.  
- ☐ If services are reduced locally, call your Area Agency on Aging (1‑800‑677‑1116) or state health department for help finding alternatives.

> As of Nov. 7, 2026: this article is based on the Kaiser Family Foundation report (Nov. 7, 2026) and the HRSA 340B program overview. For local and provider‑specific effects, check official statements from your clinic, hospital, or state health authorities.
