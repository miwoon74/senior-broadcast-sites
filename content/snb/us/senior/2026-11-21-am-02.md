Can lifestyle coaching, nutrition guidance, and regular in-home monitoring keep older adults healthier and out of the hospital? The federal government is investing heavily to find out. The Department of Health and Human Services announced **$47 million** in new federal grants to launch the MAHA ELEVATE model through the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Innovation Center. Designed to test whether proactive health habits can treat the underlying causes of chronic illnesses like heart disease and diabetes, this initiative marks a significant federal shift toward proactive wellness.

For millions of older Americans managing ongoing conditions, the pilot program tests a model of care that emphasizes registered dietitian counseling, exercise support, and remote health tracking alongside traditional medical appointments.

## By the Numbers: The New Federal Prevention Initiative

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Innovation Center launched the MAHA ELEVATE model as a competitive demonstration project. Healthcare systems, academic medical centers, and non-profit organizations across the country submitted proposals to demonstrate how non-drug lifestyle therapies could be delivered directly to Medicare beneficiaries.

Federal officials received roughly 450 applications from across the United States. From that competitive pool, the agency selected 15 organizations to administer the test programs and receive a share of the total funding allocation.

## Moving Beyond Pills: What the Demonstration Projects Cover

Standard Original Medicare traditionally reimburses doctors and hospitals for diagnosing and treating diseases after symptoms appear. Many non-drug lifestyle therapies—such as ongoing nutritional coaching by a registered dietitian, personalized fitness guidance, and specialized lifestyle counseling—have historically faced strict coverage limitations or were excluded entirely.

Under the new initiative, the 15 awarded healthcare networks will test interventions targeting root causes rather than simply managing advanced symptoms. Each funded program includes specific nutrition or physical activity coaching, focusing on several key pillars:

- **Registered Dietitian Coaching and Nutrition Counseling:** Tailored nutritional education to assist older adults in managing high blood pressure and blood sugar levels through everyday dietary changes.
- **In-Home Blood Pressure Monitoring with Clinician Alerts:** Equipping patients with remote physiological monitoring devices that transmit real-time readings directly to primary care teams, catching dangerous spikes before an emergency occurs.
- **Structured Exercise and Physical Activity:** Supervised exercise programs built to build strength, enhance cardiovascular stamina, and improve mobility.
- **Personalized Fall-Prevention Education:** Individualized coaching designed to identify personal mobility hazards, improve balance, and reduce catastrophic fall-related injuries.
- **Virtual Lifestyle Counseling:** Remote sessions with care coordinators or community health workers to reinforce healthy daily habits between standard clinic visits.

By addressing nutrition, physical movement, and routine home monitoring, the demonstration projects seek to determine whether these supportive services deliver measurable improvements in daily quality of life while reducing total healthcare expenditures.

## Why Chronic Disease Prevention Matters for Older Adults

Managing chronic conditions represents one of the largest challenges facing older Americans today. According to federal health data, over two-thirds of Medicare beneficiaries live with multiple chronic conditions. Coping with multiple illnesses often leads to recurring doctor visits, complex medication schedules, and substantial out-of-pocket medical bills.

When illnesses like hypertension or diabetes are left unchecked, they frequently escalate into emergency room visits or inpatient hospital stays. These crises disrupt independent living and place immense physical and financial stress on seniors and their family caregivers.

By integrating community health worker support, home monitoring technology, and preventive lifestyle coaching into routine Medicare care, health officials hope to keep seniors healthier at home. Preventing disease progression protects an older adult's independence, helping them stay active within their local communities while avoiding avoidable medical crises.

## How to Access Preventive Care and Coaching Under Medicare Today

While the 15 selected healthcare organizations begin rolling out their demonstration programs, older adults do not have to wait to take advantage of preventive health services. Standard Medicare already includes several important prevention benefits that many beneficiaries overlook each year.

### Schedule an Annual Wellness Visit
Every Medicare beneficiary is eligible for an Annual Wellness Visit at no out-of-pocket cost through Medicare Part B. Unlike a basic physical exam, this visit focuses specifically on developing or updating a personalized prevention plan. During this appointment, your primary care team conducts health risk assessments, reviews your medical and family history, screens for cognitive changes or fall risks, and establishes a checklist of recommended preventive screenings.

### Inquire About Chronic Care Management and Remote Monitoring
If you live with two or more chronic conditions, ask your primary care physician's billing office whether the practice offers Medicare Chronic Care Management or remote physiological monitoring programs. These established Medicare services provide regular monthly check-ins with care coordinators, dedicated treatment plans, and connected health devices such as blood pressure monitors that share vital signs with your physician.

### Check Nutrition Therapy Rules
Original Medicare provides coverage for medical nutrition therapy under specific circumstances. If you have a formal medical diagnosis of diabetes or kidney disease, Medicare Part B covers visits with a registered dietitian or nutrition professional to help you manage your diet. You can contact Medicare directly at 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) to verify your individual coverage rules and locate eligible providers in your area.

### Connect with Local Support Resources
Navigating healthcare options and local wellness services can feel overwhelming. Older adults and family caregivers can find free, local, and unbiased assistance through the State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP). Local counselors help seniors understand their preventive benefits, verify clinic participation, and locate community wellness resources. You can contact SHIP online at shiphelp.org or by calling 1-877-839-2675.

## Action Checklist for Older Adults and Caregivers

Use this checklist to review your current preventive care options and prepare for discussions with your medical providers:

- ☐ Check Medicare.gov to verify that you have used your free Annual Wellness Visit for the current calendar year.
- ☐ Contact your primary care provider's clinic to ask if they participate in one of the 15 awarded health networks or offer Medicare lifestyle coaching.
- ☐ Ask your physician whether your clinic offers remote physiological monitoring or community health worker support.
- ☐ Inquire whether you qualify for Medicare-covered medical nutrition therapy if you manage diabetes or kidney disease.
- ☐ Schedule an Annual Wellness Visit before the calendar year wraps up to review your personal risk assessments and screening schedules.
- ☐ Reach out to your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) at 1-877-839-2675 or shiphelp.org for assistance with Medicare preventive benefits.
