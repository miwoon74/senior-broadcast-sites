Are you prepared for the rapid onset of this winter's respiratory illness season? Federal public health officials reported that nationwide laboratory-confirmed influenza hospitalizations reached **8,396 admissions in a single tracking week**, marking a steep rise as families prepare to gather indoors for the holidays. 

For older adults, respiratory viruses present unique physiological challenges. Understanding the current numbers, exploring specialized immunization options, and knowing your coverage options under Medicare Part B can help protect your household over the coming weeks.

## The Numbers: Weekly Hospitalizations Climb Rapidly

Data released by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) through its FluView surveillance system reveals a marked acceleration in influenza transmission across the United States. In late October (Week 43, 2026), confirmed hospital stays surged from 6,100 patients the previous week to 8,396 patients—an increase of 38 percent in just seven days.

Beyond inpatient hospital wards, outpatient facilities and urgent care centers are recording similar expansions in patient volume. Emergency department visits attributed to influenza-like illness grew by 42 percent over the same period, reaching 1.7 percent of all emergency encounters nationwide.

Federal health surveillance estimates that the ongoing season has already resulted in significant cumulative totals:
- At least **1.7 million** seasonal illnesses nationwide
- **25,000** hospitalizations
- **1,400** flu-related deaths

Ensemble forecasting models maintained by the CDC indicate that transmission will intensify further, projecting between 6,900 and 30,100 new hospital admissions for the week ending November 21, 2026 alone.

## Why Older Adults Face Higher Vulnerability

Aging involves natural shifts in the immune system, often referred to as immunosenescence. As our bodies age, the immune defense responds more slowly to pathogens, making it harder to fight off respiratory infections once they take hold in the lungs.

Because of this physiological change, adults aged 65 and older consistently account for the vast majority of severe influenza complications and extended hospitalizations. When holiday celebrations bring multiple generations together in enclosed living rooms and dining areas, exposure risks multiply quickly. 

A standard influenza dose that protects a healthy young adult may not elicit an adequate protective antibody response in an older body. For this reason, federal health guidelines recommend specialized vaccine formulations for older populations.

## Vaccine Formulations: What Changes at Age 65

If you are 65 or older, public health agencies specifically advise against receiving standard, unadjuvanted flu shots whenever preferred formulations are available. The CDC explicitly prioritizes three specific higher-potency options for older adults:

1. **Fluzone High-Dose Quadrivalent:** Contains four times the antigen amount of standard-dose shots, providing a stronger prompt for the immune system to build protective antibodies.
2. **Flublok Recombinant Quadrivalent:** Built with three times the standard antigen concentration, manufactured without egg proteins.
3. **Fluad Adjuvanted Quadrivalent:** Formulated with an adjuvant—an additive that stimulates a broader, more robust immune response using standard antigen quantities.

All three formulations have demonstrated superior efficacy in preventing laboratory-confirmed influenza, emergency visits, and inpatient stays in seniors compared to standard vaccines.

## Medicare Coverage and Practical Scheduling

A major barrier for many seniors seeking preventive healthcare is navigating out-of-pocket medical expenses. Under federal law, annual influenza immunizations are fully covered as an essential preventive benefit.

- **Medicare Part B Coverage:** Beneficiaries pay **$0** copayment or coinsurance, and no annual Part B deductible applies, provided you use a participating pharmacy or healthcare provider.
- **Co-Administration:** You can safely receive your annual high-dose flu vaccine alongside updated COVID-19 boosters or RSV vaccines during the same appointment. Consult your physician or pharmacist to discuss injection site placement.
- **Transportation Assistance:** Older adults who cannot easily drive to clinics or retail pharmacies can coordinate free community rides or homebound vaccination visits through their local Area Agency on Aging.

## Action Steps for the Holiday Season

Protecting your household before family gatherings requires straightforward planning. Use this checklist to schedule your preventive care:

- ☐ Check your immunization records to confirm whether you have received this season's flu vaccine.
- ☐ Use the federal locator tool at vaccines.gov to find nearby pharmacies stocking Fluzone High-Dose, Flublok Recombinant, or Fluad Adjuvanted.
- ☐ Contact your local provider or call 1-800-232-0233 (or 1-800-CDC-INFO / 1-800-232-4636) to confirm vaccine availability.
- ☐ Verify your coverage status by contacting Medicare directly at 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).
- ☐ If you lack reliable transportation, reach out to the Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov or call 1-800-677-1116 to ask about local rides or mobile visits.
- ☐ Schedule your visit well before holiday travel, as vaccines typically require approximately two weeks to generate full antibody protection.
