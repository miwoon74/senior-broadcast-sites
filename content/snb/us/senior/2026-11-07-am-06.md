Teens worldwide are exercising less, and girls are especially affected, according to an NPR compilation published Nov. 5, 2026. For grandparents, this is a chance to help: a simple, evidence-aligned target is **15–30 minutes per session, 3–5 times per week** of shared, enjoyable movement with grandchildren. Below we break down why it matters, practical activities, a short planning table, and a checklist to get started.

## Why this matters for families

- Recent international reporting (NPR, Nov. 5, 2026) highlights a downward trend in adolescent physical activity and organized-sports participation, with girls showing larger declines. Lower activity in youth raises public‑health concerns for later risk of obesity, depression, and chronic disease.
- Short, regular shared activities are practical for busy families and can help build routine without pressure. The recommendation used here—sessions of 15–30 minutes, 3–5 times per week—comes from practical tips in the broadcast summary and related senior‑health resources (NLM/NIH referenced in the source brief).

## What to try: simple intergenerational activities

The following activities are low-barrier, safe for many ages, and easy to repeat. Each can be adapted to fitness and mobility levels.

- Short after-dinner walk — 15–30 minutes around the block.
- Backyard or community gardening — light digging, planting, watering together.
- Bike rides on a safe route — short loops or shared tandem rides if available.
- Dance-along sessions to favorite songs — 15–20 minutes of music and movement.
- Playtime games — catch, frisbee, or obstacle courses using household items.

## How to make it stick: practical tips

- Keep it short and consistent. Aim for the minimum target (15 minutes, 3 times/week) when starting; increase toward 30 minutes and 5 sessions as habits form.
- Make it social and fun, not a chore: include music, small goals, or friendly challenges.
- Respect abilities and safety: choose flat routes, keep hydration handy, and avoid extremes of weather.
- Use ordinary routines: pair walks with regular events (after school or after dinner) so activity becomes expected.
- Consider local organized options: parks, senior centers, and schools sometimes run intergenerational programs—check municipal resources.

## A brief planning checklist for grandparents

- ☐ Pick one activity from the list and schedule a recurring 15–30 minute time block.  
- ☐ Start with at least 3 sessions per week for the first month.  
- ☐ Make one session explicitly for conversation (no screens) to strengthen bonding.  
- ☐ Check safety needs (shoes, sunscreen, route, any mobility aids).  
- ☐ If interested in community programs, contact your local parks department or senior center (see Sources).

> As of Nov. 5, 2026: these recommendations reflect the summary in NPR's Nov. 5, 2026 coverage and practical guidance referenced from NIH/NLM resources. Municipal program details and availability vary by location; check local government or program pages for current offerings.

### Summary

Global reporting shows adolescents—especially girls—are moving less. For grandparents who want to help, short, regular shared activities are an achievable way to support health and deepen relationships. Aim for 15–30 minutes per session, 3–5 times weekly, start small, and make it social and safe.
