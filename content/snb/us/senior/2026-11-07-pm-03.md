## Why this matters to older drivers
- Service and parts: When a manufacturer scales back or discontinues a model line, dealers and suppliers may deprioritize stocking parts or supporting specialty components used on that model. That can mean longer wait times or higher costs for repairs.
- Software updates: ADAS often relies on ongoing software updates to maintain sensor calibration, maps, and performance. Strategic shifts that reallocate engineering resources can affect the cadence of over‑the‑air or dealer‑delivered updates.
- Resale and trade‑in: Changes in model support can influence resale value and the availability of trade‑in or buyback programs; seniors who make vehicle decisions partly based on long‑term ownership should factor this in.
- Safety continuity: NHTSA involvement and recall activity during 2024–2026 shows regulators are watching ADAS performance. Owners should monitor official channels to ensure any required fixes are applied.

## Practical checklist for seniors who own an EV or ADAS‑equipped vehicle
- ☐ Register your vehicle identification number (VIN) with your manufacturer to receive direct safety and software update notifications.
- ☐ Enroll in NHTSA recall alerts at https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls or call 1‑888‑327‑4236 and provide your VIN when asked.
- ☐ Keep a record of software update history and dates of the most recent over‑the‑air or dealer updates.
- ☐ Maintain scheduled dealer service appointments focused on ADAS calibration and sensor checks.
- ☐ Ask your dealer about parts availability and any extended service or retrofit programs if your model is discontinued or scaled back.
- ☐ Keep documentation of any ADAS‑related issues (dates, behaviors, dashboard alerts) and report significant safety incidents to NHTSA if needed.

## If your model is affected by a program change
1. Contact your dealer to confirm ongoing service arrangements and parts availability for your vehicle.
2. Ask whether the manufacturer offers extended warranties, retrofit options, or software‑support commitments for the model.
3. Keep NHTSA recall status and press releases bookmarked; enroll your VIN so you receive direct alerts.
