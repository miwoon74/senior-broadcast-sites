Will your Medicare premium take an unexpected bite out of your Social Security check next year? Federal health administrators have locked in the official Medicare premium brackets for the 2026 calendar year, establishing new thresholds for the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount. While the standard monthly Part B premium is set at **$202.90**, retirees whose income crossed statutory limits two years ago will see their monthly payments jump directly to **$284.10** or higher.

Because these surcharges are deducted straight from monthly Social Security deposits, an unexpected income increase from selling property or taking retirement withdrawals can create an unwelcome surprise for household budgets. Reviewing the updated tiers and the federal appeals process can help safeguard your monthly cash flow.

## The 2026 Medicare Part B Numbers at a Glance

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services establishes standard rates and income surcharges annually. For 2026, the baseline monthly cost for Medicare Part B coverage is $202.90. In addition, the annual Part B deductible rises to $283 for 2026, which represents a $26 rise from 2025.

For most beneficiaries, the base rate is all they owe for Part B medical insurance. However, the federal government assesses additional monthly surcharges on individual and joint filers whose income exceeds specified thresholds. 



The adjustments are applied in distinct income steps. Beneficiaries who exceed the initial threshold enter the first surcharge bracket, paying an additional $81.20 per month on top of the standard premium. Crossing into the subsequent tier adds a $202.90 monthly surcharge.

| Income Filing Status (2024 Tax Year) | 2026 Monthly Part B Premium | Total Monthly Increase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single up to $109,000 / Joint to $218,000** | $202.90 (Standard Rate) | Base Premium |
| **Single $109,001–$137,000 / Joint $218,001–$274,000** | $284.10 | +$81.20 monthly surcharge |
| **Single $137,001–$171,000 / Joint $274,001–$342,000** | $405.80 | +$202.90 monthly surcharge |

Beyond Part B medical coverage, the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount framework applies surcharges to Part D prescription drug plans as well, compounding total healthcare costs for affected households.

## How the Two-Year Lookback Rule Works

The Social Security Administration determines your Medicare premium bracket using federal income tax data from two calendar years prior. To set your 2026 monthly payments, the agency evaluates the modified adjusted gross income reported on your 2024 tax filing.

This statutory two-year lookback period creates significant financial traps for retirees. The thresholds operate on a cliff: exceeding the income cutoff by even a single dollar triggers the entire surcharge for the full calendar year. For example, a single filer who reported $109,001 in 2024 modified adjusted gross income is required to pay the elevated rate of $284.10 each month throughout 2026, rather than the standard $202.90.

Many retirees cross these thresholds unintentionally. Common drivers of sudden income spikes include:
- Taking mandatory retirement account distributions from tax-deferred savings.
- Selling a primary residence or family property.
- Realizing substantial capital gains during portfolio rebalancing.
- Converting traditional retirement funds into post-tax accounts.

Because the Social Security Administration automatically deducts Medicare premiums from benefit payments, these surcharges directly diminish net deposits. Consider an individual receiving a regular gross Social Security benefit of $1,900 per month. Under standard Part B coverage, deducting the $202.90 base premium leaves roughly $1,697 in net income. If a surcharge elevates that Part B premium to $284.10, the net monthly deposit falls to $1,615.90. Over twelve months, this reduction represents a substantial loss in discretionary spending.

## Appealing an IRMAA Surcharge with Form SSA-44

If your current financial situation differs significantly from your 2024 tax return, you do not necessarily have to pay the higher premium. The Social Security Administration allows beneficiaries to request a formal redetermination if their income decreased due to a recognized life-changing event.

Qualifying life-changing events recognized by the agency include:
- Full retirement from employment.
- A significant reduction in work hours.
- The death of a spouse.

When you experience one of these qualifying circumstances, you can ask the government to calculate your Medicare premium using an estimate of your current-year earnings rather than your historical 2024 tax return. 

To initiate this review, download Form SSA-44 ("Medicare Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount - Life-Changing Event") from the official Social Security website at ssa.gov. You will need to provide documentation confirming the qualifying event, such as a letter from a former employer or a death certificate, alongside proof of your reduced income.

Beneficiaries can contact their local Social Security office or call the national customer service line at 1-800-772-1213 to schedule a redetermination appointment. Submitting this form promptly ensures your benefit check accurately reflects your actual income before the new plan year begins.

## Steps to Take Before the New Plan Year

Navigating Medicare premium rules requires proactive planning, particularly when managing fixed retirement checks. Taking concrete steps now can help you identify upcoming surcharges and file necessary appeals.

- ☐ Review your 2024 federal tax return to check whether your modified adjusted gross income exceeded $109,000 for single filers or $218,000 for joint filers.
- ☐ Inspect your annual Social Security benefit notice to confirm whether an IRMAA surcharge has been added to your 2026 Part B or Part D coverage.
- ☐ Determine whether a qualifying life-changing event—such as retirement, work reduction, or the loss of a spouse—caused your current income to drop below 2024 levels.
- ☐ Download Form SSA-44 from ssa.gov and assemble supporting documents if you qualify to appeal the adjustment.
- ☐ Consult the Medicare Plan Finder tool at Medicare.gov to evaluate how prescription plan choices may impact your overall healthcare budget.
- ☐ Contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) through shiphelp.org or by calling 1-877-839-2675 for free, individualized guidance on Medicare rules and plan reviews.
