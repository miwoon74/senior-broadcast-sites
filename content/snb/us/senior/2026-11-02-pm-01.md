When your doctor prescribes a treatment for chronic pain or an open wound, you expect your coverage to process smoothly. But what happens when an automated algorithm steps between you and your care? Federal records released on November 2, 2026, reveal that Medicare’s pilot program using artificial intelligence to evaluate medical claims has led to substantial delays, with the review contractor approving only **63.7%** of initial claims during testing. 

While the program aimed to curb improper billing, hospital associations and lawmakers warn that automated rejections leave older adults waiting weeks for vital therapies. Understanding how this pilot works, why initial approval rates are so low, and how to appeal a flagged procedure can protect your access to timely healthcare.

## The Numbers Behind the WISeR AI Review Pilot

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) rolled out the demonstration program, known as the WISeR pilot, across six states in early 2026. The initiative deployed automated software through commercial review vendor Virtix Health to screen prior authorization requests for specialized outpatient procedures, specifically targeted at skin substitutes and implanted nerve stimulators.

According to oversight documents obtained under the Freedom of Information Act, the algorithm initially rejected or flagged more than a third of incoming claims. However, when doctors contested these initial determinations by submitting additional clinical documentation, the approval rate surged dramatically.

## Operational Hurdles and the 72-Hour Turnaround Rule

Federal guidelines for prior authorization require prompt reviews so that patient therapy is not disrupted. Under the demonstration agreement, the automated review system was required to provide a coverage decision within a strict 72-hour turnaround deadline. 

Records show that Virtix Health breached this mandatory 72-hour timeframe, leaving beneficiaries in clinical limbo. In response, regulators stepped in to place the vendor on a corrective action plan to resolve operational backlogs.

CMS leadership acknowledged these operational shortcomings at a recent health summit. Meanwhile, the administrative friction has caught the attention of Capitol Hill. The House Appropriations Committee advanced bipartisan legislation to pause funding for algorithmic pilots in traditional Medicare, arguing that computer programs must never replace clinical judgment when vulnerable patients need timely treatment.

## Why Algorithmic Reviews Create Obstacles for Seniors

Artificial intelligence models in health insurance function by matching clinical codes in a doctor's request against rigid data rubrics. When an algorithm screens a complex medical case, minor formatting mismatches or missing attachments can generate an immediate flag or rejection.

For older adults, these automated interruptions carry real consequences:

1. **Wound care setbacks:** Advanced wound care involving skin substitutes requires strict scheduling. A multi-day review pause can jeopardize healing progress.
2. **Prolonged chronic pain:** Specialized therapies such as spinal stimulators require clear coordination between pain specialists and surgeons. Coverage holds force patients to manage pain without planned interventions.
3. **Burden on medical clinics:** Physician offices must spend additional administrative time pulling chart notes, preparing justifications, and resubmitting documentation.ing claims that an algorithm initially flagged.

## International Comparison: How Other Systems Handle Prior Authorization

Concerns about automated insurance processing are not limited to the United States, but policy responses differ significantly across borders.

In Canada, provincial single-payer healthcare plans administered under Health Canada take a strictly regulated stance on medical oversight. Canadian provincial health systems prohibit private algorithms from making automated prior-authorization denials for older patients. 

Instead, coverage determinations require dual-physician clinical sign-offs before any necessary procedure can be withheld. By requiring licensed medical practitioners to review every denial, Canada ensures that human clinical judgment remains the deciding factor, avoiding automated rejections driven by software flags.

## How to Protect Your Care: Action Steps and Appeals

If you or a family member receive care in one of the six demonstration states, or if your provider mentions an automated coverage review, staying proactive is essential. You do not have to accept an initial denial as final.

### Practical Steps to Take Immediately

1. **Check Explanation of Benefits (EOB) statements regularly:** Inspect your quarterly Medicare summary notices promptly online at medicare.gov or by phone. Identifying a flagged claim early prevents surprise medical bills.
2. **Request an expedited clinical review:** If an urgent outpatient treatment or specialized procedure is turned down, ask your healthcare provider to request an immediate expedited appeal.
3. **Request a peer-to-peer conference:** Your doctor may request a peer-to-peer discussion with the reviewer to clarify clinical details.tor. In many cases, these doctor-to-doctor discussions overturn algorithmic determinations quickly.
4. **Track review progress through official channels:** Contact Medicare support to check the status of your authorization and learn available appeal steps.work.

## Checklist for Managing a Flagged Medicare Claim

- ☐ Check your quarterly Explanation of Benefits statement on medicare.gov for flagged outpatient claims.
- ☐ Ask your doctor's billing office if your procedure requires prior authorization under the WISeR pilot.
- ☐ Verify whether your review contractor responded within the required 72-hour turnaround deadline.
- ☐ Request that your physician submit comprehensive supplemental clinical records for a resubmission.
- ☐ Ask your doctor to schedule an immediate peer-to-peer clinical review with the review contractor.
- ☐ Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) to download official appeal forms if coverage is formally denied.

Automated claims processing is evolving rapidly, but coverage decisions must always support patient well-being. By staying informed about pilot programs, monitoring your coverage notices, and working closely with your healthcare providers, you can ensure that technology does not compromise the medical care you have earned.
