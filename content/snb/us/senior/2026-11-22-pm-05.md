## The Scale of Grandfamily Caregiving in the United States

Across the nation, families step up when parents are unable to care for their children. According to Generations United, more than 2.5 million children nationwide are being raised by grandparents and other kinship relatives without parents in the home. Many of these arrangements happen outside the traditional foster care system, meaning older relatives often step in without immediate access to formal foster parent stipends.

Stepping into a parenting role on short notice can quickly stretch a fixed retirement income to the breaking point. Grandparents frequently face sudden expenses for clothing, groceries, and pediatric medical co-pays, all while trying to navigate complex school enrollment guidelines without formal legal custody.

## Expanding Kinship Navigator Programs and Federal Support

To address these challenges, the Administration for Community Living (ACL) established a $9.95 million national technical assistance center to coordinate family services under the Supporting Grandparents Raising Grandchildren Act. 

In addition, the federal Administration for Children and Families (ACF) recognizes 11 states and Puerto Rico as operating federally approved, evidence-based Title IV-E kinship navigator programs. These jurisdictions include:
- Colorado
- Iowa
- Nevada
- Ohio
- Washington

These specialized navigator networks provide dedicated community caseworkers who assist older adults with vital family logistics, securing legal guardianship, obtaining school supplies, and accessing monthly support stipends.

## Unlocking Financial Aid: Child-Only TANF and Health Coverage

A major hurdle for grandparents is the belief that their personal retirement savings or Social Security checks will disqualify the household from public aid. However, specialized assistance programs are designed specifically for kinship care:

- **Child-Only TANF Grants:** Households raising kin may qualify for child-only Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) cash assistance. These monthly stipends evaluate only the child's financial situation, disregarding the grandparent's personal retirement pension or Social Security benefits.
- **Healthcare Coverage:** Dependent children can be enrolled in Medicaid and the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) to cover doctor visits, dental care, and prescriptions.
- **Nutrition Support:** Families can access food assistance programs under federal nutrition guidelines to offset rising grocery costs.
- **Respite Vouchers:** Local Area Agencies on Aging help kinship caregivers secure respite relief vouchers, providing temporary caregiving breaks to prevent burnout.

## Practical Steps for Grandfamilies

If you have stepped forward to care for relatives, take these practical steps to access available benefits and legal guidance:

- ☐ **Download Legal Guides:** Visit grandfamilies.org to download your state-specific kinship legal fact sheet and caregiving handbook.
- ☐ **Connect with a Navigator:** Dial 2-1-1 or visit 211.org to locate community kinship caseworkers and request an intake interview.
- ☐ **Explore Cash and Food Aid:** Apply for child-only TANF cash benefits and SNAP nutrition assistance through your state human services office at acf.hhs.gov/ofa.
- ☐ **Request School Liaison Support:** Contact your local school district office to ask about kinship family liaison programs that assist with enrollment and school supplies.
- ☐ **Contact Your Area Agency on Aging:** Inquire about caregiver respite vouchers and local support groups via the federal Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov or by calling 1-800-677-1116.
- ☐ **Join Peer Networks:** Connect with Generations United family advocates at 1-202-289-3979 or access the Grandfamilies & Kinship Support Network at gksnetwork.org for support group referrals.
