32 years ago today, November 28, 1994, violence inside a Wisconsin maximum-security prison brought an abrupt end to one of the most notorious criminal sagas in American history. Serial killer Jeffrey Dahmer and convicted murderer Jesse Anderson were attacked by fellow inmate Christopher Scarver at the Columbia Correctional Institution.

## The Morning Assault in Portage

On the morning of November 28, 1994, inmate Christopher Scarver attacked both Jeffrey Dahmer and Jesse Anderson inside the Columbia Correctional Institution in Portage, Wisconsin. Dahmer suffered fatal trauma and was pronounced dead while being transported to the hospital. Anderson survived the initial assault but succumbed to his injuries two days later on November 30. The sudden killings immediately sent shockwaves across the American justice system and captured headlines nationwide.

## Jeffrey Dahmer's Murders and Conviction

Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer was born in Milwaukee in May 1960. He committed his first murder in Ohio in 1978 and resumed killing in 1987. Between 1978 and 1991, Dahmer murdered and dismembered seventeen men and boys, luring victims to his apartment where they were drugged, assaulted, and killed. His horrifying crimes involved cannibalism, necrophilia, and the retention of body parts, earning him the moniker the Milwaukee Cannibal. Dahmer was finally apprehended in July 1991 after an intended victim escaped his apartment and flagged down police. Despite psychiatric diagnoses including borderline personality disorder and psychotic disorder, Dahmer was deemed legally sane at trial and sentenced in 1992 to 16 consecutive terms of life imprisonment without parole.

## The Case of Jesse Anderson

The second victim of the November 28 assault was Jesse Michael Anderson. Anderson was incarcerated at the same maximum-security facility after being convicted of the brutal murder of his wife, Barbara Anderson. Following the crime, Anderson had wounded himself and fabricated an account to police officers, falsely asserting that two Black men had assaulted the couple. Investigators unraveled his deception, leading to his trial, conviction, and placement within Wisconsin's maximum-security prison system.

## Inside the Columbia Correctional Institution

The killings took place within the Columbia Correctional Institution, a maximum-security state prison operated by the Wisconsin Department of Corrections Division of Adult Institutions. Opened in May 1986 on 110 acres of land at a cost of $38.6 million, the facility was designed with 27 acres enclosed inside its security perimeter. Originally built to house 450 inmates in single-bed cells across 12 housing units, the facility held a variety of high-profile offenders over the decades, including Filemon Amaro, James Oswald, and Brendan Dassey. A 20-foot tall painted steel sculpture titled Chromatic Fragments—Vortex to the Sky had even been installed in the parking lot in 1988.

## Dahmer and the Modern Definition of Serial Murder

Dahmer’s gruesome spree came to embody the criminological concept of the modern serial killer. The term serial killer, popularized in the 1970s by former FBI special agent Robert Ressler, entered broader American usage when The New York Times covered the Wayne Williams case in early 1981. The newspaper used the phrase 233 times during the 1980s, but that figure surged to 2,514 mentions in the 1990s as the nation grappled with Dahmer's horrific crimes. Criminologists Fuller and Hickey later singled out Dahmer as the defining example of serial killing, contrasting his deliberate pattern with spree killers such as Andrew Cunanan or mass murderers like Charles Whitman.

## The Prison in the Decades Following the Attack

In the decades following the deaths of Dahmer and Anderson, Columbia Correctional Institution continued to operate as Wisconsin’s primary maximum-security prison. In 1997, the facility added a 150-bed barracks for minimum-security inmates, and many single cells were converted into double-occupancy units to manage growing numbers. By fiscal year 2018, the daily average inmate population stood at 830 under state administration, later overseen by warden Larry Fuchs starting in 2020. Even decades later, the institution in Portage remains permanently marked by the November morning when Christopher Scarver killed America’s most infamous serial offender.
